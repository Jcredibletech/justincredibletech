A coalition of advocacy groups has gone to court to try to hit the brakes on the AI race. On Thursday, November 12, 2026, four nonprofits filed a lawsuit in **San Francisco Superior Court** against **Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceXSI** (SpaceX's AI unit), asking a judge to freeze development of more powerful AI models.

## What the lawsuit asks for

The plaintiffs are **Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative and The Alliance for Secure AI**. Former New Jersey Attorney General Matthew Platkin filed the complaint on their behalf ([Public Citizen](https://www.citizen.org/news/coalition-of-nonprofits-files-landmark-lawsuit-to-stop-development-of-advanced-ai-systems-at-anthropic-google-meta-openai-and-spacexsi-that-proliferate-bioweapons-and-cyberattacks/)).

According to [Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/11/12/nonprofits-sue-ai-companies-over-existential-risk-01161965), they want an injunction that would:

- stop each company from developing or deploying AI that is **more capable than its most advanced current system**, and
- bar the companies from running AI programs **built to improve themselves autonomously**.

## The legal argument

The suit relies on **California's Unfair Competition Law**, which lets people and organizations sue to stop business practices they say are unlawful or harmful. The groups argue that:

- AI company leaders have themselves warned that frontier models could help with bioweapons or major cyberattacks, so continuing to build them threatens public health and safety;
- AI-driven hacking forces others to spend money defending their systems, which they call a public nuisance; and
- continued development is inconsistent with **SB 53**, California's law on catastrophic AI risk.

"We hold every other industry accountable when its products threaten public safety, and AI companies should be no different," said Brendan Steinhauser, CEO of The Alliance for Secure AI ([Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/11/12/nonprofits-sue-ai-companies-over-existential-risk-01161965)).

## What the companies say

The companies did not immediately respond to requests for comment, Politico reported. [AP](https://apnews.com/article/artificial-intelligence-ai-lawsuit-cyberattacks-anthropic-openai-03ece37b96979fe834f60976648504ba) also reported on the filing. At this stage these are allegations in a complaint; no judge has ruled on them.

## Why it matters

This is a long-shot legal strategy, and it is not the first of its kind. Politico notes a central question is whether groups that **have not been directly harmed** by an AI product can sue over it at all. Another nonprofit, Legal Advocates for Safe Science and Technology, used the same California law against OpenAI in September.

If a court ever granted the freeze, it would effectively pause the next generation of chatbots and coding tools from five of the biggest AI developers in the world. For now, the more likely near-term result is a fight over whether the case can proceed.
