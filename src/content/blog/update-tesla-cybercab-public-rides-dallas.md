*This is an update to our October 23 story, [Tesla's Cybercab Expands Beyond Austin, and Dallas Is Next](/blog/tesla-cybercab-dallas-expansion/).*

Last month Tesla promised Dallas would be the next city for its steering-wheel-free Cybercab. That has now happened.

## What's new

On **Thursday, November 19, 2026**, Tesla's Robotaxi account posted, "Howdy Dallas. Cybercab has arrived in your city," [Teslarati reported](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-cybercab-without-steering-wheel-pedals-expands-public-rides-dallas/). [InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/816139/tesla-cybercab-robotaxi-expands-to-dallas/) confirmed Tesla is rolling out public Cybercab rides in Dallas, its second city for the vehicle after Austin, where rides began in September.

What riders need to know:

- The **Cybercab** seats **up to two passengers** and has **no steering wheel or pedals**.
- Rides are booked through the **Tesla Robotaxi app**.
- Tesla already ran **Model Y robotaxis** in Dallas (since April, per Teslarati), so this adds a new vehicle to an existing service rather than opening a new market.
- Tesla has **not said how many Cybercabs** are running in Dallas.

## Where Tesla's robotaxis are now

According to InsideEVs, Tesla's robotaxi service also runs in **Houston, Fort Worth, Tampa, San Antonio, Orlando and Miami** using Model Ys. In the **San Francisco Bay Area**, cars still carry safety drivers under California rules. Only **Austin and Dallas** have the Cybercab.

## The bigger picture

The Cybercab is central to Tesla's robotaxi plans. Elon Musk has said Tesla eventually wants about **two million** Cybercabs in the U.S.; InsideEVs notes that figure "deserves some skepticism" given Tesla's record on past targets. Tesla also plans to wind down Cybertruck production at Gigafactory Texas next summer to make room for Cybercab production, as [we reported](/blog/tesla-cybertruck-wind-down-2027/).

Tesla has also begun inviting select customers to **buy** a Cybercab for personal use. Teslarati reports a price of **$29,990** before taxes plus a required **$299-a-month** autonomy subscription, with first deliveries targeted for December in Austin. InsideEVs points out it's still unclear how a car without manual controls will meet federal motor vehicle safety standards for private owners.

The main competitor, **Waymo**, runs larger driverless fleets across more cities, Teslarati notes.

## Why it matters

Moving the Cybercab out of one city is the first real test of whether Tesla's purpose-built robotaxi can scale the way Tesla says it will. Dallas is a friendly place to try: InsideEVs notes Texas and Florida have fewer regulations, which lets Tesla expand faster there than in states like California.
