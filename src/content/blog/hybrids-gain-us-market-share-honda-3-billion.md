Electric vehicles have hit a speed bump in the U.S. this year, but the car that splits the difference is having a moment. Hybrids, which pair a gas engine with an electric motor and battery and never need to be plugged in, are taking a bigger share of new-car sales than ever, and automakers are spending billions to build more of them.

## The numbers

According to [Cox Automotive data cited by AFP](https://www.afp.com/en/hybrids-make-gains-us-auto-market-amid-high-gasoline-prices), hybrid volumes have risen by more than a fifth since mid-2025, pushing hybrids to a **record 16.3% share** of the U.S. market at the end of the second quarter, up from 13% a year earlier. Over the same stretch, the shares of gas-only cars and fully electric cars both fell. EVs lost ground after federal EV tax credits were eliminated.

The big driver is fuel prices. AFP reports gasoline sat above **$4 a gallon** for much of 2026 because of the war in the Middle East, and put the current national average at about **$3.87**, citing AAA. Buyers are moving fast, too: [USA Today, citing Cars.com](https://www.usatoday.com/story/cars/news/automotive-trends/2026/11/06/hybrids-sell-fast-high-gas-prices/92631054007/), reports new hybrids sold in about **44 days** in September, compared with **75 days** for gas-only vehicles.

Compact SUVs like the Toyota RAV4 and Honda CR-V make up about **30% of U.S. hybrid sales**, said Stephanie Valdez Streaty of Cox. She told AFP that hybrid share "definitely has room to grow," especially if more pickups and full-size SUVs get hybrid versions.

## Who's investing

- **Honda** [announced on November 9](https://global.honda/en/newsroom/news/2026/c261110beng.html) that it will spend **$3 billion** on a new auto plant in central Ohio, its fourth there. It's planned to open in **2029** (pending final government approvals), add **more than 2,000 jobs** and build up to **240,000 vehicles a year**. Honda is also retooling plants in Ohio, Alabama and Georgia and says its Ohio hybrid-system capacity will rise **250%**.
- **Nissan** is launching its first major U.S. hybrid this fall, a hybrid Rogue using its "e-POWER" system, where the gas engine only generates electricity and the electric motor drives the wheels. "We were losing that traffic," Nissan Americas senior vice president Ponz Pandikuthira told AFP.
- **GM**, which skipped hybrids earlier this decade to focus on EVs, plans them "in multiple segments," CEO Mary Barra said on an October earnings call.

Toyota remains the U.S. hybrid market leader, and Hyundai and Kia have added more choices.

## Why it matters

If you're shopping for a car, you'll likely see more hybrid options, in more vehicle types, over the next few years. For the industry, it's a shift in where the money goes: plants like Honda's are built to switch between hybrid, gas and electric models, so automakers can follow what buyers actually want instead of betting everything on one powertrain.
