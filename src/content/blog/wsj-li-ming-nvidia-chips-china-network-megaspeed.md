The investigation into how restricted Nvidia AI chips may have reached China has a new name at its center.

## What happened

The **Wall Street Journal** [reported](https://www.wsj.com/tech/li-ming-megaspeed-nvidia-chips-china-6260d836) this weekend that **Li Ming**, a 52-year-old Chinese national based in Singapore, was closely involved in the operations and major business decisions of **Megaspeed**, a Singapore cloud and data-center company that U.S. authorities are investigating over possible export-control violations. The report was summarized by [The Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/world-news/nvidia-chip-probe-who-is-alice-huangs-associate-li-ming-and-what-is-he-accused-of/4391123/) and [Mint](https://www.livemint.com/companies/news/who-is-li-ming-how-did-alice-huangs-associate-funnel-nvidia-ai-chips-to-china-read-about-the-accusation-11795345041140.html).

According to the Journal, citing people familiar with the businesses:

- Li worked for years with **Alice Huang**, Megaspeed's former managing director, and her husband, **Kang Wei**. Alice Huang is the woman at the center of the scandal that led to Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO this month.
- Megaspeed won **Nvidia cloud-partner status in 2023**, citing Alibaba as an anchor customer, then provided offshore computing capacity to Alibaba from Nvidia-powered servers in Malaysia.
- Some servers were reportedly sent to Shanghai-based **Century Litong**, which the Journal linked through registry records to a venture founded by Alice Huang and Kang Wei. The newspaper said those transfers violated U.S. export rules.

## What's established and what isn't

- **Li's charges are separate.** In February 2025, Singapore prosecutors charged Li and others with fraud, alleging they bought Nvidia-equipped servers by claiming they were for a Singapore company before shipping them to Malaysia and an undisclosed third country. He is out on bail. Megaspeed is not named in those charges, and **he has not been convicted** of anything.
- **The U.S. probe is ongoing.** In a November 16 joint statement, the Commerce and Justice Departments said Commerce began examining Megaspeed in 2024, and Nvidia put the company on an internal "no sell" list in June 2025, The Financial Express reported. The agencies have not charged Megaspeed.
- **Megaspeed denies wrongdoing.** The company has said it conducted lawful business, designed its supply chain to comply with U.S. export controls, and hired outside lawyers for an internal review. It says Alice Huang left in August 2025.

## Why it matters

Washington restricts sales of Nvidia's most advanced chips to China on national-security grounds. Reporting by the Journal, The New York Times and the Financial Times this week suggests those rules may have been worked around through cloud services and Southeast Asian data centers, which could put pressure on Nvidia and Congress to tighten how "cloud partners" are vetted.
