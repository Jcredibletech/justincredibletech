After more than eight years, nine planetary flybys and billions of kilometers, a spacecraft from Europe and Japan is about to become only the second mission ever to orbit Mercury, after NASA's MESSENGER.

## What's happening today

On **Saturday, November 21, 2026**, the **BepiColombo** mission, a joint project of the **European Space Agency (ESA)** and the **Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA)**, is scheduled to perform its **Mercury orbit insertion**. According to [Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/missions/esa-jaxa-bepicolomobo-mission-mercury-orbit-arrival), the burn is set to start at **1:24 p.m. EST (12:24 p.m. CT)** and last **37 minutes**.

[ESA says](https://www.esa.int/media/about-us/directorates/science/bepicolombo-entering-orbit-around-mercury) four thrusters on its **Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO)** will fire to slow the spacecraft as it approaches Mercury's south pole from the planet's night side, so Mercury's gravity can capture it into an initial, stretched-out orbit of about **590 by 11,640 kilometers**.

*At the time of writing, the burn had not yet happened. We'll note ESA's confirmation here once it's announced.*

## Why getting to Mercury is so hard

Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun, and the Sun's gravity speeds up anything falling toward it. A spacecraft has to shed a huge amount of speed to be caught by tiny Mercury. That's why BepiColombo, which launched on an **Ariane 5** in **October 2018**, took the long way around: one flyby of Earth, two of Venus and six of Mercury. A problem with the power available to its electric thrusters added about a year to the trip, Space.com reports.

## Two spacecraft, one mission

BepiColombo is really two orbiters traveling stacked together:

- **ESA's MPO**, which will map Mercury's surface and study its interior.
- **JAXA's Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter, nicknamed Mio**, which will study the planet's magnetic field and how it interacts with the solar wind.

What comes next, per ESA's [arrival timeline](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/science/bepicolombo/latest-updates-bepicolombo-s-arrival-at-mercury):

- **December 9-10, 2026:** MPO and Mio separate, making BepiColombo the first mission to fly two spacecraft around Mercury at once.
- **By March 2027:** MPO uses more burns to reach its final science orbit.
- **April 2027:** science operations begin for both orbiters.

## Why it matters

Only NASA's Mariner 10 (flybys in the 1970s) and MESSENGER (in orbit 2011-2015) have studied Mercury up close. Scientists still don't fully understand why such a small planet has a huge iron core and its own magnetic field. Two orbiters watching from different vantage points should help answer how planets form so close to their stars.
