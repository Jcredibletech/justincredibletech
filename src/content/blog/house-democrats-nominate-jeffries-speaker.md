The House will have a new speaker in January, and Democrats have picked their candidate.

## What happened

On **Wednesday, November 18, 2026**, House Democrats voted **251–11** in a closed-door, secret-ballot caucus election to nominate Minority Leader **Hakeem Jeffries** of New York for speaker in the next Congress, [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/11/18/democrats-nominate-jeffries-to-be-house-speaker-a-historic-first) and [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/5f374b32b07b/world/us/urgent-democrats-nominate-hakeem-jeffries-next-us-house-speaker-2026-11-18/) reported. He had no announced opponent but asked for a recorded vote.

Democrats won the House majority in this month's midterms. Jeffries still has to win **218 votes** when the full House votes after the new Congress convenes in January. If he does, he would be the **first Black speaker**, [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/democrats-formally-nominate-rep-hakeem-jeffries-paving-way-first-black-rcna609619) reported.

The caucus also kept the rest of the team in place: **Katherine Clark** of Massachusetts as majority leader (the first woman in that job, per Jeffries) and **Pete Aguilar** of California as majority whip. **Ted Lieu** of California was elected caucus chair.

## What each side is saying

- **Jeffries:** "Our job is to fight MAGA extremism, not each other," he said when asked about the 11 no votes, adding Democrats would "get big things done for the American people."
- **The holdouts:** Seven of the 11 incoming members who planned to vote no said talks with leadership are "ongoing" and there is "work to be done," citing issues including Medicare for All and immigration enforcement, Roll Call reported.
- **On oversight:** Jeffries said House Democrats will hold "the crooks accountable, not for partisan reasons, but for patriotic ones."

Jeffries has listed health care, immigration legislation and investigations of the Trump administration among his priorities, Roll Call reported.

## What comes next

The floor vote for speaker happens when the new Congress opens in January. Over in the Senate, Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer, whose party takes the Senate majority in January, faces a challenge from Sen. Chris Van Hollen, with that vote scheduled for **December 1**, according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/live/2026/nov/19/hakeem-jeffries-us-politics-demicrats-republicans-latest-news-updates). Before any of that, Congress needs a funding deal by **December 11** to avoid a shutdown, [The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/6229008-congress-faces-government-shutdown-deadline/) reported.

## Why it matters

The speaker controls which bills reach the House floor. With a Republican president and a Democratic House, expect more oversight fights and tougher negotiations over spending in 2027.
