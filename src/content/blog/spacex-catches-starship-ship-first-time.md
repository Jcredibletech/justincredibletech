**This is the follow-up** to this morning's story, [Starship Flight 15's Booster Catch Was Aborted, and SpaceX Plans Its First-Ever Ship Catch Saturday Afternoon](https://justincredibletech.com/blog/starship-flight-15-ship-catch-attempt-saturday/). The catch happened, and it worked.

## What happened

At about **3:20 p.m. Central (21:20 UTC) on Saturday, November 14**, Starship's upper stage, Ship 42, descended over Starbase, Texas, and settled between the launch tower's "chopstick" arms, according to [Space.com's live coverage](https://www.space.com/news/live/spacex-starship-flight-15-live-updates-nov-14-2026-starship-first-double-landing-attempt). It was the **first time SpaceX has ever caught a Starship upper stage**, and the first recovery of one from orbit.

The Ship had launched from the same pad about 22.5 hours earlier, on Friday afternoon. In between it reached orbit, deployed **26 Starlink V3 satellites**, circled Earth roughly 14 times, and fired one engine for a roughly 10-second deorbit burn before coming back through the atmosphere, per Space.com, [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/14/spacex-plans-to-try-first-ever-catch-of-starship-rocket-from-orbit.html) and [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2316926/watch-spacex-attempt-to-catch-the-starship-upper-stage-for-the-first-time/).

"We caught a Starship, and now everything changes," SpaceX's Dan Huot said on the company's webcast, calling it "the missing link." CNBC reports Elon Musk later posted a video on X of himself inspecting the caught vehicle.

## Not a double catch

SpaceX had hoped to catch **both** stages on this flight. That didn't happen. On Friday the Super Heavy booster aborted its own tower catch, diverted to the Gulf of Mexico and was destroyed before it hit the water. On Saturday's webcast, SpaceX's Kate Tice said early indications are that the booster cut its boostback burn short "due to a low fuel mass alarm," then its guidance saw it wasn't in the right position and "correctly aborted itself." SpaceX says it is still investigating the root cause. (On Friday, SpaceX had described the abort as a guidance, navigation and control issue; the fuel alarm is the newer, still-preliminary explanation.)

SpaceX has caught Super Heavy boosters on three earlier flights, so the booster miss was on a maneuver it has already done before. The Ship catch is new.

## Why it matters

The Ship comes in higher and faster than the booster, and it has to survive a full reentry before it lines up with the tower, which makes it the harder catch. Bringing both stages back to the pad is the core of SpaceX's plan to refly Starship quickly and cut launch costs. With the Ship catch now done once, the next big test is doing both catches on the same flight, and then actually flying a caught Ship again.
