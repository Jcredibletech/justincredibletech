AI agents have been able to research products and fill shopping carts for a while. Paying was the part that made people nervous. On Friday, November 13, 2026, OpenAI started rolling out **ChatGPT Wallet**, its answer to that problem: your agent can check out for you, but it never gets your actual card number, and nothing is bought until you approve it.

## What happened

- **What it is.** ChatGPT Wallet lets OpenAI's "dots," the always-on AI agents it introduced in late September, complete purchases on a user's behalf ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/13/openai-chatgpt-wallet-dot-purchases.html)).
- **Who gets it first.** It is rolling out to **ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the U.S. who are 18 or older**, with eligible U.S.-issued cards. OpenAI's help center lists eligible American Express, Chase, Mastercard and Visa cards, and says Wallet only works inside conversations with your dot, not in regular chats ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001654-making-purchases-with-chatgpt-wallet)).
- **Who built it.** OpenAI says it built Wallet with **American Express, Mastercard and Visa** ([Reuters, via CNA](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/openai-launches-chatgpt-wallet-letting-ai-agents-pay-purchases-6550151)).

## How a purchase works

You add a card once. Before each purchase, ChatGPT shows you the items, the total and the card it will use, and you approve it with a security check such as **Face ID or a fingerprint scan**. After you approve, the card network issues a **payment token**, a stand-in card number, security code and expiration date. CNBC reports that the token is specific to that transaction. The business and the agent never see your real card details, and the charge still lands on your chosen card.

OpenAI says you keep your card's existing **rewards and dispute protections** (for example, if an item arrives defective), and that you still buy directly from the store, which handles pricing, shipping and returns. CNBC reports OpenAI is **not taking a cut of transactions** to start.

## The catch: not every store will take it

Agent shopping is still new, and some big names are opting out. According to CNBC, **Amazon prevents these agents from buying on its platform**, and **United Airlines said this week it will block agents from booking tickets**. OpenAI itself says some sites may "unintentionally block purchases," and it published a playbook to help businesses let legitimate agent purchases through without tripping their fraud filters.

## What's next

OpenAI says that over time it expects users to be able to hand purchases off entirely, within limits they set, such as **spending caps, purchase categories or time periods**. For now, every single purchase needs your OK. OpenAI calls this a limited launch to learn from real-world use before expanding.

## Why it matters to you

If you pay for ChatGPT Pro, your agent can now finish the job instead of handing you a link. If you don't, this is still a preview of where online shopping is going: the card networks are building agent payments into the same systems your card already uses. The token approach is the same basic idea as tapping your phone at a register, applied to an AI doing the clicking.
