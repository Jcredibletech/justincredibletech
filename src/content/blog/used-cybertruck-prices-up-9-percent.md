Used Tesla Cybertrucks are getting more expensive, not less. CarGurus' price tracker shows the average asking price for a used Cybertruck at **$89,292**, up **9.09% over the last 30 days** and 12.95% over 90 days. Over the same 30 days, CarGurus' index for the whole used-car market **fell 1.31%**.

## What changed

The jump lines up with Tesla's own moves this month. On Nov. 6, Elon Musk said Tesla would [wind down Cybertruck production at Gigafactory Texas](/blog/tesla-cybertruck-wind-down-2027/) to make room for Cybercab, with production now expected to run into 2027. Since then:

- Tesla stopped taking new U.S. orders for the cheaper Dual Motor and Premium AWD trims, leaving the **Cyberbeast** as the only Cybertruck you can order new.
- On Nov. 24, Tesla raised the Cyberbeast by **$5,000 to $114,990** before fees and taxes, the latest of several increases this month, according to [Tesla North](https://teslanorth.com/2026/11/24/tesla-cyberbeast-us-price-114990/) and [Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/5079/tesla-raises-cyberbeast-price-again-ends-cybertruck-leases). Not a Tesla App also reports Tesla stopped offering leases on new Cybertruck orders.

With the cheaper versions no longer available new, a used truck is now the only way to get a Cybertruck for less than $114,990.

## The numbers, and what they don't say

[Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/27/used-cybertruck-prices-jump-after-tesla-kills-it/) reported that Visor, which tracks dealer listings, shows a 15% like-for-like rise versus last month, with an average asking price of $101,784 across 171 used trucks. The most expensive CarGurus listing it found was a used 2026 Cyberbeast at a New Jersey dealer for **$150,790** with fees, about $36,000 more than Tesla charges for a new one. CarGurus flags that listing as overpriced.

A few important caveats:

- These are **asking prices**, not what buyers actually paid.
- The rise isn't even across model years. CarGurus shows 2024 trucks up about 12% in 30 days, while 2025 trucks are flat. Electrek notes many 2024 Foundation Series trucks came with Full Self-Driving bought outright, which new trucks no longer offer.
- The bottom of the market hasn't moved much. Electrek found higher-mileage 2024 and 2025 trucks listed from the high $60,000s to around $80,000.

## What it means if you own one or want one

If you own a Cybertruck, listings suggest the market has turned in your favor for now, though asking prices can move quickly in either direction. If you're shopping, compare a listing against CarGurus' market value and the new Cyberbeast's $114,990 sticker before paying a premium. Long-term parts and repair costs for a discontinued, low-volume truck are still an open question.
