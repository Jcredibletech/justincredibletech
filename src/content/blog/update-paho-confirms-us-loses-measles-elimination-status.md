Last weekend we covered [reports](/blog/us-loses-measles-elimination-status-paho/) that the United States was about to lose its measles elimination status. On **Tuesday, November 17, 2026**, it became official.

## What happened

The **Pan American Health Organization (PAHO)**, the World Health Organization's regional office for the Americas, announced the decision at a news briefing. "The commission concluded that endemic measles transmission has now been reestablished in Bolivia, Mexico, and the United States, and recommended that their measles elimination status be withdrawn — a recommendation which I have accepted," PAHO Director **Dr. Jarbas Barbosa** said, as quoted by [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/us-measles-elimination-status.html) and [The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/healthcare/6225090-us-loses-measles-elimination-status/).

The decision followed a meeting of PAHO's independent **Measles and Rubella Elimination Regional Monitoring and Re-Verification Commission**, an eight-member expert panel that reviewed U.S. and Mexican evidence last week. "Endemic" means measles is again spreading continuously inside the country, with outbreaks seeding new ones, rather than just arriving with travelers. The U.S. first earned elimination status in **2000**.

## What's new since the reports

- **It's formal.** Last week's news was based on unnamed sources; now PAHO's director has signed off.
- **Mexico and Bolivia** also lost their status.
- **Next step:** PAHO expects the U.S. to present a **corrective action plan** in the coming weeks laying out how it will regain elimination, said Dr. Daniel Salas of PAHO's immunization program, per CNBC.
- The Hill reports PAHO had first scheduled the U.S. review for April but moved it to November to give the U.S. more time to finish viral genome sequencing.

## The numbers

- More than **7,700 cases** in the past two years, The Hill reports, including more than **5,400 in 2026** as of mid-November, per CNBC, the worst in more than three decades.
- States have confirmed **10 measles-associated deaths in 2026**: seven in Pennsylvania, one in New York and two in Wisconsin, per CNBC. Among deaths where vaccination status was reported, all were unvaccinated.
- Kindergarten MMR coverage was **92.4%** in the 2025-26 school year, below the roughly 95% generally needed to stop sustained spread.

## What officials say

"The measles elimination designation is not what protects Americans. Vaccination does," CDC Director **Dr. Erica Schwartz** said in a statement reported by [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Health/us-officially-loses-measles-elimination-status-paho/story?id=138203543). She said the CDC has invested **$100 million** in state and local health departments and that "losing a designation doesn't mean we abandon an evidence-based approach." The CDC says it's committed to regaining elimination status.

Public health experts quoted by CNBC and KFF point to falling vaccination rates, funding cuts and mixed federal messaging as factors. HHS has previously said vaccination gaps opened years before the current administration.

## What it means for you

Day to day, nothing about how measles spreads changes with the label. What it does show is that outbreaks are lasting longer than a year. According to the CDC, two doses of MMR vaccine are about **97% effective** against measles.
