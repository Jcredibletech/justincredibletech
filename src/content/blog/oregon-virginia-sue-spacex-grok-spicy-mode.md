Two states have become the first to take Elon Musk's SpaceX to court over Grok, the AI chatbot and image generator built by xAI and woven into the X social network. All three are now part of SpaceX.

## What happened

Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield filed his complaint in Multnomah County Circuit Court on Sunday evening, and Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones filed a separate but similar one in Richmond Circuit Court on Monday morning, [CNBC reports](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/oregon-virginia-spacex-grok-deepfake-lawsuits.html). Both are Democrats.

What the states allege, according to their announcements ([Virginia](https://www.oag.state.va.us/media-center/news-releases/jay-jones/3162-attorneys-general-jones-and-rayfield-file-suit-against-spacex-parent-company-of-x-xai-and-grok-over-creation-and-promotion-of-child-exploitation-and-non-consensual-images-on-platform), [Oregon](https://www.doj.state.or.us/media-home/news-media-releases/ag-rayfield-takes-legal-action-against-grok-for-failing-to-block-child-exploitation/)):

- Grok could turn a single photo of a real person into a realistic nude or sexual image or video **without that person's consent**, including images that appeared to show children.
- xAI promoted a **"spicy mode"** and marketed Grok as having a "rebellious streak," while publicly assuring users that strong safeguards were in place.
- Those assurances were misleading, which the states say violates Virginia's Consumer Protection Act and Oregon's Unlawful Trade Practices Act.

Both complaints lean on a study by the Center for Countering Digital Hate, which estimated that between December 29, 2025 and January 8, 2026, Grok created and posted about **3 million sexualized images on X**, including roughly 23,000 that appeared to depict children.

"The lack of needed safety measures was considered a feature, not a bug," Rayfield said. Jones said Virginians "were misled by the company's false reassurances."

The states want court orders forcing stricter guardrails, plus civil penalties, disgorgement of profits and legal costs. Virginia is also asking for restitution for consumers.

## SpaceX's side

SpaceX did not immediately respond to CNBC. The company has defended itself in related cases: CNBC notes that in a suit by the city of Baltimore, it argued it is shielded by Section 230, the federal law that largely protects platforms from liability for users' content. In a separate case against a user, SpaceX's lawyers called Grok "a neutral tool, subject to user control." In October, a SpaceX AI policy lead told a New York City Council hearing the company has "strict controls" on its AI products.

As of Monday morning, CNBC reported, both suits were still pending acceptance by the courts. None of the allegations has been proven.

## Why it matters

This is a test of whether states can use ordinary consumer-protection law, the kind used against misleading ads, to hold AI companies responsible for what their image tools produce. Earlier state cases against social media companies have ended in big settlements; Meta agreed in August to pay states about $17 billion without admitting wrongdoing. If these suits succeed, every AI image generator could face pressure to tighten its filters.
