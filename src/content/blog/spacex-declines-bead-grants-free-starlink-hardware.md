**SpaceX** is giving up roughly **$738 million** in federal broadband grants it won through the **Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment (BEAD)** program, but says it will still serve the rural homes and businesses it was awarded, with **Starlink equipment at no cost** to those customers. The National Telecommunications and Information Administration (**NTIA**) announced the new "model agreement" on **Monday, November 23**, and sent it to state broadband offices that night.

## What happened

BEAD is the $42.45 billion federal program meant to bring high-speed internet to places that don't have it. After the program was reopened to satellite and wireless providers, Starlink won close to $700 million in awards across many states. SpaceX then complained that each state's contract terms were different, and in January said the conditions might make its participation "untenable," [Quartz](https://qz.com/spacex-738-million-broadband-grants-free-starlink-hardware-112526) reports, citing PCMag.

Under NTIA's [model agreement](https://broadbandusa.ntia.gov/sites/default/files/2026-11/BEAD_Model_Zero_Outlay_Agreement.pdf), a low-Earth-orbit provider that turns down the subsidy must still:

- Deliver BEAD speeds (at least **100 Mbps down and 20 Mbps up**)
- Provide customer equipment to BEAD subscribers at **no additional cost**
- Pass performance testing, offer a **low-cost plan**, and file reports twice a year

Customers would still pay the **monthly Starlink subscription**. Administration officials put the amount SpaceX is giving up at about $636 million, so reported totals differ.

"I am pleased this new agreement has the potential to save taxpayers hundreds of millions of dollars while getting more Americans connected faster," NTIA Administrator **Arielle Roth** said.

## The catches

States can accept the model agreement or find another provider for those locations at a similar cost to the original award. [Fierce Network](https://www.fierce-network.com/broadband/spacex-declines-bead-awards-putting-state-broadband-offices-pickle) notes that matching satellite prices with fiber or fixed wireless will be very hard. Because SpaceX stays in the program with a $0 award, other providers can't bid on those locations. Quartz, citing Teslarati, reports that states can't sanction SpaceX for missing targets, only hand locations back for re-award.

The Fiber Broadband Association asked NTIA to let states reopen those locations to competition. Minnesota's broadband director, Bree Maki, said her office had "not heard anything from SpaceX" and called it "another program change states are being asked to navigate."

## Why it matters

For rural households in Starlink's award areas, the practical change is a free dish instead of a subsidized one. For taxpayers, it frees hundreds of millions in grant money. For the broader fight over fiber vs. satellite, it keeps Starlink locked in those areas.
