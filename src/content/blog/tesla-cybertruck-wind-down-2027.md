If you own a Cybertruck — or you've been waiting on one — this is the Texas timeline. On Friday, Elon Musk said Tesla will be **"winding down"** Cybertruck production at **Gigafactory Texas around mid-2027** "to make room for increased Cybercab production."

That lands as a mega-cluster across [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/tesla-cybertruck-texas-musk.html), [NBC News / Reuters](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/tesla-wind-cybertruck-production-years-poor-sales-markets-rcna607817), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/transportation/1028542/tesla-cybertruck-winding-down-production-2027), and Musk's own X post. It's also near the top of Hacker News.

## What Musk actually said

Important nuance: Musk specified **Gigafactory Texas**, not a global kill order. That plant is the **only known Cybertruck assembly site**, so the practical impact is huge — but he did **not** say production ends everywhere or that the model is cancelled worldwide. Prefer the accurate line: winding down at Giga Texas around mid-2027 for Cybercab capacity.

Later, responding on X, Musk called Cybertruck the **"best vehicle Tesla has ever made"** and said the fleet **"will be around for a long time."** Cybertruck lead engineer Wes Morrill also said Tesla will keep spare parts and service support.

## The sales gap

Deliveries began in **late 2023**. Pre-launch, Musk once floated roughly **250,000 units a year**. Reality was much smaller:

- About **38,965** U.S. sales in **2024**
- About **20,237** in **2025** (roughly a 48% drop), per Kelley Blue Book figures cited by NBC/Reuters and Cox Automotive estimates in CNBC's report

The truck also racked up multiple recalls over its short life — CNBC notes the 2024 model alone had a long NHTSA recall list — which didn't help the mass-market pitch.

## Why Cybercab takes the floor space

Tesla is pushing hard on its **Cybercab** robotaxi bet. Freeing Texas line space for that two-seat, no-steering-wheel vehicle is the stated reason for the wind-down. For owners and shoppers, the clearest clock Musk gave is roughly through the **first half of 2027** at Giga Texas — unless Tesla fills in the blanks he left open (full stop vs residual rate, or any move to another plant).

So: if you've got one in the driveway, Musk says the fleet sticks around. If you're still deciding, the Texas build window just got a much clearer end date. Would you still order one with that mid-2027 clock ticking?
