Google is still growing. Search queries are growing at their fastest pace in five years, and Google Cloud more than doubled. So why did Wall Street just knock more than **half a trillion dollars** off Alphabet's value in one morning? Short version: AI is changing how people search, and right now those AI searches make Google less money.

## What happened

Alphabet reported its third-quarter results on Wednesday, October 28. On Thursday, October 29, the stock fell **more than 12%**, erasing **more than $500 billion** in market value, according to CNBC, Bloomberg and NBC News. That put it on track for its worst day since going public in 2004. (Markets were still open as this was written, so the final number may differ.)

The headline numbers from Alphabet's own report:

- **Revenue:** $127.6 billion, up 25% from a year ago.
- **Google Search & other:** $63.35 billion, up 12%. That's slower than the 17% growth in Q2, and it missed analyst estimates.
- **Google Cloud:** $30.7 billion, up **103%**.
- **Free cash flow:** **negative $9.07 billion**, as capital spending hit $51.36 billion in the quarter, much of it on servers, networking and data centers for AI.

## The AI Mode problem

Here's the part that matters most for regular users. On the earnings call, Google's chief business officer Philipp Schindler pinned part of the search slowdown on "lower monetization" in **AI Mode**, the chatbot-style tab in Google Search. In plain terms: when you ask AI Mode a long question and get a written answer, Google currently earns less from ads than when you click through a classic page of results.

People clearly like it. Google says AI Mode queries are about three times longer than regular searches, and the Gemini app passed 1 billion users. But usage that pays less is a tough trade when you're spending around $200 billion a year on AI infrastructure.

## What it means for you

Google's fix is pretty predictable: **more ads in AI answers**. Schindler said Google is "scaling high-quality ads within AI Overviews in AI Mode to more users and queries" and is expanding ads in AI Mode to international markets for the first time, along with new multi-turn ad formats in the U.S. Google says early click-through rates on sponsored listings in AI Mode are comparable to organic listings.

So if you've noticed AI answers in Search starting to look more like shopping carousels, expect more of that, in more countries.
