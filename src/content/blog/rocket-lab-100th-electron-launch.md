SpaceX's Starship grabbed the headlines this week, but another rocket company quietly hit a big round number. Rocket Lab's small **Electron** rocket flew for the **100th time**, and the company says no commercially developed rocket has ever gotten to 100 launches faster.

## What happened

Electron lifted off from Rocket Lab Launch Complex 1 in New Zealand at **6:30 p.m. local time on Saturday, November 14** (11:30 p.m. CT Friday, November 13). The mission, named "The Mountain God Rises," carried the **YAMATSUMI-II** radar imaging satellite for Japanese company iQPS and dropped it into an orbit about **575 km (357 miles)** up, according to [Rocket Lab](https://investors.rocketlabcorp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/rocket-lab-hits-100-electron-launches-fastest-commercial-rocket) and [Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/rocket-lab-100th-launch-iqps-electron-sar-satellite).

The rocket wore special black-and-gold livery for the occasion. It was Rocket Lab's 11th launch for iQPS, which has booked seven more Electron launches through 2030 to finish its constellation of radar satellites. Radar satellites can see through clouds and image Earth at night.

## The road to 100

- Electron first flew in **May 2017**, and that test flight failed. Its second launch, in **January 2018**, reached orbit.
- The rocket is about **18 meters (59 feet)** tall and built for small payloads. Rocket Lab calls it the world's first carbon-composite orbital rocket.
- Founder and CEO Peter Beck said on the company's November 9 earnings call that Electron had a **96% reliability rate across its first 99 launches**.
- Rocket Lab also flies a suborbital version called **HASTE** for hypersonic testing, which Space.com says has nine missions so far.

"Few rockets reach 100 launches in their total operational lifetime," Beck said in Rocket Lab's announcement. "The next 100 Electrons are already booked so we're only accelerating from here."

The "fastest" record is Rocket Lab's own claim. According to Space.com, Beck says that means Electron got there faster than even SpaceX's workhorse Falcon 9.

## Why it matters

A decade ago, small satellites usually had to hitch a ride on big rockets and wait for a spot. Electron helped prove there's a real business in dedicated rides for small spacecraft, from Earth-imaging companies to NASA science missions and national security customers. Rocket Lab's next step is **Neutron**, a larger, reusable rocket meant to compete for bigger payloads.
