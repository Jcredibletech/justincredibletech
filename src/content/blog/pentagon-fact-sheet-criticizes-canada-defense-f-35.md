The United States and Canada share a border, a continental air defense command and more than 75 years in NATO. This week the Pentagon put its complaints about Canada's military in writing, in public.

## What happened

The U.S. Department of War posted a fact sheet on U.S.-Canada defense relations on Monday, [CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/politics/pentagon-criticizes-canadian-military-readiness-9.7433335) reports. [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/22/pentagon-canada-defense-restrictions-fact-sheet) first reported its contents on Sunday and said it was issued to justify new limits on official contact between U.S. and Canadian military personnel. The fact sheet makes three main points.

**Readiness.** It says only **41.9%** of the Royal Canadian Air Force fleet was serviceable in 2025-26, **53.9%** of the navy's fleet, and that the army's readiness rate was **48%**. It says only one of Canada's four older Victoria-class submarines is seaworthy at a time.

**Spending.** It says Canada "has yet to articulate a resourced path" to NATO's target of 5% of GDP for defense by 2035, and is "deferring the largest increases" until after the 2029 federal election. Canada announced in March that it had reached NATO's earlier 2% goal, according to Axios.

**The F-35.** It notes Ottawa has been reviewing its planned purchase of **88 F-35 fighters** since March 2025 and is considering a mixed fleet, which the Pentagon says would add to personnel shortages. "Canada must get serious and focused," the fact sheet says.

## The restrictions

The fact sheet follows a Washington Post report that Secretary of War Pete Hegseth issued a classified directive limiting military cooperation with Canada. According to Axios and CBC, the limits require U.S. personnel to get permission before attending some events with Canadian counterparts. They do **not** affect intelligence sharing, day-to-day operations or defense planning. Sen. Mike Rounds (R-S.D.), who sits on the Armed Services Committee, called them largely symbolic, CBC reported.

## Canada's response

Canada's Department of National Defence said in a statement to Axios that "Canada continues to engage constructively and professionally with our American counterparts" and that the countries' "collaborative work continues." Axios also notes that Canada's own defense department found 61% of its military units ready for operations, while acknowledging the same equipment shortages. Defence Minister David McGuinty has described relations as "business as usual," according to CBC.

## Why it matters

The U.S. and Canada jointly run NORAD, which watches North American airspace. The Pentagon argues Canada's readiness gaps put more of that burden on U.S. forces and taxpayers. Canada says the partnership remains strong. What happens next likely depends on Ottawa's F-35 decision and on whether its next budgets lay out a clear path to the 5% target.
