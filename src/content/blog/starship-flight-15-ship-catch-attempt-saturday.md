**This is an update** to our Friday morning story, [Starship Flight 15 Targets Friday Launch and Double Catch](https://justincredibletech.com/blog/starship-flight-15-launch-day-faa-license/). The rocket flew, but the "double catch" is now down to one.

## What happened Friday

SpaceX launched Starship Flight 15 from Starbase, Texas, at about **4:47 p.m. Central on Friday, November 13**. The plan was to catch *both* stages with the launch tower's "chopstick" arms, something no one has ever done with an orbital rocket.

The first half didn't happen. The Super Heavy booster (Booster 22) headed back toward the tower as planned, but late in its descent it **aborted itself**. "We were initially 'go' for a catch," SpaceX spokesperson Dan Huot said on the webcast. "But in the later stages of the coast, the booster aborted itself for a GNC issue that it detected." GNC stands for guidance, navigation and control. The booster diverted to its backup landing zone in the Gulf, and its flight termination system destroyed it just above the water about 6.5 minutes after liftoff, according to [Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-says-starship-flight-15-booster-aborted-chopsticks-landing-catch-after-launch-due-to-guidance-issue-video) and [AFP](https://www.afp.com/en/spacexs-starship-set-return-earth-after-booster-catch-aborted).

SpaceX has caught Super Heavy boosters on three earlier flights, so this was a miss on a maneuver it had already proven.

## The Ship is in orbit and healthy

The upper stage (Ship 42) reached orbit and deployed **26 Starlink V3 satellites**, SpaceX said. AFP reports SpaceX described the Ship as "healthy." Huot said the heat shield team checked the tiles using ground tracking cameras and cameras on some of the new Starlink satellites, and "confirmed the heat shield is in a good posture for a reentry and return to the launch site," per [Space.com's live coverage](https://www.space.com/news/live/spacex-starship-flight-15-live-updates-nov-14-2026-starship-first-double-landing-attempt).

## What to watch today

- **Catch time:** about **3:20 p.m. Central (21:20 UTC) on Saturday, November 14**, roughly 22.5 hours after liftoff and about 14 orbits.
- **Final go/no-go:** Space.com says controllers make a last call around 20 hours into the flight (about 1 p.m. Central). If anything looks off, SpaceX says it will skip the catch and send the Ship to an ocean splashdown instead, just as the booster bailed out on Friday.
- **Why it's hard:** AFP notes the Ship falls from much higher and much faster than the booster, so it has to survive a full reentry before lining up with the tower.

## Why it matters

A caught Ship would be the **first recovery of a Starship upper stage from orbit**. Reusing both stages is the core of SpaceX's plan to make launches far cheaper and more frequent, and Space.com notes Starship is slated to fly an Artemis III demonstration in Earth orbit in summer 2027. Even with the booster loss, a successful Ship catch would be a major milestone. A wave-off would not be a failure of the test by itself; it would mean the safety logic did its job again.
