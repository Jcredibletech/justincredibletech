**This is an update** to our afternoon report, [FAA Issues a Nationwide Ground Stop After a Communication Issue at Air Traffic Control Centers](https://justincredibletech.com/blog/faa-nationwide-ground-stop-communication-issue/).

**Correction:** That earlier report said the FAA's live status board no longer listed a nationwide stop as of 3:32 p.m. Central. The ground stop had not ended. The FAA's Command Center reissued the order at 4:46 p.m. Central saying it "remains in effect," and it was still in place as of 7 p.m. Central.

## Where things stand (as of about 7 p.m. Central, Friday)

- **Still grounded.** The FAA ordered all departing flights at U.S. airports held at 2:54 p.m. Central (3:54 p.m. Eastern) "due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety." A [follow-up FAA advisory](https://www.fly.faa.gov/ois/oisedit/adv_oisedit_dis?day=11132026&number=120) issued at 4:46 p.m. Central lists the reason as a "communications failure," says the ground stop "remains in effect," and says the next update is expected around **10 p.m. Central**. It adds that "as en route facilities recover, flights will be allowed to depart" in coordination with the FAA Command Center.
- **The numbers.** Flight tracker [FlightAware](https://www.flightaware.com/live/cancelled/) showed about **5,200 cancellations** and about **9,500 delays** for flights within, into or out of the U.S. on Friday as of about 7 p.m. Central. [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/live-blog/live-updates-flights-grounded-faa-communications-outage-rcna609268) reported that the previous day saw about 1,024 U.S. cancellations.
- **Some exceptions.** Certain flights within Alaska and Hawaii and some international departures were cleared, [the AP reported](https://apnews.com/article/faa-us-flights-grounded-extraordinary-circumstances-6aaa6bd4991ef22eb0c3baa6961a91d1). San Francisco International said Pacific-bound flights, including to Hawaii and Asia, were cleared to leave starting at 3 p.m. Pacific, according to NBC News. Flights already in the air continued to land.
- **Airports filling up.** FAA notices show Washington's Reagan National (DCA) and Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood (FLL) closed to diverted aircraft, with Fort Lauderdale's notice citing capacity. NBC News reported, citing a source, that Reagan National was at capacity and Chicago O'Hare was nearing it.

## What officials say caused it

The FAA said on X: "There has been a temporary telecommunications outage — out of an abundance of caution all flights have been halted. Every flight has been accounted for, and international flights will be handled on a case by case basis."

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the problem affected the Air Route Traffic Control Centers, the regional facilities that handle planes between airports. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told reporters there was "no indication as of yet" that anything "nefarious" was behind it, calling it "just a telecommunications outage," while adding that officials are "not ruling out anything at this point," according to NBC News and [WBEZ Chicago](https://www.wbez.org/transportation/2026/11/13/all-departing-flights-grounded-nationwide-due-to-faa-order).

Asked about the FAA's new AI-assisted SMART traffic-management system, which the agency said this week was active nationwide, Mullin said, "You just can't rule out anything at this point." Air Space Intelligence, the vendor behind SMART, said in a statement that the ground stop "is not based on or a result of the SMART system." Officials have not announced a root cause. Microsoft said it has "no indication" the ground stop is related to its recent service outage.

## Why it's rare

A nationwide ground stop has happened only a handful of times. The FAA halted all civilian air traffic after the September 11, 2001, attacks, and it grounded departures nationwide in January 2023 after a failure in its NOTAM system, which officials later blamed on contract personnel who accidentally deleted files.

## If you're flying this weekend

A ground stop holds departures; it does not automatically cancel every flight. Check your airline's app before heading to the airport, and expect knock-on delays even after flights resume, because planes and crews are out of position.
