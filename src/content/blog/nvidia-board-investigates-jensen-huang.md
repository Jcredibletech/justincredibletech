If there's a GeForce card in your gaming PC, or you've asked a chatbot anything this week, Nvidia's hardware is part of your life. Tonight the company that makes it is investigating its own founder. Yesterday we covered reports that China's spy agency targeted CEO Jensen Huang. Today Huang confirmed the personal part, and "Jensen" and "Nvidia" both shot into X's US trending list, alongside Sunday football. Posts about it jumped from about 100–180 an hour to more than 5,000 an hour after the news broke.

## What happened today

- **Huang's email to staff.** In a message titled "A message to our company," which Nvidia also posted to its newsroom, Huang wrote: "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did." He said the board has opened an independent investigation into "the national security concerns raised in news reports," that he supports it and will cooperate fully, and asked for privacy for his family ([Nvidia](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/a-message-to-our-company)).
- **The board's statement.** A committee of independent directors, working with outside counsel, will run the investigation "in light of recent reporting that founder and CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort." The board says it "has no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised," but will "examine all available information," and that it has confidence in Huang ([Nvidia](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors)).
- **He's staying.** "Today's news will not slow NVIDIA. Jensen is committed to continuing to lead NVIDIA as CEO without interruption," an Nvidia spokesperson said ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/08/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-admits-affair-board-to-launch-investigation), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/08/nvidia-jensen-huang-investigation-china-spy.html)).

## The China connection (as reported)

The New York Times and Financial Times reported that U.S. intelligence officials believe the woman, an executive at Nvidia cloud customer **Megaspeed**, was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security. Megaspeed has been accused of helping restricted Chinese companies get Nvidia AI chips. Nvidia has said Megaspeed was already investigated over export controls ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/08/nvidia-jensen-huang-investigation-china-spy.html)). The Times also reports that Secretary of State Marco Rubio oversaw a classified memo this spring warning administration officials about the relationship. None of the espionage claims have been proven in public, and China has called earlier reports "baseless." House Republican committee chairs had already asked Huang to a closed-door briefing on November 18, the day after Nvidia's earnings.

## My take

Huang's marriage is his family's business, and I'm not going to pile on there. The part that's everyone's business is the chips. The most valuable hardware in the AI race is made by one American company, and U.S. officials think Beijing's top spy agency went after its CEO through a customer accused of moving chips into China. That's a pretty good argument for enforcing the export rules we already have. Look harder at middlemen and resellers instead of loosening sales to China. An independent board probe is the right first step, and I'd rather see the free market win the AI race than hand our best tech to a rival government.
