Apple dropped its first foldable yesterday. Today the executives showed up to explain the part everyone was already arguing about in group chats: **the price**.

In a joint interview with *Tom’s Guide* and *TechRadar* (also covered by MacRumors), new Apple CEO **John Ternus** and marketing chief **Greg Joswiak** walked through why Duo took so long, how they kept it quiet, and why it starts at **$1,999**.

Joswiak’s line is the keepers’ quote: **“It’s called iPhone Duo for a reason. It’s like two products.”**

Ternus’s version: yes it’s more expensive than other iPhones, but Apple thinks it’s “great value” for an “incredibly complex and well-engineered device.” Classic Cupertino framing—you’re not buying a phone, you’re buying a *category*.

## What’s actually new in today’s interview (vs the unveil)

The September 10 keynote already gave us the hardware postcard: foldable iPhone, big inner canvas, A20 Pro, star white / night sky, **preorders October 16**, **availability October 23**.

Today’s chat fills in the *why*:

**They waited on purpose.** Ternus said Apple ships when an idea hits the bar for quality, durability, *and* design—not when rivals already have foldables on shelves. Joswiak said other foldables didn’t feel “particularly inspiring,” and Apple’s pitch is twofold (pun intentional on their side): a wider **~1:1.4** aspect ratio so it doesn’t feel like two phones taped together, plus software that makes Duo “unlike any foldable that’s out there.”

**The screen science.** Ternus said the inner display’s top layer is **polymer, not glass**, so Apple had to invent a new way to apply its **nano-texture** finish. His dig at rivals: plastic surfaces that feel cheap, wavy, wrinkly. Nano-texture, he claims, makes it flat, durable, and nice to touch.

**Secrecy flex.** Joswiak said they put “extra security” on the project, used a “very funny” code name he won’t repeat, and mostly succeeded at keeping the *finished* product under wraps—even while leaks correctly sniffed the aspect ratio. He sounded genuinely proud of that part.

None of that makes $1,999 cheaper. It does explain why Apple thinks you should feel okay paying laptop money for pocket glass.

## The calendar (so you can panic-schedule)

- **Preorders:** Friday, **October 16** (5 a.m. PT / 8 a.m. ET in the U.S. and many other countries)
- **In hand / in store:** Friday, **October 23** (70+ countries first; more regions Oct 30)

Storage climbs past the $1,999 256GB base—higher capacities go well into the $2k–$3k range, depending on config. Carrier financing will soften the sticker for some people; for others it just spreads the sticker out.

## Evening poll: be honest

Forget the nano-texture for a second. Gut check:

**A) Day-one buy** — $1,999, I’m in; “two products” sells me  
**B) Wait** — reviews, crease checks, and maybe a trade-in deal first  
**C) Skip** — Pro Max / current phone is enough; foldable tax is too high  
**D) Showroom only** — I’ll fold one in an Apple Store and leave empty-handed (for now)

There’s no wrong answer—just different budgets and different FOMO tolerances.

## Why this argument matters

Foldables aren’t new. Apple’s first foldable *is*. When the company that popularized “it just works” finally enters a category, it sets the default expectation for everyone else: display feel, software multitasking, durability theater, and—yes—price anchors.

If Duo lands as a niche flex for people who already buy Pro Max + iPad mini vibes in one slab, Apple still wins the headline. If enough people treat “two products” as marketing poetry and wait for Duo 2, the $1,999 experiment becomes a very expensive lesson in patience.

Either way, October 16 is circled. The only open question is whether *your* thumb hits Pre-Order or Close Tab.
