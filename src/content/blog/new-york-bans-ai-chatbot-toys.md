New York Gov. **Kathy Hochul** signed a law on **Tuesday, Nov. 24** that puts a **five-year moratorium** on making, distributing and selling toys with built-in AI chatbots in the state. That covers talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots that use the internet to hold conversations with kids. The ban takes effect **Feb. 22, 2027**, 90 days after signing, so it does not apply to this holiday season.

## What happened

The bill, **S.9408A/A.11144B**, was sponsored by state Sen. Andrew Gounardes and Assemblymember Rebecca Kassay. According to the [governor's office](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/consumers-begin-their-holiday-shopping-season-governor-hochul-signs-five-year-ban-ai-chatbot):

- Violations can bring civil penalties of up to **$15,000 per day** for each violation, enforced by the state attorney general.
- The ban runs five years, which supporters say gives the state time to study the toys and write rules.
- The office cited a national consumer watchdog that tested chatbot toys marketed to kids ages 3 to 12 and found some discussed sexually explicit topics, told kids where matches might be in the home, and acted upset when a child had to leave.

[NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/new-york-governor-kathy-hochul-sign-ai-toy-ban-law-rcna610962) reports New York is the second state to ban the toys, after California.

## What each side says

Hochul said parents "should not have to worry that dangerous chatbots disguised as toys could expose their children to inappropriate conversations, manipulate their feelings or put their privacy at risk." Attorney General Letitia James said her office "will not hesitate to enforce this law."

The toy industry opposed a ban. [The Toy Association](https://toyfoundation.org/ttf/PressRoom2/News/2026-News/new-york-governor-signs-ai-chatbot-toy-bill-into-law.aspx) says it has "maintained a consistent position opposed to moratoriums and in support of a study approach," and that federal legislation was changed to a study approach instead of a product ban after talks with the group. It has published its own safety guidelines for AI-enabled toys and says it will share compliance guidance with members.

## Why it matters

This is one of the first outright product bans aimed at consumer AI. Toy makers selling nationally will have to decide whether to pull chatbot toys from New York, build state-specific versions, or drop the feature. It follows New York's October law limiting certain chatbot features for minors, and it lands as more states write their own AI rules. We've covered similar state moves, like [New York's ban on car feature subscription fees](/blog/new-york-bans-car-feature-subscription-fees/) and [Oregon and Virginia suing over Grok's "spicy mode"](/blog/oregon-virginia-sue-spacex-grok-spicy-mode/).

## What's next

The law kicks in Feb. 22, 2027 and lasts five years. Watch for the attorney general's enforcement guidance and whether Congress takes up the study-first federal approach the Toy Association describes.
