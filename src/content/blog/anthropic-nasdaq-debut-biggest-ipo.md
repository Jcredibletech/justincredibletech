*Update to our November 15 story on Anthropic's IPO week. Pricing is done and the stock is listed.*

Anthropic, the company behind the **Claude** AI models, is now a public company.

## What happened

On Wednesday, November 18, Anthropic [priced its IPO](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering) at **$83 a share**, above the $70 to $78 range it had marketed. It sold **980 million shares** (about 956 million from the company and the rest from existing shareholders), raising roughly **$81 billion**. That makes it the **largest IPO on record**, topping SpaceX's June offering, according to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/anthropic-ipo-ant-live-updates.html) and the [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/live/c933xxm82mr8t). At that price the company is valued at about **$1.5 trillion**.

Shares became eligible to trade on the Nasdaq under the ticker **ANT** on Thursday, November 19, at 10 a.m. ET, [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/markets/live-blog/anthropic-ipo-live-updates-rcna610159) reported. As of late morning Eastern time, before the first trade, CNBC reported the stock was **indicated to open around $105**, about 27% above the IPO price. Check a live quote for where it's trading now.

## The numbers behind it

- **Revenue:** $33.24 billion for the nine months ended September 30, up about 1,200% from a year earlier, per the prospectus as reported by CNBC.
- **Losses:** a $104 billion net loss for the same period, most of it (about $94.3 billion) from accounting changes in the value of convertible notes and similar instruments, CNBC reported.
- **Control:** Anthropic's seven co-founders hold majority voting power through a new entity called Founder LLC.

## What the CEO said

Before the opening bell, CEO **Dario Amodei** said the company "will pace the training and deployment of AI systems, taking as long as is necessary to have confidence that they are safe and will remain under human control," NBC News reported. "We will not bend to either business nor political pressure," he added.

CNBC noted Anthropic has faced sharp criticism over its positions on safety, regulation and national security, including from President Trump and other senior officials.

## Why it matters

This is the first time everyday investors can buy shares of a major stand-alone AI lab, and Wall Street sees it as a test of demand for AI stocks. Rivals are watching closely: OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said Wednesday, "We plan to be public at some point," NBC News reported. This is news, not investment advice.
