*This is an update to our November 21 story, [BepiColombo Is Set to Enter Orbit Around Mercury Today After an Eight-Year Trip](/blog/bepicolombo-mercury-orbit-insertion/).*

Yesterday we reported that BepiColombo was about to attempt the most important maneuver of its journey. It worked.

## What happened

On **Saturday, November 21, 2026**, the European Space Agency announced that BepiColombo had been captured into orbit around Mercury. The four thrusters on ESA's Mercury Planetary Orbiter fired for about 37 minutes starting around 18:24 GMT (12:24 PM CT) to slow the spacecraft enough for Mercury's gravity to hold it, according to Spanish broadcaster [RTVE](https://www.rtve.es/noticias/20261122/mision-espacial-bepicolombo-entra-orbita-mercurio-travesia-ocho-anos/17325481.shtml).

ESA then had to wait to hear from the spacecraft. "We have news from Bepi," the agency posted, saying the spacecraft was in contact with ground control and that its flight dynamics team had confirmed orbital capture. [The Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/mercury-bepicolombo-space-planet-esa-0c80bbf2039ed4f6ee4a34426b3594a0) reported the "pair of spacecraft slipped into orbit around Mercury on Saturday," and [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03803-8) called it the end of an "epic eight-year journey."

That makes BepiColombo only the **second mission ever to orbit Mercury**, after NASA's MESSENGER, which circled the planet from 2011 to 2015. AP puts the mission's cost at about $2 billion (1.7 billion euros).

## What comes next

BepiColombo is really two science spacecraft traveling as one stack:

- ESA's **Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO)**, which will map the surface, interior and composition.
- JAXA's **Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter, nicknamed Mio**, which will study Mercury's magnetic field.

ESA's timeline calls for the two to **separate on December 9-10**, which would make BepiColombo the first mission with two spacecraft orbiting Mercury at once. MPO will then fire its thrusters several more times to reach its final science orbit by **March 2027**, and science operations are planned to begin in **April 2027**. RTVE reported ESA will share its next update early this week.

## Why it matters

Mercury is the hardest planet to orbit because the Sun's gravity speeds up anything falling toward it; BepiColombo needed nine planetary flybys to slow down. Scientists hope two orbiters watching from different altitudes can explain why such a small planet has an oversized iron core and its own magnetic field. Nature notes the spacecraft has already tested instruments on the way, including MIXS, the first X-ray imaging camera sent on an interplanetary mission.
