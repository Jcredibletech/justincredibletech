The next car you buy may have been touched by more robots than ever. General Motors is quietly planning a big step up in factory automation, according to a new report.

## What happened

**Reuters** reported on **Tuesday, November 17, 2026**, that GM is aiming to increase the use of **robots and other automation by 20%** at its **North American factories by 2028**. The report, republished by [CNA](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/exclusive-gm-plans-20-boost-in-robotics-factory-costs-rise-6558871) and [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/exclusivegm-plans-20-boost-in-robotics-as-factory-costs-rise-5032317), cites two people familiar with the matter. GM has not publicly announced the 20% figure.

According to the report:

- GM is **developing its own robots** and **assembling them at its Kokomo, Indiana, plant**, which hadn't been reported before.
- The focus is on **autonomous mobile robots** that move parts around plants.
- GM is still buying robotics equipment from outside suppliers too.
- GM is asking **parts suppliers** bidding on new contracts to submit **automation plans** of their own, and is using a scorecard to rate those plans when it awards work.

Trade outlet [CBT News](https://www.cbtnews.com/gm-targets-2028-factory-automation/) also covered the plan, citing the Reuters report.

## What GM says

GM didn't confirm the target, but said in a statement to Reuters that it uses automation to improve safety, quality and efficiency. "As competition intensifies globally, we continue to put these technologies to work in ways that support our manufacturing teams," the company said.

## Why GM is doing this

The report ties the push to rising costs. When the **United Auto Workers** won record contract gains in 2023, GM estimated the deal would add about **$575 in labor costs per vehicle**; Ford put its figure at about $900, Reuters notes. President Trump's push to bring more factory work back to the U.S. has also added pressure on production costs, according to the report. Detroit automakers have long argued their labor costs are higher than those of foreign rivals and of Tesla, whose U.S. plants are largely nonunion.

## The labor angle

The current UAW contracts expire in **2028**, the same year as GM's reported target. UAW President **Shawn Fain** has already warned that AI and increased automation pose a threat to workers, Reuters reports. Expect automation to be a central issue when bargaining starts.

## Why it matters to you

For car buyers, automation is one way automakers try to hold down costs that would otherwise show up in sticker prices. For autoworkers and the towns built around plants, it raises real questions about how many jobs these factories will need in the future. Both things can be true at once, and that tension is likely to shape the industry over the next few years.
