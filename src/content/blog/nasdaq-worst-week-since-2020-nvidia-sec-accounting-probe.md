If you have a 401(k) or an index fund, this was the week the AI trade bit back.

On Friday, November 20, 2026, the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% to 22,617.73, wiping out its gain for the year, [CNBC reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/stock-market-today-live-updates.html). The S&P 500 dropped 3.7% to 6,910.39 and the Dow fell 949.73 points, or 1.93%, to 48,147.33. Both the S&P 500 and the Dow closed at least 10% below their records, which is what Wall Street calls a correction.

For the week, the Nasdaq fell about 10%, its biggest weekly drop since 2020, according to [Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nasdaq-plunges-5-a-day-after-anthropic-debut-but-investors-still-believe-in-ai-5042641). The Dow lost 8%, its worst week since March 2020, and the S&P 500 fell 8.6%, its worst week since the April 2025 tariff sell-off, CNBC said. Wall Street's fear gauge, the VIX, jumped 33% to 37.

## Nvidia led the slide

Nvidia fell about 14% on Friday. The stock was already down more than 6% when [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/5f374b32b07b/world/us-secs-nvidia-probe-is-examining-potential-accounting-issues-sources-say-2026-11-20/), citing two people briefed on the matter, that Securities and Exchange Commission enforcement staff are scrutinizing potential accounting issues at the company. The Wall Street Journal had reported on Tuesday that the SEC was probing Nvidia but could not say what the focus was.

Some important context from that report:

- The SEC declined to comment, and Nvidia did not immediately respond.
- Reuters could not determine when the inquiry opened or exactly which accounting issues are being examined.
- Investigations are not evidence of wrongdoing, and many close without any enforcement action.

Reuters noted renewed scrutiny of a change Nvidia made last year to how it reports revenue by region (using customer headquarters instead of billing addresses), which removed Singapore from its geographic breakdown. Analysts have also raised questions about "circular" AI financing deals, where chipmakers help customers pay for chips and data centers.

According to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/nvidias-7-week-losing-streak-is-longest-since-2008-financial-crisis.html), Friday capped Nvidia's seventh straight losing week, its longest streak since 2008. The stock is down 46% over that stretch, about $2.6 trillion in market value, and Nvidia is now the fourth most valuable company behind Apple, Microsoft and Alphabet. That's despite a better-than-expected earnings report on Tuesday from interim CEO Colette Kress, who took over after co-founder Jensen Huang [resigned on November 12](/blog/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo/).

## Not just Nvidia

AMD and Micron each fell roughly 13% to 14%. Anthropic, which [debuted on the Nasdaq on Thursday](/blog/anthropic-nasdaq-debut-biggest-ipo/), gave back about 8.5% after its 17% first-day jump, CNBC said. Even so, many investors told Reuters they still believe in the long-term AI trade; the selling reflects doubts about how fast the AI spending boom can keep growing.
