For people treated for the most common type of breast cancer, the years after surgery are about one thing: keeping it from coming back. On Monday the FDA approved a new pill for that job, the first new option of its kind in more than two decades.

## What happened

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration [approved Wilserdly (giredestrant)](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-new-hormone-therapy-early-stage-breast-cancer-more-20-years), a once-daily tablet from Genentech, part of the Roche Group. It is approved for adults with **estrogen receptor-positive, HER2-negative, stage I, II or III breast cancer** who have an intermediate to high risk of recurrence after surgery. The FDA calls it the first new hormone therapy for early-stage breast cancer in more than 20 years.

This is a full approval, not an experimental treatment. One week earlier, on November 16, the FDA approved Wilserdly with another drug, everolimus, for certain advanced breast cancers with an ESR1 mutation. Monday's approval is the first for early-stage disease.

## What the trial showed

The approval rests on **lidERA**, a large phase 3 trial of 4,170 adults who were randomly assigned to Wilserdly or standard hormone therapy (tamoxifen or an aromatase inhibitor) for up to five years:

- Wilserdly cut the risk of invasive cancer returning or death by **30%** (140 events vs. 196).
- At three years, **92.4%** of Wilserdly patients were alive and free of invasive breast cancer, compared with **89.6%** on standard therapy. The FDA says that's about 28 more people out of every 1,000.
- It is **not yet known** whether the drug helps people live longer. Patients are still being followed, and Genentech has agreed to submit those results.

[MedicalDaily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/giredestrant-early-breast-cancer-fda-decision-lidera-481591) notes some limits: the trial was open-label (patients and doctors knew which drug was given) and funded by Roche, and results beyond about three years aren't known yet.

## Side effects

The FDA lists the most common side effects as muscle and joint pain, hot flashes, tiredness, headache, nausea, a slower-than-normal heart rate, dizziness, and numbness or tingling in the hands and feet. It may harm a developing baby. Women who haven't gone through menopause also need a medicine that suppresses ovarian hormones. In the trial, fewer people quit Wilserdly because of side effects than quit standard therapy (5.3% vs. 8.2%), according to MedicalDaily.

## Why it matters

The FDA estimates about **321,910 new breast cancer cases in women** in the U.S. this year, and about 70% are this ER-positive, HER2-negative type. Wilserdly works by blocking and breaking down the estrogen receptor that feeds these tumors. MedicalDaily reported that Roche had not announced a price for early-stage use as of the weekend.

This is news, not medical advice. Anyone on hormone therapy should not stop or switch medicines on their own; talk with an oncologist about whether this option fits.
