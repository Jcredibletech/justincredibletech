Canada and Stellantis, the company behind Jeep, Ram, Dodge and Chrysler, are heading toward a legal fight over an empty car factory. On Thursday, November 12, 2026, Canada's federal government **served Stellantis a formal notice of default** on a funding agreement tied to its **Brampton, Ontario, assembly plant**.

## What happened

- **The plant.** Brampton was supposed to be retooled to build the next **Jeep Compass**, including an electric version. Stellantis paused that plan in February 2025 and later moved Compass production to **Illinois**, leaving more than 2,000 workers laid off and the plant idle ([The Canadian Press via CP24](https://www.cp24.com/politics/queens-park/2026/11/12/ottawa-serves-stellantis-notice-of-default-on-funding-agreement-after-roshel-deal/)).
- **The sale.** On Monday, Stellantis agreed to sell the plant to **Roshel**, a Brampton-based defense manufacturer. The deal is expected to close by year's end.
- **The notice.** Industry Minister **Mélanie Joly** says Stellantis broke the terms of a 2022 deal by halting Compass production, not naming a replacement vehicle and closing the plant. The company has **30 days** to show how it will fix that. "Basically they have 30 days or else, of course, we'll sue them," Joly told reporters in Toronto ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/13/canada-stellantis-auto-trump-tariffs-jeep.html)).

## The money gap

In 2022, Ottawa committed up to **C$529 million** to help retool Stellantis' Brampton and Windsor plants for electrified vehicles, and Ontario committed **C$513 million**. Stellantis Canada CEO **Trevor Longley** said the company will voluntarily return **$24 million** it received for Brampton's original retooling plan. Joly says the company could owe "hundreds of millions of dollars," plus interest.

Longley said the Roshel deal gives the site "a path to return the facility to productive industrial use," with priority hiring for Brampton workers. Roshel plans to lease about a quarter of the space back to Stellantis for automotive work.

## What Canadian politicians are saying

Prime Minister **Mark Carney** called the Roshel deal a "partial solution" and said Stellantis has not met its obligations to all of its workers. Conservative Leader **Pierre Poilievre** asked whether the government secured any job guarantee for the taxpayer money it handed over, and called the situation "negligence on a mass scale" if it did not. The union Unifor, whose members voted 97.3% to authorize a possible strike last month, says a strike remains a last resort.

## Why it matters to U.S. readers

Stellantis paused the Brampton plan a month after President Trump returned to office and began imposing tariffs, and the move shifts production to the U.S. The Compass that was meant to be built in Ontario will now come from an American plant. Canada's response is a test of how far governments can go to claw back subsidies when automakers change plans.
