If you're flying Delta this weekend, a piece of software you've never heard of is probably why you're still at the gate.

A problem with Delta Air Lines' automated weight-and-balance system delayed more than 1,000 Delta flights on Sunday, November 22, 2026, the first big travel day of Thanksgiving week. Flight-tracking site FlightAware counted nearly 1,300 delayed Delta flights by Sunday afternoon, [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/delta-flight-delays-automated-system-issue-tech-2026-11) reported. As of 12:41 p.m. ET, 862 flights into and out of Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport had been delayed and 16 canceled, according to FlightAware data cited by [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/delta-tech-issues-thanksgiving-travel-rcna610685).

## What broke

Before any airliner takes off, the crew needs a final load sheet: the plane's total weight and where that weight sits (its center of gravity) after passengers, bags, cargo and fuel are loaded. Pilots use those numbers to set takeoff speeds and engine thrust. Airlines normally calculate this automatically. With Delta's system down, staff had to do the calculations by hand, which slows every departure.

"Delta teams are using manual workarounds this morning to safely get flights departed while work continues to restore automation," Delta said in a statement to several outlets. "We thank our customers for their patience and our people for their professionalism." Delta has not said what caused the failure. As of early Sunday afternoon, the airline had not said the system was fully restored, [The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/news/delta-air-lines-tech-outage-messy-sunday-thanksgiving-travel) reported.

## The FAA's role

At Delta's request, the Federal Aviation Administration briefly issued a ground stop for Atlanta-bound Delta flights Sunday morning, then replaced it with a ground delay program that let flights depart at staggered times. The FAA projected average delays of 57 minutes and up to about two hours. That program has since been lifted, according to [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/delta-air-lines-nationwide-delays-amid-baggage-network-system-issue/) and NBC News.

The FAA said on X that the delays "are not related to any FAA air traffic systems," the [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/delta-atlanta-flight-delays-d4bdb732da930205ec3453d35c28daa2) and others reported. Hartsfield-Jackson said the disruption was limited to Delta and was not airport-wide.

## Third flight tech outage in 10 days

This is the latest in a run of aviation tech problems:

- **November 13:** The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop after a communications problem in its air traffic control network ([our coverage](https://justincredibletech.com/blog/faa-ground-stop-still-in-effect-friday-night/)).
- **November 16:** A separate system outage delayed United Airlines departures, affecting crew communications and the airline's ability to send pilots final weight data, according to the pilots' union, NBC News reported. United said it fixed the issue that day.
- **November 22:** Delta's weight-and-balance outage.

There's no indication the incidents are connected, and the FAA says Sunday's problem is Delta's alone.

## What to do if you're flying

- Check your flight status in the Delta app or on delta.com before heading to the airport.
- Hartsfield-Jackson recommends arriving at least two hours early for domestic flights and three hours for international ones.
- Delays can ripple into later flights as planes and crews get out of position, so keep an eye on connections.
