Four anonymous social media users are suing to stop a federal grand jury investigation that they say is aimed at unmasking people who criticize **U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE)** online. The class-action lawsuit, filed under seal on **Nov. 16** in federal court in Washington, D.C., was made public on **Friday, Nov. 27**. The **ACLU** represents the plaintiffs. The government has not yet responded in court.

## What happened

According to the [complaint](https://assets.aclu.org/live/uploads/2026/11/19-1.-Redacted-Complaint.pdf), federal prosecutors sent grand jury subpoenas to **Bluesky, X and Meta** asking for the four users' identifying details: names, addresses, phone and device numbers, IP addresses, payment information, and any history of account suspensions for "threatening or harassing language." The subpoenas all carry the same investigation number, which the lawsuit calls **"Investigation 18."**

The plaintiffs are two Bluesky users, one Instagram user and one X user, all suing under pseudonyms. They say they have no link to each other beyond posting criticism of the administration's immigration enforcement. The suit claims at least **35** grand jury subpoenas have been issued under Investigation 18. One court filing says Bluesky told a user that the requesting agency entered the case category as **"Violence."** The plaintiffs say they have not been involved in any criminal activity and don't advocate violence.

The defendants are **U.S. Attorney for D.C. Jeanine Pirro**, **Attorney General Todd Blanche** and **Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin**, each in their official capacity.

## What each side says

The ACLU argues the investigation violates the **First and Fourth Amendments**. "The government is weaponizing the grand jury process to retaliate against people for expressing their political views online," ACLU attorney **Laura Moraff** said in the group's [press release](https://www.aclu.org/press-releases/lawsuit-alleges-sprawling-federal-grand-jury-investigation-into-anonymous-ice-critics-online).

The government hasn't responded publicly. Spokespeople for DHS and Pirro's office didn't immediately respond to the [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/trump-aclu-anonymous-ice-critics-social-media-5f2b57cdfd67d92b8eb6b810ca2607a0). Grand jury proceedings are secret under federal rules, which limits what prosecutors can say about an active investigation. The Department of Homeland Security has repeatedly said ICE officers face sharply rising [assaults and threats](https://www.dhs.gov/news/2026/09/24/dhs-calls-sanctuary-politicians-stop-violent-rhetoric-against-ice-assaults-increase), and that some critics have tried to expose officers' identities, as in a [North Carolina case DHS publicized in March](https://www.dhs.gov/news/2026/03/31/anti-ice-agitator-doxxes-ice-officer-north-carolina). Some news outlets have questioned how DHS counts those numbers. Every claim in the lawsuit is still an allegation that hasn't been tested in court.

## Why it matters

This case sits where two big questions meet: how far the government can go to investigate possible threats against federal officers, and how much protection anonymous online speech gets. The complaint says it's the first broad civil challenge to these grand jury subpoenas to be argued in public. The outcome could shape how often platforms like X, Meta and Bluesky have to hand over users' identities. We've covered other fights over online speech and platforms, including [Oregon and Virginia's lawsuit against SpaceX over Grok](/blog/oregon-virginia-sue-spacex-grok-spicy-mode/).

## What's next

Chief Judge **James Boasberg** unsealed the case. The government's written response is due **Dec. 14**, and a hearing on the request to halt the investigation is set for **Jan. 6, 2027**, according to the AP. One subpoena gave Bluesky until **Dec. 11** to respond, according to the complaint.
