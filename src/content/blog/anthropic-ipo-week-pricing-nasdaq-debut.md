Back in October we covered [Anthropic filing to go public](/blog/anthropic-ipo-s1-filing/). Now the Claude maker is in the final stretch: its investor roadshow wraps up this week, and the stock is expected to start trading on the Nasdaq on **Thursday, November 19**.

## Where things stand

Anthropic [launched its roadshow on November 10](https://www.anthropic.com/news/roadshow-launch). Here's what the company has said officially:

- It is offering **825 million shares** of Class A stock. About **801.4 million** are new shares from Anthropic, and about **23.6 million** are being sold by existing shareholders (Anthropic gets no money from those).
- The expected price range is **$70 to $78 per share**. At that range, the 825 million shares add up to roughly $58 billion to $64 billion.
- Underwriters get a 30-day option to buy up to **123.75 million** more shares.
- The planned listing is the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker **ANT**. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are the lead banks named in the announcement.

[Reuters reported](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/exclusiveinvestors-flock-to-anthropic-ipo-but-question-durability-of-ai-lead-5026644) on November 13 that the roadshow runs through **Wednesday**, when the deal is expected to be priced, and that the order book was about **six times oversubscribed** by Thursday, according to people familiar with the offering. Those people cautioned that the details could change, and Anthropic declined to comment.

## The questions investors are asking

Reuters says investors are weighing two big questions: whether Anthropic can keep its lead as rivals like Google catch up, and whether Claude can grow beyond coding into everyday office work. One investor told Reuters the company didn't give detailed answers to some questions at a roadshow meeting.

[CNBC laid out the scale](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/15/anthropic-ipo-is-make-or-break-for-the-technology-industry.html) on Sunday, citing Anthropic's latest prospectus:

- A **net loss of $104 billion** in the first nine months of 2026.
- More than **$900 billion in spending commitments**, including at least $110 billion with Amazon over a decade.
- AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC are collectively investing **$26 billion** at the IPO price.

## Why it matters

This is one of the largest tech IPOs in years, and it lands during a rough stretch for AI stocks, including the fallout from [Jensen Huang's exit at Nvidia](/blog/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo/). A strong debut would signal that public investors are willing to fund the expensive race to build frontier AI. A weak one would raise new questions for every company spending heavily on AI chips and data centers.

This article is news, not investment advice. The final price, share count and timing can still change until the deal is priced.
