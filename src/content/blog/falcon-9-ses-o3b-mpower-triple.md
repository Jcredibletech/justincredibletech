While Starbase is busy being *countdown drama*, Florida is busy being *Sunday afternoon launch*.

SpaceX is targeting **Sunday, September 13, 2026** for a **Falcon 9** flight of the **SES O3b mPOWER** mission from **Space Launch Complex 40** at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station. The **87-minute window opens at 2:49 p.m. ET** (about **1:49 p.m. Central**). Backup: **Monday, September 14**, same window. Webcast starts roughly **T-15** on SpaceX’s mission page and @SpaceX.

This isn’t another Starship Flight 14 remake. It’s the workhorse rocket doing what workhorses do — lofting real broadband birds for a paying customer — with a fun wrinkle: **three** mPOWER satellites at once.

## Why “three” is the headline

Earlier O3b mPOWER flights typically lofted **two** spacecraft. Today’s **mPOWER-F** mission is lined up to send **F11, F12, and F13** — Boeing-built birds that complete SES’s **initial 13-satellite** mPOWER constellation build.

Ten mPOWER sats are already working in **medium Earth orbit**. These last three finish the manufacturing/delivery chapter; SES/Boeing still talk **mid-2027** for them entering full commercial service after orbit-raising and commissioning. So: launch today ≠ “flip the switch tomorrow.” Launch today = “the factory finally shipped the last three.”

SES pitches O3b mPOWER as high-throughput connectivity for **enterprise, government, airlines, cruise ships** — the “global broadband that isn’t your home Starlink dish” lane. Different orbit class, different customers, same vibe: **more satellites = better internet in weird places**.

## The rocket trivia snack tray

Per SpaceX’s mission page:

- **Booster:** flying its **29th** mission (a very used, very trusted stick)  
- Prior résumé includes ISS crew/cargo flights, ESA’s Euclid, SES Astra 1P, and a pile of Starlinks  
- **Landing:** **A Shortfall of Gravitas** droneship in the Atlantic  
- **Deployments:** first sat ~**T+34 min**, then roughly **+7 minutes** between the second and third

If you only watch for the boom and the landing, you’ll miss half the plot. The staggered deployments are the quiet “yes, we really brought three” moment.
