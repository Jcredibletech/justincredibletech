A federal jury in Brooklyn, New York, convicted Chinese telecom giant **Huawei Technologies** and three of its subsidiaries on **Tuesday, November 24**, finding the company guilty on **10 of 12 counts**, including bank fraud, wire fraud, Iran sanctions violations, money laundering conspiracy and conspiracy to steal trade secrets. Jurors **acquitted** Huawei of the most serious charge, racketeering conspiracy, and of conspiring to obstruct justice.

## What happened

The case was filed in 2018 and made public in January 2019. After a trial of roughly three months, with prosecutors calling more than 60 witnesses, the jury deliberated about four days, according to [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/tech-and-telecom-law/huawei-found-guilty-of-us-criminal-charges-in-landmark-case-2) and [Reuters via CNA](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/world/huawei-convicted-us-criminal-trial-iran-business-trade-secrets-6579566).

The [Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-tech-giant-huawei-and-subsidiaries-convicted-sanctions-violations-fraud-conspiracy) says the evidence showed Huawei:

- Ran business in Iran through a company called **Skycom** while telling banks it was only a partner, which helped Huawei keep access to billions of U.S. dollars and dollar-clearing services.
- Supplied Iran with network services and surveillance cameras that prosecutors say helped authorities monitor protests.
- Took trade secrets from U.S. companies, including details of **T-Mobile's touchscreen-testing robot "Tappy"** (an employee carried a part out of the lab in a backpack) and **Cisco router source code**.

The convicted entities are Huawei, Huawei Device, and U.S. units Futurewei Technologies and Huawei Device USA.

## What each side says

Attorney General Todd Blanche said, "No person or company is above the law," according to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/huawei-fraud-trade-trial.html).

Huawei said it was disappointed and called the remaining charges "equally without legal or factual merit," adding that the five trade-secret disputes cited at trial had already been resolved as civil matters. Huawei lawyer Brian Heberlig called the racketeering acquittal a vindication of the company's "good-faith compliance culture," and the [AP](https://apnews.com/article/huawei-racketeering-trial-verdict-b39ac780e2e31140015a1be02f0329ab) reports Huawei's lawyer described it as a "profound rejection" of the government's narrative. Huawei says it will review the verdict and consider further legal action.

## Why it matters

This is a rare U.S. criminal trial of a major foreign corporation. Huawei has been on a U.S. trade blacklist since 2019, which already limits its access to American chips and software, so the verdict doesn't change what you can buy here. It does put a jury's findings on the record in the long U.S.-China fight over tech and trade, which also includes cases like [the alleged Megaspeed network that moved Nvidia chips to China](/blog/wsj-li-ming-nvidia-chips-china-network-megaspeed/).

## What's next

U.S. District Judge **Ann Donnelly** set sentencing for **September 14, 2027**. A company can't go to prison, so the possible penalties are fines, forfeiture and restitution. The amounts haven't been set. The case also ties back to Huawei CFO **Meng Wanzhou**, whose 2018 arrest in Canada started the dispute. She returned to China in 2021 under a deferred prosecution agreement.
