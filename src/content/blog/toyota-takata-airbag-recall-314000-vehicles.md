If you drive an older Toyota or Lexus, this one is worth a VIN check.

## What happened

Toyota is recalling **314,029 vehicles** in the U.S. because Takata front passenger air bag inflators may rupture, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration said on Thursday, according to [Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/toyota-to-recall-over-314000-us-vehicles-over-takata-air-bag-rupture-risk-nhtsa-says-5038101). Toyota first announced the voluntary recall on November 12 in its [newsroom](https://pressroom.toyota.com/toyota-recalls-front-passenger-airbags-in-certain-vehicles/).

The NHTSA campaign is **26V-748**. According to the [recall summary](https://www.usatoday.com/recalls/automotive/NHTSA-26V748000/), it covers certain:

- **2003–2008 Toyota Corolla**
- **2005–2006 Toyota Tundra**
- **2005–2007 Toyota Sequoia**
- **2016–2017 Toyota 4Runner**
- **2002–2010 Lexus SC430**, **2011 Lexus IS250** and **2017 Lexus GX460**

The recall includes inflators installed at the factory and some installed as replacement parts under an earlier recall.

## The risk

Toyota says the propellant inside these inflators can break down over time, especially after years of high humidity, heat and temperature swings. If the air bag then deploys, the inflator can rupture and send metal fragments into the cabin, which "may result in injury or death."

Toyota told NHTSA it has received **no reports of ruptures** involving these specific inflators and is acting "out of an abundance of caution" because they are nearing the end of their estimated safe service life, Reuters reported.

## What owners should do

Dealers will replace the front passenger inflator or the whole air bag assembly, depending on the model, **free of charge**. Enter your VIN or plate at [nhtsa.gov/recalls](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) or Toyota.com/recall. Toyota's customer line is 1-800-331-4331.

## Why it matters

Takata inflators are behind the largest auto recall in U.S. history, and many of these cars are now 10 to 20+ years old, often on their second or third owner, who may never have gotten a recall letter. Hot, humid states are where the risk is highest.
