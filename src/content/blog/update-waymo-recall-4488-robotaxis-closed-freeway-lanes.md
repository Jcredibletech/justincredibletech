*This is an update to our November 18 story, [Waymo Pauses Driverless Freeway Rides in Every U.S. Market After California Bans Them From State Freeways](/blog/waymo-pauses-freeway-rides-after-california-ban/).*

**Waymo** has filed a voluntary software recall covering **4,488 driverless vehicles** after its robotaxis drove into **closed freeway lanes 11 times** since October 1. The filing with the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (**NHTSA**) was submitted November 19 and made public on **Tuesday, November 24**. No collisions or injuries were reported in any of the incidents.

## What the filing says

According to the [NHTSA recall report (26E086)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2026/RCLRPT-26E086-1357.pdf), the defect is that a Waymo vehicle "may enter and continue driving in a closed freeway lane due to improperly recognizing and failing to avoid a closed lane." The timeline:

- **Oct. 1 to Nov. 6:** Eight Waymo vehicles entered freeway lanes closed by cones, highway patrol "traffic breaks" or construction vehicles.
- **Nov. 6:** Waymo narrowed where its cars could drive on freeways.
- **Nov. 9:** Three more vehicles entered lanes coned off for construction, three days after that change.
- **Nov. 10-11:** Waymo restricted freeway driving further, including pausing planned commercial freeway trips.
- **Nov. 12:** Waymo's Safety Board decided to recall the software.

The recall covers Waymo's **fifth- and sixth-generation** driving systems, every vehicle in its fleet capable of driving with no one behind the wheel. Waymo owns all of them, so there's nothing for riders to do. Its interim fix is the freeway restrictions; the final software remedy is still in development.

A second recall (26E087) covers vehicles that could move into an intersection after a police officer's hand signals became blocked or stopped. [Yahoo Autos](https://autos.yahoo.com/safety-and-recalls/articles/waymo-recalls-4-488-robotaxis-154924320.html) and [MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/waymo-recalls-vehicles-due-to-issues-stopping-entering-closed-highway-lanes-b089d8a6) report that fix was already applied by November 6.

## What's new since last week

When California's DMV banned Waymo from state freeways, the public knew about only two incidents. Reuters, as cited by [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/waymo-recalls-vehicles-after-11-robotaxis-entered-closed-freeway-lanes-2000844815), reports that **only two of the 11** had been publicly reported before these filings. The filing also shows the problem continued after Waymo's first fix.

Waymo told Gizmodo none of the incidents caused crashes or injuries, and that it drives about six million driverless miles a week. Waymo said Tuesday the recall was prompted by the 11 incidents, including three on a single day, according to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/waymo-recall-prompted-by-robotaxis-entering-closed-freeway-lanes.html).

## Why it matters

Freeway driving is where robotaxi companies are trying to grow next, and construction zones are among the hardest cases for any self-driving system. Waymo's city-street service continues, but freeway rides stay paused until regulators and the company are satisfied. Competitors are watching the same problem as they start testing, including [Uber, Nuro and Lucid in the Bay Area](/blog/uber-nuro-lucid-driverless-robotaxi-testing-bay-area/) and [Tesla's Cybercab in Dallas](/blog/update-tesla-cybercab-public-rides-dallas/).
