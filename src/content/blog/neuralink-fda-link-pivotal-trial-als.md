**Neuralink** says the U.S. Food and Drug Administration has approved its **LINK Pivotal Study**, a clinical trial of the company's brain-computer interface in adults with severe paralysis caused by **ALS** (amyotrophic lateral sclerosis). The company announced it on **Wednesday, Nov. 25**, calling it the first pivotal trial for an implantable brain-computer interface in the United States.

To be clear about the stage: this approval lets Neuralink **run a study**. The N1 implant is still an investigational device. It is **not approved** for sale or general medical use.

## What happened

According to [Neuralink's announcement](https://neuralink.com/updates/neuralink-receives-fda-approval-to-launch-link-pivotal-study/), LINK will test the safety and effectiveness of the system, focusing on whether people can control digital devices by thought in ways that improve independence in daily life. It will also keep tracking long-term safety and reliability across multiple clinical sites. Enrollment is open.

Neuralink President **DJ Seo** posted more details on X, as reported by [Neura Pod](https://www.neurapod.com/blog/neuralink-fda-approval-link-pivotal-trial) and [Joho no Todai](https://joho-todai.com/en/neuralink-brain-implant-fda-clinical-trial/):

- **20** participants.
- Primary endpoints measured at **90 days**.
- Neuralink plans to file for FDA **Premarket Approval (PMA)** after the 20th participant reaches that point, and Seo said FDA review takes months.

The trial builds on Neuralink's earlier **feasibility studies** in the U.S., Canada and the U.K., where the company says more than 30 people have received the implant. Hospital sites haven't been named, and as of Nov. 26 the study wasn't yet listed on ClinicalTrials.gov.

## Why it matters

Medical devices go through different stages than drugs. Feasibility studies are small and ask whether a device is safe and works at all. A **pivotal** study is the larger trial whose results the FDA uses to decide whether a device can be sold as a treatment, as [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/neuralink-fda-approval-link-pivotal-study-als) explains. ALS gradually takes away people's ability to move and speak, and there's no cure, so a device that restores computer control could matter a lot for communication and independence.

There are real risks. Neuralink itself lists surgical risks such as bleeding and infection, and notes the device could stop working. A pivotal trial is where those risks get measured in a bigger group. Results aren't guaranteed, and the FDA can still say no.

## What's next

Enrollment pace decides the timeline: the PMA filing waits until the last of 20 participants reaches 90 days, then the FDA review begins. Neuralink hasn't given a date for the first LINK implant.
