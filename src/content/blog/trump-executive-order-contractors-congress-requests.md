Six weeks before a new Congress is sworn in, the White House has set new rules for companies that do business with the federal government and get questions from lawmakers.

## What happened

On **Friday, November 20, 2026**, President Donald Trump signed **Executive Order 14438**, titled "[Protecting the Executive's Privileges and Enhancing Efficiencies in Federal Contracting](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/11/protecting-the-executives-privileges-and-enhancing-efficiencies-in-federal-contracting/)." [Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/politics-news/trump-order-says-contractors-must-notify-white-house-of-congressional-probes-5043101) and [The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/11/21/trump-white-house-orders-contractors-limit-information-shared-with-congress/) both reported on the order.

What the order requires, according to its text:

- All federal contractors must **notify the White House and their contracting agencies** of any pending or future **congressional request** about their communications or work with the executive branch.
- Contractors must **follow White House and agency instructions** on withholding communications the White House or agency has determined are protected by **executive privilege**.
- The rules also cover **subcontractors**, and agencies must tell all contractors about the new obligations. The Office of Management and Budget will write compliance guidance.
- Contractors that don't comply could face **termination or cancellation of contracts** and possible **suspension or debarment** from future federal work.

## The administration's case

The order says Congress cannot seek executive-branch information "without a valid legislative purpose" and cannot obtain privileged information "before the executive branch has an opportunity to assert relevant privileges." It states that "certain congressional leaders" have sought confidential information from agencies while "harassing private companies that contract with the Federal Government," and argues that uncoordinated responses waste taxpayer money on duplicate legal fights and could expose sensitive work, including on national security. Reuters reported the White House defended the order on those grounds.

## The other side

Reuters described the order as part of a broader effort to limit what information reaches Congress before **Democrats take control of both chambers on January 3**. Democrats have said they plan to investigate the administration, including federal contracts and possible conflicts of interest, Reuters reported. Rep. **Robert Garcia** of California, expected to lead the House Oversight Committee, sent document-preservation letters this week to a dozen Trump allies, family members and current and former officials.

The offices of Garcia, House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer and Sen. Elizabeth Warren did not respond to Reuters' requests for comment Friday night.

## What comes next

Reuters noted the order follows a directive this week from White House Counsel **Will Scharf** requiring agencies to get White House approval before employees agree to testify to Congress. Courts have stepped into past disputes between presidents and Congress over executive privilege, and Reuters reported the new restrictions could face legal challenges, particularly if contractors are told to withhold material sought by congressional **subpoenas**.
