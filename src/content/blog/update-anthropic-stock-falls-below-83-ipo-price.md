*Update to our story on [Anthropic's record Nasdaq debut](/blog/anthropic-nasdaq-debut-biggest-ipo/) on November 19.*

Five days ago, Anthropic opened at $111.50 a share in the biggest IPO on record. On Tuesday, the maker of Claude traded below the $83 price its IPO buyers paid for the first time.

## What happened

Anthropic (ticker: ANT) broke below $83 in premarket trading Tuesday and kept sliding after the open. [Bloomberg](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/capital-markets/anthropic-breaks-below-83-ipo-price-in-premarket-trading) reported it fell as low as **$76.03**, about 8% under the IPO price, during a broad pullback in stocks after Monday's rally. Reuters, [as reported by The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/markets/stock-markets/anthropic-shares-fall-below-83-ipo-price-days-after-record-breaking-nasdaq-debut/article71738365.ece), noted that Anthropic missed Monday's AI-stock rally after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, and that the S&P 500 and Dow ended last week in correction territory.

Shares closed Monday at $87.64, according to [Quartz](https://qz.com/anthropic-ceo-president-stock-sales-ipo-112426).

## The founders' share sales

A Form 4 filed with the SEC late Monday shows CEO **Dario Amodei** sold 3,378,378 shares to the IPO's underwriters at $83 each on November 20, about $280.4 million. Quartz and [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-shares-below-83-ipo-price-first-time-amodei-sales-analysts) report that President **Daniela Amodei** made an identical sale. These were planned sales: the IPO prospectus said all seven co-founders would each sell that same number of shares in the offering. They were not sales into the falling market.

After the sale, Dario Amodei still directly holds more than 202 million Class A shares, plus about 35.9 million in trusts, according to the [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/0002157947-26-000004.txt). Most co-founder shares stay locked up for up to 12 months.

## Why it matters

Anthropic's IPO was a test of how much investors will pay for a pure AI company. At Reuters' Tuesday price, the company was valued at about $1.5 trillion, roughly 40 times its sales over the past year, according to The Next Web. Analysts quoted by Reuters pointed to worries about AI spending, rising interest rates, and cheaper open-weight AI models as risks.

Some context: falling below an IPO price early is not unusual. Reuters noted that SpaceX took about a month to dip under its offer price after its June listing, and Facebook did it on its second trading day in 2012. MSCI plans to add Anthropic to its global indexes at the November 30 close, which could bring buying from index funds.
