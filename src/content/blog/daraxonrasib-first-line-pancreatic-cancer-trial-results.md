A pill called daraxonrasib is getting a lot of attention this weekend, with Cancer Research UK calling the latest results "the biggest leap forward we've seen in decades for pancreatic cancer patients." Here is what the new data actually show, and what they don't.

## What happened

On **November 20, 2026**, Dr. Wungki Park of Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center presented updated results at the EORTC-NCI-AACR Symposium in Barcelona. The study tested daraxonrasib, sold as **RASONQUE** by Revolution Medicines, combined with standard chemotherapy (gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel) as a **first treatment** for people whose pancreatic cancer had already spread.

According to [Revolution Medicines](https://ir.revmed.com/news-releases/news-release-details/revolution-medicines-presents-updated-phase-12-data-rasonquer/) and [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2026/nov/20/pill-pancreatic-cancer-breakthrough-daraxonrasib), among **61 patients** followed for a median of about 18 months:

- Tumors shrank in **60.7%** of patients, and in **8.2%** the cancer could no longer be seen on scans (a complete response).
- The disease was kept from growing in **90.2%**.
- Median progression-free survival was **12.8 months**.
- **76.6%** were alive one year after starting treatment. The Guardian notes 40-45% typically survive a year on chemotherapy alone.

## The important caveats

This was an **open-label phase 1/2 study with no comparison group**, as [OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/daraxonrasib-plus-gemcitabine-nab-paclitaxel-generates-durable-responses-in-first-line-metastatic-pdac) and Medical Daily pointed out. That means the numbers can't yet prove the combination beats chemo alone. Doctors quoted by The Guardian said it **cannot be considered a cure**, and it isn't known how long the benefit lasts.

The company itself says using daraxonrasib with chemo as a first treatment "remains investigational with safety and efficacy not yet established." The answer will come from **RASolute 303**, an ongoing phase 3 trial comparing daraxonrasib alone and with chemo against chemo alone.

## Why it matters

Pancreatic cancer is one of the deadliest cancers; The Guardian reports only about 3% of patients survive five years once it spreads. Daraxonrasib works by switching off mutated **RAS** proteins (including KRAS), which drive most pancreatic tumors and were long considered "undruggable."

The drug is already approved for a different situation. On **August 26, 2026**, the [FDA approved](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-daraxonrasib-metastatic-pancreatic-adenocarcinoma) it for adults with metastatic pancreatic cancer who have had at least one prior treatment, after a 500-patient randomized trial showed median survival of 13.2 months versus 6.7 months on chemotherapy. The FDA lists side effects including skin problems, mouth sores, diarrhea and lung inflammation.
