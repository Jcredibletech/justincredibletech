If your banking app showed an error or a blank balance on Monday, you had plenty of company.

## What happened

Starting Monday morning, November 16, 2026, and surging in the afternoon, thousands of customers reported trouble with online and mobile banking. By 2 p.m. ET, nearly **4,800** people had reported problems with **Chase** on Downdetector, the most of any bank. Users also reported issues at **Bank of America, U.S. Bank and Wells Fargo**, including failed logins, missing balances and delayed transfers, and some **Zelle** payments would not go through ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/banks-online-service-outages-jpmorgan-chase-bank-of-america-wells-fargo/); [AP](https://apnews.com/article/bank-outage-chase-wells-fargo-truist-50fee2693c22f597fa6ca51a97ef2cda)).

## What Chase says caused it

JPMorgan Chase said it had "a temporary issue related to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems" and that it was **not a cyberattack**. The bank said it was "not a systemic issue" and that other banks were slowed only when they were party to a transaction with Chase. It also said customers still had access to their accounts and funds, though some transactions took longer than usual ([Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/11/16/jpmorgan-chase-banking-outage-software-bug-01168592); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/banks-online-service-outages-jpmorgan-chase-bank-of-america-wells-fargo/)). Truist, PNC and Capital One told the BBC they were not affected, and Zelle said its own service was operating as it should.

By about 6 p.m. ET, outage reports for Chase, Bank of America and U.S. Bank were falling ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cj752wvqneq6o)). Chase has not yet given a detailed root cause.

## Why it matters

Chase is the largest U.S. bank, and Monday showed how one bank's internal bug can spill into payments at other banks through shared rails like Zelle. It also lands right after last week's [Microsoft 365 outage](https://justincredibletech.com/blog/microsoft-outage-hotel-pool-chlorine-alarm-email/) and the FAA ground stop, a rough stretch for the systems people count on every day.

A practical takeaway: keep a little cash or a second account or card for days like this, and if a transfer looks stuck, check before sending it again so it doesn't go through twice.
