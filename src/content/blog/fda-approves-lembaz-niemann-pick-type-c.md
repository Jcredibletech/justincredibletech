For families facing one of the rarest and most devastating childhood diseases, there is now, for the first time, an approved medicine shown to extend life.

## What happened

On **Tuesday, November 17, 2026**, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration [approved](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-drug-shown-help-patients-infantile-onset-niemann-pick-disease-type-c-live-longer) **Lembaz (adrabetadex)** injection to reduce the risk of death in people with **Niemann-Pick disease type C (NPC)** whose neurological symptoms began **before age 6**, the most severe form, called infantile-onset NPC. It's approved for adults and children who weigh at least 7 kg (about 15 pounds).

The drug's maker, [Beren Therapeutics](https://www.berentx.com/news/beren-therapeutics-announces-us-fda-approval-of-lembaz/) of Thousand Oaks, California, announced the approval the same day. The FDA granted it to Beren's subsidiary, Mandos LLC.

## What NPC is

NPC is an inherited disorder that affects an estimated **1 in 150,000 people**, according to the FDA. The body can't properly move cholesterol and other fats inside cells, so they build up, especially in the brain. That causes progressive loss of movement, coordination and the ability to swallow.

Two other therapies were already approved for NPC, but the FDA says neither has been shown to reduce the risk of death, and both have age and weight limits that leave out the youngest patients.

## What the evidence shows

The FDA looked at survival in **36 patients** treated with Lembaz across **three clinical studies and an expanded access program**, compared with a **matched external control group** of untreated patients rather than a placebo group in the same trial. The FDA says that comparison served as the key study supporting approval. Its findings:

- a **55% reduction in the risk of death**, and
- median survival of **8.5 years** with treatment versus **4.9 years** in the comparison group.

Lembaz doesn't cure NPC. Beren says it works by restoring how cells move cholesterol. A trained provider gives it **every two weeks by injection into the spinal canal**.

## Side effects

The label carries a **boxed warning for hearing loss, including permanent hearing loss**. The FDA says patients need a full hearing evaluation before starting and regularly during treatment. Other common side effects include balance and coordination problems, fatigue, vomiting, back pain, headache, fever and nausea.

## Why it matters

Rare-disease approvals often rely on small numbers of patients and external comparisons, because randomized trials with placebo groups can be impractical or ethically difficult for fatal childhood conditions. Questions about treatment belong with a patient's own doctors. Families can find disease information through groups such as the National Niemann-Pick Disease Foundation.
