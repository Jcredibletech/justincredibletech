Here's what happened: on Thursday afternoon, somebody walked out of Binance, the world's biggest crypto exchange, with roughly **$6.3 billion** in digital assets. Most of it moved in about **20 minutes**. If the numbers hold up, it's the largest crypto theft ever recorded, and possibly one of the largest thefts of any kind.

## What happened

According to blockchain analysis firm [TRM Labs](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/binance-links-63-billion-crypto-theft-to-compromised-devops-laptop/), about $6.36 billion left Binance wallets on **ten blockchains**, including Bitcoin, Ethereum, Tron and Solana, starting around 4 p.m. Eastern on October 29. [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/binance-exploit-latest-findings-and-response/) puts the total at about $6.4 billion. For scale, that's roughly four times the $1.5 billion Bybit hack of February 2025, which until now held the record.

The money came out of Binance's **hot wallets**, the internet-connected wallets exchanges use to process customer withdrawals. TRM says the attacker prepared for at least 36 hours, tested the code on a few small wallets, rehearsed it, and then ran it on a Binance hot wallet about four hours later, according to [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/the-dollar63-billion-binance-hack-was-pulled-off-in-just-20-minutes/).

## How did they get in?

Binance says its early investigation pointed to a **compromised laptop belonging to one of its engineers**, which let the attacker reach an internal system and send unauthorized withdrawals. It has since said it found "additional points of breach" and called it "a complex attack." BleepingComputer reports Binance hasn't found evidence that a wallet's private key leaked.

## Who's behind it?

TRM Labs and Chainalysis both say they're highly confident the attack was carried out by **TraderTraitor**, a North Korean state-linked hacking group the FBI blamed for the Bybit theft. Chainalysis says it watched stolen Binance funds mix with money from last month's Bitget hack, which had already been tied to North Korea. Elliptic also believes North Korea is responsible.

One important caveat: those are private analysts. **Binance and law enforcement haven't publicly named a culprit.** A person familiar with the situation told [Reuters](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/hackers-steal-63-billion-binance-record-heist-blamed-north-korea) that Binance is working with the FBI and the U.S. Secret Service.

## What happens to customers' money

- **About $3 billion has been frozen**, almost all of it stablecoins, after issuers like Tether and Circle stepped in, per [AP](https://apnews.com/article/binance-cryptocurrency-security-hack-north-korea-550e9b876550f71f07098494fe735beb) and Reuters.
- The other roughly $3.3 billion is mostly Bitcoin, Ether and XRP, which can't simply be frozen. Elliptic's Tom Robinson told Wired he doesn't expect the frozen share to rise much further.
- **Binance says it will cover all losses** and has set aside about $6.34 billion of company funds to do it.
- **Withdrawals are paused.** Binance said Friday it expects to bring them back within about 48 hours.

## Why it matters

The scary part isn't a clever bug in some blockchain. It's that one person's work computer may have opened the door to billions. Security teams talk about this constantly: the weakest link is usually a human and a device, not the math. (This is news, not financial advice.)
