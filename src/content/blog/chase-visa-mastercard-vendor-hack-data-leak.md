If you have a Chase account or carry a Visa or Mastercard, your inbox is the thing to watch this week, not your balance.

On Thursday, November 19, 2026, [JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Mastercard all said](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/jpmorgan-visa-and-mastercard-disclose-cyberattack-on-third-party-vendors-190314490.html) that attackers got at some customer data after cyberattacks on outside vendors. All three say their own systems were not breached and that no card numbers or payment data were taken.

## What each company said

- **JPMorgan Chase:** "Based on our investigation so far, we believe the attackers accessed certain marketing data held by those vendors, which may include some customer names and email addresses." The bank said it will contact affected customers directly and offer support, including free credit monitoring, according to [Bloomberg](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/banking-law/jpmorgan-visa-warn-of-cyberattack-that-hit-outside-vendor-2) and [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/money/business/2026/11/19/jpmorgan-chase-bank-cyberattack-customer-data-vendor/92868710007/).
- **Mastercard:** a third-party provider exposed "a limited amount of email addresses – non-payments information – from a small subset of cardholders enrolled in a specific benefit program," and there is "no impact to the Mastercard payment network."
- **Visa:** said the claims are tied to a third-party service provider in the Asia-Pacific region.

Chase also said the leak has nothing to do with [Monday's online banking outage](/blog/chase-software-bug-online-banking-outage/), which it blamed on a software bug.

## Who is behind it, and what leaked

A ransomware group called n0n has claimed responsibility and posted sample data on its leak site after a 48-hour ransom deadline passed, according to the cybersecurity firm SOCRadar, which told Yahoo Finance it had not independently verified the claim. The Wall Street Journal first reported the link to the group.

The surprising part is whose data it is. Researchers at [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/security/jpmorgan-chase-data-leak-non-ransomware-gang/) and [BankInfoSecurity](https://www.bankinfosecurity.com/jpmorgans-elite-cardholders-exposed-in-vendor-hack-a-33456) say the Chase files cover roughly 16,000 customer email addresses, some phone numbers and postal codes, plus more than 100 bank employee emails, all tied to the invite-only J.P. Morgan Reserve card, which those outlets report is typically for clients with around $10 million or more in assets. The [AP](https://apnews.com/article/chase-data-leak-cyberattack-8470d475450721b66858bdace47091c0) described it as thousands of customers' emails and some phone numbers. The Visa data reportedly comes from premium dining and loyalty programs in Asia-Pacific.

## What it means for regular cardholders

For most people, the practical risk is phishing. "The most immediate risk for consumers is targeted phishing that impersonates their bank or card provider," SOCRadar's Ensar Seker told Yahoo Finance. Fake "your account is locked" emails and texts tend to spike after a story like this. The simple habit: don't tap links in surprise bank messages; open your bank's app or type the website yourself.

The companies have not said exactly when the vendor attacks happened or named the vendors. We'll update this story if they do.
