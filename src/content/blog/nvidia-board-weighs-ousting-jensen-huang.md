If you have a 401(k) or an S&P 500 index fund, you almost certainly own a slice of Nvidia. So tonight's news matters even if you've never bought a share yourself: the company's own board may be about to fire the man who founded it 33 years ago. "Jensen," "Nvidia" and "$NVDA" were the top trends on X in the US this evening, and the story led Techmeme and Hacker News.

## What happened

- **The board is reportedly moving.** The Wall Street Journal reported Wednesday that Nvidia's board is preparing to oust CEO Jensen Huang and could meet as soon as that day to decide his future, citing people familiar with the matter ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1031652/jensen-huang-rumor), [MarketWatch](https://www.morningstar.com/news/marketwatch/20261111357/nvidias-stock-drops-as-the-board-reportedly-prepares-to-remove-ceo-jensen-huang)). Reuters said a source confirmed the board was meeting Wednesday ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/instant-view-nvidia-is-preparing-to-oust-ceo-jensen-huang-wsj-reports-5020061)).
- **A second alleged affair.** Earlier Wednesday, The New York Times reported that Huang had a relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of health care, who reports to him, citing people who worked at the company. Both are married to other people. Nvidia did not immediately comment.
- **On top of the first one.** On Sunday, November 8, Huang told employees in a letter that he had been unfaithful to his wife. That affair was with Alice Huang, an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer, whom U.S. intelligence officials suspect of spying for China. The board had already opened an independent investigation and retained former U.S. attorney Joon H. Kim and the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz. Nvidia has said it has seen no basis so far to believe national security or company secrets were compromised.
- **The search may already be on.** Bloomberg reports the board has been working with an outside firm to identify possible replacements and is weighing a temporary CEO during a search.
- **The market reacted.** Nvidia shares fell more than 7% in after-hours trading Wednesday, reversing a gain during the regular session, while rivals AMD and Broadcom rose about 4% and 3% after hours ([MarketWatch](https://www.morningstar.com/news/marketwatch/20261111357/nvidias-stock-drops-as-the-board-reportedly-prepares-to-remove-ceo-jensen-huang)). Nvidia is due to report earnings on November 17.

## Why it matters to you

Nvidia makes the chips behind almost every big AI product you use, from chatbots to the self-driving software in newer cars, and it is one of the largest holdings in the index funds most retirement accounts own. A messy leadership change at the top could ripple into your 401(k) and into how fast AI hardware keeps getting better and cheaper. Analysts quoted by Reuters pointed out that Nvidia's current products and roadmap were set long before this, so the near-term business shouldn't vanish overnight, but Huang famously runs the company with about 60 direct reports and no obvious second-in-command.

## My take

Nobody's above the rules, and a CEO in a relationship with someone who reports to him is a real governance problem at any company, let alone the most important chipmaker in America. The national security questions around the first affair make it worse. At the same time, the board should finish its investigation and have a solid plan before it acts, because a panicked handoff would hurt the workers and everyday shareholders who did nothing wrong. Remember too that these are reports from unnamed sources, and Nvidia hasn't announced anything yet. That's my opinion; the facts above come from the outlets linked and cited.

*Update, November 12, 2026: Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO, effective immediately, and CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. [Read the follow-up](/blog/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo/).*
