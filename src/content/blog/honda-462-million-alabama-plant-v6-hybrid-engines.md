If you've been eyeing a hybrid Honda Pilot, Passport or Ridgeline someday, this is the kind of factory news that comes before it. Honda is putting nearly half a billion dollars into the Alabama plant that builds all four of its big light trucks.

## What happened

On **Tuesday, November 17, 2026**, Honda [announced](https://hondanews.com/en-US/releases/honda-invests-462-million-in-alabama-auto-plant-as-facility-marks-25-years-of-production) a **$462 million** investment in its **Alabama Auto Plant (AAP)** in Lincoln, timed to the plant's 25th anniversary. The first Odyssey minivan rolled off the line there on November 14, 2001.

According to Honda and [AL.com](https://www.al.com/business/2026/11/honda-offers-more-details-on-462-million-expansion-of-alabama-auto-plant.html), the money will:

- add a new **V6 hybrid engine assembly line**,
- let the plant build **multiple models on the same line**, so it can shift production toward whatever is selling,
- increase output of "high-demand models," and
- create **200 new jobs**.

AL.com reported that the state and Talladega County approved more than **$61 million in incentives** tied to the project.

## Why this plant matters

Lincoln is Honda's largest plant in North America by employment and production, with capacity for **340,000 vehicles and 340,000 engines a year**, and the only Honda site in the U.S. that builds engines and vehicles in the same place. It's the exclusive global home of the **Odyssey, Pilot, Passport and Ridgeline**. Honda says nearly 15% of all Honda and Acura vehicles built in North America since 2001 came from there.

A Honda-commissioned study by the Economic Development Partnership of Alabama and the University of Alabama estimated the plant generated **$8.5 billion** in economic output in 2024, or about $11.7 billion including suppliers.

## The bigger picture

Honda hasn't said which models will get the new V6 hybrid. But the move fits a wider shift: several automakers, Honda included, have announced bigger hybrid plans this year as hybrids reached a record share of U.S. sales, and AL.com noted Honda also recently announced a new Ohio plant as part of a goal to build more than **1.3 million vehicles a year in the U.S.**

## Why it matters to you

More U.S.-built hybrid engines could mean more hybrid options on family SUVs and pickups down the road. For Alabama, it's 200 more jobs at a plant whose average pay AL.com put at about $85,000.
