If you do freelance work, write code, or build spreadsheets and slide decks for a living, today's AI launch is about your line of work. Anthropic released **Claude Fable 5.2** and **Claude Mythos 5.2** on Monday, and within hours "Fable 5.2" was trending on X, with roughly 9,500 posts in its first full hour, and sitting at the top of Hacker News.

## What happened

- **The freelance test.** On the Remote Labor Index, a benchmark built from real freelance projects across many fields, Anthropic says Fable 5.2 completed 41.3% (at high effort). Fable 5.1 scored 17.9%, Anthropic's own Opus 5.5 scored 21.3%, and OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra scored 20.8% ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2)).
- **The math stunt.** Anthropic says a team of about 1,000 Fable 5.2 agents fully formalized the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours. "Formalized" means translating the proof into a language a computer can check step by step; Andrew Wiles' original proof ran over a hundred pages ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/claude-fable-5-2-and-mythos-5-2-cement-anthropics-ai-lead-just-before-its-ipo/)).
- **Cheaper and faster.** API pricing fell to $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, down from $10 and $50, about 20% less. Anthropic says outputs come up to 30% faster than 5.1, with an optional fast mode up to 2.5 times quicker ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropic-releases-claude-fable-5-2-and-mythos-5-2-pairing-new-record-high-benchmarks-with-20-api-cost-reduction), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2)).
- **Who gets it.** Fable 5.2 is available to all users, with cybersecurity and biology safeguards. Mythos 5.2 has the same specs and pricing but is limited to organizations verified through Anthropic's programs, like its Cyber Verification Program ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/mythos-5-2/overview)).

## Why it matters to you

A benchmark is not a pink slip. The Remote Labor Index scores are Anthropic's own reported results, and "41% of projects" still means the model fell short on most of them. But the jump from about 18% to 41% in one version is the kind of curve that changes how companies price freelance work, how fast small businesses can ship, and what entry-level jobs look like. The price cut matters too: cheaper models mean these tools end up inside the apps you already use, not just in labs.

## My take

This is what American tech looks like when it's allowed to compete. On the same day, Meta launched a cheaper small model and Google unveiled new AI chips, and that kind of race is how customers win. The answer to job worries isn't Washington slamming on the brakes with heavy-handed AI rules. It's letting this competition run, keeping the U.S. ahead of China, and making sure workers can pick up these tools and use them themselves. That's my opinion; the numbers above are Anthropic's.
