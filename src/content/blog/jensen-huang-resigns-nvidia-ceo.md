**Update, Nov. 13, 2026:** Nvidia closed Thursday down 4.3% and was slightly higher in Friday premarket trading. Earnings are still scheduled for November 17. [Read the Friday morning update](https://justincredibletech.com/blog/nvidia-stock-edges-up-after-huang-exit/).

Last night I wrote that Nvidia's board was reportedly getting ready to push Jensen Huang out. This morning it happened. The man who co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and turned it into the chipmaker behind nearly the whole AI boom is gone, and if you own an S&P 500 index fund, you felt it: Nvidia is one of the biggest companies in that index. "Nvidia," "Colette Kress," "Tim Cook" and "$NVDA" are all trending on X right now, and the resignation is the top story on Techmeme and Hacker News.

## What happened

- **He's out, effective immediately.** Nvidia's board announced Thursday that Huang resigned as president, chief executive officer and a member of the board, effective immediately ([Nvidia Newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/)). The release didn't include a quote from Huang.
- **Who's in charge now.** CFO Colette Kress, a 13-year Nvidia veteran who previously worked at Microsoft and Cisco, is interim CEO while the board looks for a permanent pick, and former Synopsys CEO Harvey C. Jones becomes Nvidia's first-ever board chair ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/nvidia-jensen-huang-colette-kress-39ea208c040539166e10d8c6f473bc57)).
- **Why.** It follows a week of reports about two extramarital affairs: one with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive U.S. intelligence suspects of spying for China (Beijing denies it), and, per The New York Times, one with an Nvidia executive who reported to him directly ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/nvidia-jensen-huang-resigns-ceo-affairs-314714d463cf578cbb0dbd49de903107)). Huang admitted the first affair in a letter to employees on Sunday.
- **The market hit.** Nvidia dropped about 14% early Thursday, then trimmed that to roughly a 5% loss by the afternoon, while the broader S&P 500 recovered most of its early dip ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-017d4af2ad194ec2981d661663da9f9b), [AP markets](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-oil-ai-earnings-nvidia-8bf08407eafe1655ed73112f7494029a)).
- **Trump weighs in.** President Trump posted on Truth Social that Huang "has done a fantastic job" and that "there is only one man to take his place - TIM COOK of Apple. A Legend replaces a Legend!" Cook stepped down as Apple's CEO on September 1 and is now its executive chairman ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/11/12/trump-apple-tim-cook-jensen-huang-nvidia-resignation/92741233007/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/trump-floats-apple-chairman-tim-cook-as-jensen-huangs-successor/)).
- **Not over yet.** Huang has agreed to appear before lawmakers, according to a House Republican cited by The Hill, and Nvidia reports earnings on November 17.

## Why it matters to you

Nvidia's chips run the chatbots, image generators and driver-assist software you already use, and its stock sits inside most retirement accounts. A founder exit this sudden adds uncertainty to both: how fast AI hardware keeps improving and getting cheaper, and how your 401(k) behaves over the next few weeks. The products and roadmap were set long before this week, so the chips you're waiting on aren't vanishing, but the company now has to prove it can run without the guy who ran everything.

## My take

The board made the right call. A CEO in a relationship with a direct report breaks the rules any company would fire a regular employee over, and the national security questions around the first affair made staying impossible. Kress is a steady, Wall Street-trusted pick for the interim job. As for Tim Cook: he's a proven operator who already succeeded a legendary founder once, so I get the appeal. But in a free market, Nvidia's board and shareholders should pick the next CEO, not Washington, and the bigger lesson for every tech company is to have a real succession plan before they need one.
