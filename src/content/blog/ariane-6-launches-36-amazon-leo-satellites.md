Amazon's satellite internet project needed a win after a rough month. On Tuesday morning it got one, thanks to Europe's newest rocket.

## What happened

An **Ariane 6** rocket lifted off from Europe's Spaceport in Kourou, French Guiana, at 8:14 a.m. local time (5:14 a.m. Central Time) on November 24, carrying **36 Amazon Leo satellites**. [Arianespace](https://newsroom.arianespace.com/arianespace-successfully-launches-another-36-amazon-leo-satellites-on-ariane-6s-10th-flight/) said all 36 were released into low Earth orbit at about 460 km (roughly 285 miles), 2 hours and 4 minutes after liftoff. [Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/11/23/live-coverage-arianespaces-launches-amazon-leo-sats-amid-rocket-crunch/) confirmed the full deployment.

Quick facts on the mission, called VA271 by Arianespace and LE-04 by Amazon:

- It was Ariane 6's **10th flight, and its 10th success**.
- It flew in the most powerful **Ariane 64** setup, with four strap-on boosters. This was the second flight using the newer **P160C boosters**, which Arianespace says carry 156 tonnes of solid fuel each and raise low-orbit payload capacity to about 22 tonnes.
- Arianespace has now launched **136 Amazon Leo satellites** in under 10 months, on four missions this year. Amazon has booked 24 Ariane 6 launches in total.

## Why it matters

Nine days earlier, on November 15, a United Launch Alliance Vulcan rocket [failed to reach orbit](/blog/ula-vulcan-failure-amazon-leo-booster-underperformed/) and destroyed 40 Amazon Leo satellites. ULA has said early data points to an [inadequate fuel supply](/blog/update-ula-vulcan-failure-fuel-supply-be-4/) to the booster's engines, and Vulcan is grounded while the investigation continues.

[SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/ariane-6-launches-fourth-set-of-amazon-leo-satellites/) reported that these are the first Amazon Leo satellites to reach orbit in about four and a half months. Amazon is racing to build out a network that competes with SpaceX's Starlink, and losing one of its main rockets makes every working ride more valuable. Spaceflight Now described the situation as a "rocket crunch."

For Europe, the launch is a milestone too. "Seeing Ariane 6 fly again in its most powerful four-booster configuration is a powerful demonstration of the capabilities Europe now has at its disposal," said Géraldine Naja, ESA's Director of Space Transportation, in an [ESA statement](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/space-transportation/ariane/ariane-6-launches-another-set-of-amazon-leo-satellites-to-orbit).

## What's next

Amazon still has to get many more satellites up before it can offer broad service, and it depends on several launch providers to do it. How quickly Vulcan returns to flight will shape how fast that happens.
