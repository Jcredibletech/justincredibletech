Paying a monthly fee to turn on heated seats your car already has is about to be illegal in New York. But the new law has a long list of exceptions, so it won't end every in-car subscription.

## What happened

On Monday, Gov. Kathy Hochul [signed S.9348A/A.10411A](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/money-your-pockets-governor-hochul-signs-bill-restricting-exploitative-vehicle-subscription), which became Chapter 414 of 2026, according to the [state Senate's bill page](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A10411). The bill passed the legislature in June and takes effect **February 27, 2027**.

The law says a carmaker, dealer, or their agent can't sell a subscription for a vehicle feature when both of these are true:

1. The feature uses parts and hardware **already installed** when you buy or lease the car, and
2. Once switched on, it would work **without any ongoing cost** or support from the company.

The law names heated seats as an example. Regular car loan and lease payments don't count as subscriptions. Companies that break the rule face a civil penalty of up to **$250 per point of sale** for each violation.

"People spending their hard-earned money on a car shouldn't have to fork over even more money for features that should automatically come with the car," Hochul said in her announcement.

## The exceptions

This is where it gets narrower. According to the bill text, the ban does **not** apply to:

- Navigation system updates, infotainment features and satellite radio
- In-car Wi-Fi, telematics and roadside assistance
- Software-dependent driver assistance and driving automation features
- Connected services that need cellular or data networks to keep working

Automakers can still push software updates, but they can't charge for an update that fixes a safety defect when federal recall law requires the fix to be free, according to [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/blog/new-york-limits-what-automakers-can-sell-as-subscriptions).

## Why it matters

Several automakers have experimented with selling built-in features through apps after purchase, so a law in a state as large as New York gets noticed across the industry. There is one gray area to watch: the governor's announcement lists hands-free driving among the features the law guards against, but the bill text exempts software-dependent driver assistance and automation features. How that line gets drawn will likely be settled in enforcement.

For buyers, the takeaway is simple: starting in late February, a New York dealer or automaker shouldn't be charging a monthly fee to unlock hardware like seat heaters that's already in your car.
