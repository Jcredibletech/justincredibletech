The database cops check during a traffic stop may have just had uninvited visitors.

The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of its National Crime Information Center, known as NCIC, two people briefed on the matter told [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/justice-department/fbi-investigating-potential-breach-crime-database-rcna610536) on Friday night, November 20, 2026. CNN reported it first, and the [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/fbi-database-ncic-hack-d066cff87a64a564db0a00172c124972) confirmed the investigation with its own source on Saturday. The FBI had no comment as of Friday night.

## What NCIC is

NCIC is the computerized system that connects federal, state and local law enforcement. According to AP, it holds records on fugitives, missing persons, criminal history, stolen property and more. Officers use it to check for outstanding warrants and run background information, the [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cvze7de86pk3o) notes. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/21/trump-fbi-database-hack.html), citing MS NOW's sources, reports that it also includes fingerprint records and files on wanted persons and violent felons. The system is restricted to law enforcement but is not classified.

What we don't know yet:

- Who was behind the apparent breach.
- What data, if any, was copied or changed.
- How long the intruders had access.

One expert quoted by CNN said the bigger concern isn't only whether hackers saw sensitive information, but whether they could compromise the accuracy of records that officers rely on in the field.

## Second FBI incident in recent months

This would be the second recent intrusion involving the FBI. In September, the bureau said it was investigating a breach claimed by the cybercrime group ShinyHunters, which said it had stolen information on bureau staff through the FBI's jobs portal. The FBI later arrested an accused co-conspirator in that case, AP reported.

## USDA was hit too

Separately, the U.S. Department of Agriculture told employees on Thursday that hackers had accessed a system it uses to pay farmers, [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/national-security/agriculture-department-breached-hackers-sources-say-rcna610497) reported. [Nextgov/FCW](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/usda-grapples-security-breach-it-staff-take-multiple-systems-offline/417350/) reported that USDA reset database passwords and took multiple applications offline over several days. It's not clear who was responsible or whether any data was taken. USDA has not publicly commented.

## The White House reaction

Asked about the FBI report in a Fox News interview on Saturday, President Donald Trump said he had "just heard" about it. "I will say that it's not good. I don't like that," he said, according to the BBC, adding, "But maybe we'll learn things that we would've never learned otherwise." He went on to say the breach might surface information about past investigations he has long called hoaxes.

The news lands in a rough week for federal tech. Banking, air traffic control and other systems saw outages last week that officials have said were not cyberattacks, and the White House's National Cyber Director, Sean Cairncross, was fired this week, the BBC reported.

## What it means for you

There's nothing for the public to do right now. NCIC is used by police agencies, not individuals, and no agency has said personal data was exposed. We'll update this story if the FBI confirms what was accessed or who was responsible.
