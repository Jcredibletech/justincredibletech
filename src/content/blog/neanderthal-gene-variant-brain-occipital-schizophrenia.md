If your ancestors came from outside Africa, a small slice of your DNA, roughly 1% to 4%, likely came from Neanderthals. A new study finds that one tiny piece of that inheritance seems to change the shape of the human brain.

## What happened

In a study published **November 16, 2026**, in the journal *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* (PNAS), evolutionary geneticists **Hugo Zeberg** (Karolinska Institutet) and **Svante Pääbo** (Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology, 2022 Nobel laureate) identified a genetic variant that modern humans inherited from Neanderthals. They compared ancient DNA with large modern data sets, including the **1000 Genomes Project** and the **UK Biobank**, as [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03762-0) reports.

## The findings

- The variant is a **deletion of four DNA letters on chromosome 14**, near a gene called **DAAM1** that's involved in early brain development.
- Each copy is associated with about **0.85 cubic centimeters less volume** in the **cuneus**, part of the occipital lobe at the back of the brain that handles vision. Nature reports that's a bigger effect on the cuneus than any other known variant.
- People with **two copies** had a **nearly 10% lower risk of schizophrenia**, according to [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/neanderthal-dna-linked-to-lower-risk-of-schizophrenia-in-modern-humans/) and [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/17/neanderthal-gene-variant-linked-to-lower-schizophrenia-risk-study-finds).
- The variant was **not linked** to intelligence or to other psychiatric conditions such as depression, bipolar disorder or ADHD.

## How they know it came from Neanderthals

The variant shows up in **all five** detailed Neanderthal genomes available to scientists, but **not** in Denisovans, another extinct human group. In living people it appears only in those with **non-African ancestry**. It also sits on a chunk of DNA about 59.7 kilobases long, which Nature says is too large to have survived intact from a much older shared ancestor.

## A surprising twist

Fossil skulls suggest Neanderthals had *larger* occipital lobes than we do, so you might expect their DNA to make that region bigger. It does the opposite, and scientists don't yet know why. "These findings caution against equating the size of a brain region with cognitive abilities," Zeberg and Pääbo wrote, as quoted by Euronews.

"Its effect is really one of the most beautiful and strong that I've ever seen," computational biologist Davide Marnetto of the University of Turin, who wasn't involved, told Nature.

## What this does and doesn't mean

This is an **association** found in large genetic databases, not a test or a treatment. It doesn't mean anyone can check their Neanderthal DNA to predict schizophrenia, and the authors haven't shown exactly how the variant works with DAAM1. The pair previously found that another Neanderthal gene cluster raises the risk of severe COVID-19, and Zeberg told Scientific American more discoveries are coming: "You might hear from us again."
