*This is an update to our November 21 story, [Update: Tesla's Cybercab Starts Public Rides in Dallas, Its Second City After Austin](/blog/update-tesla-cybercab-public-rides-dallas/).*

About a week after it started offering Cybercab rides in Dallas, **Tesla** has nearly doubled the size of its **Dallas Robotaxi** service area. Independent measurements of Tesla's new map put the zone at roughly **150 square miles**, up from about **82**, an increase of around **83%**.

## What happened

On **Wednesday, Nov. 25**, Tesla's official Robotaxi account on X posted a map of the bigger zone with the message: "Just grew wider in Dallas. More cars, less $, Happy Thanksgiving!"

Tesla didn't publish the square mileage itself. Measurements based on the new boundaries, reported by [Drive Tesla Canada](https://driveteslacanada.ca/news/tesla-robotaxi-dallas-expansion/) and [Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/5081/tesla-nearly-doubles-dallas-robotaxi-service-area), put the new area at about **150.4 square miles** (389.5 km²), compared with 82.0 square miles before.

Most of the new coverage is to the **north**. The zone now crosses **I-635** and reaches **Carrollton, Farmers Branch, Addison** and toward **Richardson**, while still covering **downtown Dallas, Highland Park** and **Love Field** airport.

Key numbers:

- **~150** square miles in Dallas now, up from ~82
- **~83%** larger
- Dallas is now slightly bigger than Tesla's **143-square-mile** Fort Worth zone, per Not a Tesla App
- Tesla gave **no** new fare or fleet numbers

Tesla said "more cars" and "less $," but didn't say how many vehicles it added, what the new prices are, or how long lower pricing will last, according to The Robotaxi Report, which cited TeslaNorth.

## Why it matters

A bigger map is what turns a robotaxi from a novelty into something people can actually use. The new area pulls in northern suburbs where many Dallas commuters live and work. Only Irving, Grand Prairie, Arlington and the area around DFW Airport now separate the Dallas and Fort Worth zones, and it's an open question whether Tesla will connect them.

The timing also lines up with Tesla's bigger plans. Musk has said customers could start adding their own cars to the Robotaxi network as early as next month, and first personal Cybercab deliveries are expected soon. More territory gives those cars more places to work. Texas records already show [more than 1,000 Cybercabs registered](/blog/tesla-cybercab-texas-registrations-top-1000/), and [FSD v15 is rolling out](/blog/tesla-fsd-v15-rollout-reality-view/). Keep in mind that Musk's product timelines have often slipped in the past.

## What's next

Watch for actual fare changes in the app, whether the Dallas and Fort Worth zones merge, and whether owner-supplied cars join the network on Musk's timeline.
