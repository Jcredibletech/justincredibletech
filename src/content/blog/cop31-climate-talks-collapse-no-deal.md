For the first time since Copenhagen in 2009, a United Nations climate summit has ended without its main agreement adopted by all countries.

## What happened

COP31 in **Antalya, Turkey**, co-hosted by Turkey and Australia, was supposed to end Friday. It ran into **Sunday, November 22**, becoming the longest climate summit on record. Nearly 200 countries needed to agree by consensus on a final "cover decision," called the **Antalya Pact**.

The draft, according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/nov/22/cop31-climate-talks-set-to-end-in-deep-division-as-key-deal-rejected), [Politico Europe](https://www.politico.eu/article/climate-deal-collapses-amid-fossil-fuel-rows/) and [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/22/fossil-fuel-impasse-pushes-cop31-climate-talks-into-second-day-of-overtime):

- Acknowledged for the first time that warming is on course to **exceed the 1.5°C** Paris target, and called for limiting the "overshoot."
- Called for **35% of global energy use to be electrified by 2035**, up from about 23% today.
- Promoted **carbon capture and storage** and carbon-removal technology.
- Asked rich countries to help poorer ones adapt.
- Did **not mention fossil fuels** or the "transition away" from them that countries agreed to in 2023.

COP31 President **Murat Kurum**, Turkey's environment minister, told the closing session: "It is clear that there are parties in this hall who oppose this text. We recognise that there is no consensus on this document. Therefore, these decisions have not been adopted." Several smaller decisions, including on a "just transition" for workers, did pass.

## Who said what

**Objecting:** Ireland's Darragh O'Brien, speaking for the EU, said the text "fails to meet the moment" and the bloc would not accept a deal "that greenwashes fossil fuels." Panama, the Marshall Islands, Norway, the UK and small island states also objected.

**Not objecting:** Saudi Arabia defended including carbon capture. The Africa group, China and Pakistan did not block the deal despite reservations. Pakistan said, "Climate action is not a menu from which we can select only what suits us." Mohamed Adow of Power Shift Africa criticized the EU, saying its governments "demand fossil fuel roadmaps from others while expanding fossil fuels themselves."

Australia's Chris Bowen, the co-president, said the red lines were "unbridgeable." UN Secretary-General António Guterres said he was "deeply disappointed," adding, "There is no ambiguity in the science," per [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/11/1168855).

## Why it matters

The fight is over how fast the world moves off oil, gas and coal, which scientists identify as the main driver of warming, versus how much to rely on technologies like carbon capture that let fuel producers keep operating. With no cover decision, countries leave without new shared targets, and the question moves to next year's COP32.
