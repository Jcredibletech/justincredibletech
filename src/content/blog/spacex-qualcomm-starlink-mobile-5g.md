Two days ago, the news was that SpaceX might buy Boost Mobile. This morning, SpaceX's phone network got a lot more real: it picked **Qualcomm** to supply the gear inside its cell sites.

Qualcomm [announced Wednesday](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261104680757/en/SpaceX-Selects-Qualcomm-to-Accelerate-Starlink-Mobiles-Nationwide-5G-Network) that SpaceX selected its Dragonwing cellular infrastructure platforms for **Starlink Mobile's nationwide US terrestrial 5G network**. "Qualcomm" is trending on X this morning, and [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/04/spacex-strikes-deal-with-qualcomm-for-mobile-network-infrastructure.html), [Fierce Network](https://www.fierce-network.com/wireless/spacexs-nationwide-terrestrial-5g-plans-get-real-qualcomm-signs) and [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/science/1026287/spacex-qualcomm-starlink-mobile-5g-netwowrk) all have it.

## What the deal covers

- **Hardware and software:** Qualcomm's Dragonwing QRU100 (radio unit) and QDU100 (distributed unit) platforms, plus its "commercial-grade" Layer 1 software. SpaceX builds the higher layers of the network software itself, according to Fierce.
- **Scale:** "tens of thousands" of sites, all big macro cell sites rather than small cells, over a multi-year agreement.
- **Timing:** the US nationwide rollout starts in 2027.
- **Price:** not disclosed.

CNBC reports Qualcomm called it its largest 5G initiative to date. That's a notable claim from a company best known for the modem inside your phone, not the equipment on the tower.

## The nerdy part

Starlink Mobile will use **Massive MIMO** radios with 64 transmit and 64 receive paths (64T64R). More antenna paths let a cell site aim signals at more phones at once, which is how you squeeze more coverage and capacity out of the same spectrum.

Massive MIMO isn't new. What Qualcomm says is new is where it's running: the **2 GHz band**, which SpaceX bought from EchoStar. Qualcomm's Durga Malladi told Fierce that this kind of radio usually shows up in higher bands, around 3.5 GHz and up, and that bringing 64T64R "all the way down to 2 GHz" is a unique part of the design.

## Why a satellite company needs cell towers

Starlink already sells direct-to-cell service from orbit, but as CNBC points out, satellites alone can't provide all the voice, video and data coverage that phones get from Verizon, AT&T and T-Mobile. Qualcomm says the ground network will "complement Starlink's satellite capabilities." Translation: towers where people are, satellites where they aren't.

It also fits the bigger picture. CNBC notes SpaceX agreed last month to buy a nationwide 800 MHz spectrum portfolio, and [we covered Monday's Washington Post report](/blog/spacex-boost-mobile-talks/) that SpaceX is in talks to buy Boost Mobile. That deal still hasn't been announced.

## What we still don't know

SpaceX hasn't named any tower partners or said how many new towers it might need, CNBC reports. Most carriers rent space from tower companies like American Tower and Crown Castle. And Starlink Mobile service from these sites won't start rolling out until 2027.

So here's my question: if Starlink Mobile shows up in your area in a couple of years, would you switch carriers, or is your current plan staying put?
