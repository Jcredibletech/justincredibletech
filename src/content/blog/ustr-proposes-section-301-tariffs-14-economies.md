The Trump administration on **Wednesday, November 25**, proposed new tariffs on **14 economies**, including **China, the European Union, Japan and India**. U.S. Trade Representative **Jamieson Greer** found that their policies on "structural excess capacity" in manufacturing are unreasonable and burden U.S. commerce under **Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974**. These are **proposals**: the public can comment until **January 5, 2027**, and hearings begin **January 25**.

## What was proposed

According to the [USTR announcement](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural), the 14 economies are China, the EU, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, India, Vietnam, Singapore, Switzerland, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Cambodia and Bangladesh. USTR made no determination for **Mexico or Norway**, which were also investigated.

Proposed maximum rates, with exemptions for some goods:

- **15%:** EU, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Switzerland
- **18%:** Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Cambodia, Bangladesh
- **20%:** China, Singapore
- **25%:** India
- **35%:** Vietnam

[Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/3fec1b0a85a0/world/china/us-proposes-new-tariffs-14-trading-partners-fight-excess-factory-capacity-2026-11-25/) reports that headline rates range from 20% to 40%, but duties would be capped at the lower levels in trade deals already reached or under negotiation. China's capped 20% would come on top of roughly 25% in tariffs from Trump's first term and would rise to 40% if a U.S.-China trade truce that expires January 10 falls apart, Reuters reports. Separately, Reuters says USTR would cap India's rate at 17% if a trade deal is reached.

## What each side says

Greer said many trading partners have adopted "market distorting practices that tilt the trade balance in their favor while eroding America's manufacturing base," and that the action aims to "level the playing field for American workers." He told [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/us-tariffs-trump-section-301.html) that for most partners the new rates are "not really additive" and would "top up a few percentage points," except for China, and that none would likely take effect "earlier than a couple months from now."

China's foreign ministry said, "The so-called 'overcapacity' does not really exist," according to Reuters via CNBC.

## Why it matters

The investigations began in March, weeks after the Supreme Court struck down tariffs Trump imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act. The administration has since used other trade laws to rebuild its tariff program. Supporters say Section 301 is the proper legal tool for answering foreign subsidies. Critics, including plaintiffs in an ongoing lawsuit over related forced-labor tariffs, argue the administration is using these statutes to revive tariffs the court rejected. For shoppers and businesses, final rates on imports from Asia and Europe won't be clear until after the January hearings.
