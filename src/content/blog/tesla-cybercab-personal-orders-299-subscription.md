You can now buy a Tesla Cybercab, if Tesla invites you and you live in the right place. On Friday, November 13, Tesla's Robotaxi account on X announced that "select customers" can order the two-seat, no-steering-wheel car **for personal use**, sharing screenshots of the order page and invite email, according to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/13/tesla-cybercab-personal-use-orders-299-subscription-geofence/) and [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/transportation/1033844/tesla-personal-cybercab-orders-299-subscription-fine-print).

## The deal

- **Price:** $29,990 before taxes.
- **Deposit:** $1,000, non-refundable.
- **Subscription:** a required **$299-a-month Autonomy Subscription**, with the first month included. The fine print says "Subscription and regular vehicle maintenance required to operate."
- **Where it works:** only in "supported service areas," meaning Tesla's Robotaxi zones. Electrek lists eight cities: Austin, Dallas, Houston, Fort Worth and San Antonio in Texas, and Miami, Orlando and Tampa in Florida.
- **Delivery:** estimated December 2026 in Austin. The invite email warns "initial availability is limited."

## The fine print

Because the Cybercab has no steering wheel or pedals, it can't be driven manually. The Verge points out that canceling the subscription leaves the car unable to operate on public roads. The Verge also reports the terms bar commercial use, so owners can't put it on Uber, DoorDash or Tesla's own Robotaxi network for now. Tesla AI chief Ashok Elluswamy has floated Q2 2027 as a possible target for letting customer cars join the network. Tesla can raise the subscription price with 30 days' notice, and the subscription covers autonomy, maintenance and towing but not insurance.

Both outlets put the five-year cost at roughly **$47,600** before taxes, insurance and charging. Elon Musk himself posted that the $299 fee is "too high," arguing it should be closer to $115, The Verge reports.

## The regulatory question

Federal safety standards were written for cars with steering wheels and pedals. Electrek and The Verge report NHTSA asked Tesla to justify how the Cybercab complies; Tesla responded in October and asked that its answer be kept confidential, and NHTSA hasn't ruled. Musk has framed a wider rollout as subject to regulatory approval.

## Why it matters

Musk promised a Cybercab under $30,000 when he unveiled it in 2024, and the sticker hits that. The subscription model is the twist: it turns a car purchase into an ongoing service, which could be a template for how self-driving cars get sold. The Verge, citing the crowd-sourced Robotaxi Tracker, says 278 Cybercabs are already running in Tesla's fleet, about 40% of it.
