If you've shopped for RAM, a new PC or a graphics card lately, you've felt the memory crunch. Now a lawsuit claiming that crunch wasn't just bad luck is headed further into court.

## What happened

On **Friday, November 14**, U.S. District Judge Noël Wise of the Northern District of California **denied in full** the joint motion by Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron to dismiss a proposed class action, according to the court order published by [LitNews](https://litnews.ai/articles/garciaguirre-samsung-sk-hynix-micron-dram-conspiracy-suit-survives-dismissal) and reporting by [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/judge-denies-motion-to-dismiss-dram-price-fixing-lawsuit-against-micron-samsung-and-sk-hynix-says-chipmakers-acted-like-hawks-flying-in-formation-finds-evidence-of-dram-collusion-plausible-after-500-percent-price-surge) and [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/federal-judge-rejects-memory-makers-defense-samsung-micron-sk-hynix-dram-shortage-lawsuit/).

The suit (*Garciaguirre v. Samsung Electronics*) was filed **June 25, 2026**, by 17 consumers and small businesses from five states. It alleges the three companies, which it says controlled **more than 91% of global DRAM revenue** as of the third quarter of 2025, agreed to cut supply of conventional memory like DDR5 starting in late 2022 and then shifted factory capacity to high-bandwidth memory (HBM) for AI chips.

## What the suit claims

According to the complaint, conventional DRAM prices rose **171.8% in one year** through Q3 2025, yet none of the three companies meaningfully expanded production. It also points to long-term HBM supply deals, Micron's December 2025 decision to close its 30-year-old Crucial consumer brand, delayed fabs, a near-simultaneous wind-down of DDR4, and nearly identical customer-vetting rules the companies allegedly adopted in January 2026.

The companies argued the case was a rerun of an earlier DRAM suit the Ninth Circuit threw out in 2022, and that rivals in a three-player market simply "watch each other like hawks." Judge Wise disagreed, writing that the plaintiffs' allegations "are more akin to hawks flying in formation – an activity that hawks ordinarily do not do." She gave no weight to some of the plaintiffs' arguments, including investor talk of "supply discipline" and executives meeting at trade conferences.

## What it does and doesn't mean

This is a ruling on whether the complaint tells a **plausible** story, not a verdict. Nothing has been proven, none of the companies has been found liable, and all three are expected to fight the claims. Their answers are due **December 7, 2026**, with a case management conference set for **January 12, 2027**.
