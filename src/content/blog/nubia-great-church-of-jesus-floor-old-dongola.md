On the banks of the Nile in northern Sudan, archaeologists have finally reached the floor where medieval worshippers once stood inside what may be the biggest church ever built in Nubia.

## What happened

A team from the Polish Centre of Mediterranean Archaeology at the University of Warsaw and Sudan's National Corporation for Antiquities and Museums has been excavating the **Great Church of Jesus** at Old Dongola, once the capital of the Christian kingdom of Makuria. In its latest season, the team uncovered a monumental **staircase** leading into the church and buttresses supporting the apse, and for the first time reached the cathedral's **original floor**, according to the [University of Warsaw](https://en.uw.edu.pl/the-largest-church-in-medieval-nubia/) and [Archaeology magazine](https://archaeology.org/news/2026/11/09/archaeologists-uncover-original-floor-of-nubias-largest-medieval-cathedral/). The find drew wide attention on Monday after an Associated Press report, which also reached the front page of Hacker News.

The numbers are impressive:

- About **47 meters wide and 57 meters long** (roughly 154 by 187 feet), with a cautiously estimated height of around 21 meters.
- Some walls near the staircase still stand about **11 meters** tall.
- Expedition director Artur Obłuski says it was "a building larger than the famous White House in Washington, D.C."

Ironically, the church's excellent preservation is the hardest part. Because the walls survive so high, the team has to dig to unusual depths, and only a small part of the building has been excavated so far.

## A second church and Greek hymns

In a building attached to the cathedral, the team found another church on an upper floor. Its walls survive only about a meter high, but researchers have filled six large shelving units with fragments of painted plaster. Some fragments carry **Greek inscriptions** from biblical odes, including a piece identified as the Prayer of Hannah from the Old Testament, written in letters only about half a centimeter tall.

## Why it matters

Makuria converted to Christianity in the sixth century and flourished for centuries along the Nile. Obłuski compares the Great Church of Jesus to Hagia Sophia in Istanbul as a possible architectural "trendsetter" for the region. Until now, another church outside the city walls was thought to be Dongola's cathedral, so that view may need revising. [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/23/2026/vast-medieval-church-uncovered-in-ancient-region-of-nubia) notes the church dates to at least the 13th century and may be older.

The work is happening despite Sudan's ongoing conflict and 2024 floods that damaged protective roofs at the site. The expedition also installed solar-powered pumps that supply Nile water to homes and fields for around 10,000 local residents. The team hopes to settle the church's full size next season.
