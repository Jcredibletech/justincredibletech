We already knew Grand Theft Auto VI's launch broke the internet a little. Now we know how much money it made on day one, and the number is bigger than any movie has ever made in its entire run.

## What happened

On **Friday, November 20, 2026**, Rockstar Games' parent company **Take-Two Interactive** [announced](https://www.take2games.com/ir/news/grand-theft-auto-vi-shatters-entertainment-box-office-records) that GTA VI's day-one retail sales reached **$3.79 billion**. Take-Two called it "a new record for entertainment across any medium" and "the biggest entertainment launch in history."

Key details from the release and from [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/gaming/news/gta-6-sales-3-79-billion-1236954198/):

- The figure is a **company estimate** of worldwide sales through the **end of the day on November 19**, launch day. Variety describes it as preorder plus first-day sales.
- GTA VI sold **32.5 million units before release**, which Take-Two says is a record across all platforms.
- Take-Two declined to tell Variety how many copies have sold in total.
- The game launched **Thursday, November 19** on **PlayStation 5** and **Xbox Series X|S**, and on Xbox One through Xbox Cloud Gaming. It costs **$80** for the standard edition and **$100** for the Ultimate edition.

## Putting $3.79 billion in perspective

Variety points out that the highest-grossing movie ever, **Avengers: Endgame**, made about **$2.9 billion** at the global box office over its whole theatrical run. GTA VI passed that in one day. Variety also reports the game cost about **$1.5 billion** to make over more than a decade of development, so day one alone covered that reported budget more than twice over.

It's worth keeping the comparison fair: game sales and box-office tickets aren't the same thing. An $80 game costs far more than a movie ticket, and these are Take-Two's own estimates, not audited results. We should see firmer numbers in Take-Two's next quarterly earnings report.

## Why it matters

GTA VI is now officially the launch every other entertainment release will be measured against. It also matters for everyone who makes games: a hit this big shows that a long, expensive, single-player-focused blockbuster can still pay off, even as costs climb. The game holds a **99 on Metacritic**, tying the highest score ever, and is nominated for Game of the Year at The Game Awards in December, according to Variety.
