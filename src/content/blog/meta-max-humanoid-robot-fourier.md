Most companies take years to get a humanoid robot standing up. Meta and its partner reportedly did it in about three months.

That's the headline from a [report by The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/meta-built-humanoid-prototype-chinese-startup-ramp-robotics-push) on Saturday, and it's one of the most talked-about tech stories on X this weekend. Here's what we know, and what we don't.

## What happened

According to The Information, which cited people familiar with the project, Meta built a prototype humanoid robot with **Fourier**, a robotics company based in Shanghai. Inside Meta, the robot is called **Max**.

The reported specs:

- About **1.5 meters (roughly 5 feet) tall** and about **48 kg (106 pounds)**
- Runs on **Nvidia's Jetson Thor** chip
- A **screen for a face** that mimics facial expressions, plus built-in speakers
- **Five-fingered hands** for picking up small objects like bottles and toys

Meta's vice president of product for robotics, Li-Chen Miller, reportedly spent almost two months at Fourier's factory in Shanghai, and Max was shown to some Meta employees at an internal robotics summit in late August, per [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-humanoid-robot-fourier-max) and [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/meta-builds-humanoid-robot-with-chinas-fourier-as-home-robot-push-expands-report-4989306), which both relayed the report.

## Max 2 is already in the works

Work on a second version reportedly started in September. **Max 2** is expected to get a redesigned torso, better hands for handling objects, a projector and extra sensors to help it deal with stairs.

## What Meta actually says

This part matters. Meta told The Information that its work with Fourier is for **internal research and experimentation** and that it has **no plans to sell these units**. One source also said Meta hasn't set a launch date for any humanoid product. The Next Web, which relayed the report, noted that it hasn't independently verified it.

So this isn't a product launch. Nobody's preordering a Meta robot butler. It's a peek at how fast Meta's robotics team is moving behind the scenes.

## Why it matters

Meta has been open about wanting humanoid robots that can help with household chores, and The Information reports the company sees them as a physical body for Muse, its AI agent. Building a working prototype in a few months, with an outside hardware partner, suggests Meta is trying to skip a lot of the slow, expensive early steps.

It also puts Meta squarely in a crowded race. Tesla is building Optimus, Figure and Agility are pushing their own humanoids, and Chinese makers like Unitree and Fourier are shipping hardware fast. The hard part, as plenty of robotics folks point out, isn't building the body. It's training a robot to handle a messy real-world kitchen without breaking anything (or anyone).
