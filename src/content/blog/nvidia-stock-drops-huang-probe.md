If you have a 401(k) or an S&P 500 index fund, there's a good chance a slice of it is Nvidia, one of the biggest stocks in the index. That's why this morning matters even if you've never bought a share yourself. The day after Nvidia's board opened an investigation into its own CEO, Wall Street weighed in, and "Nvidia" and "Jensen Huang" were still sitting on X's US trending list.

## What happened Monday

- **The drop.** Nvidia closed Friday at $186.64. When trading opened Monday, shares fell almost 7.5% to about $172.75, and as much as 8% at one point, before paring losses to around 6% ([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/live/2026/nov/09/nvidia-jensen-huang-scandal-investigation-ai-industry-tech-stocks-recovery-slump-latest-news-updates), [Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/nvidias-outofhour-shares-fall-as-firm-probes-ceo-jensen-huang-5010476)). At a 6% loss, Reuters put the hit at more than $280 billion in market value; The Telegraph, measuring from the low, put it near $395 billion ([The Telegraph](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/business/2026/11/09/nvidia-faces-200bn-wipe-out-over-jensen-huang-chinese-spy-a/)).
- **It was already sliding.** Before Monday, the stock had fallen almost 20% over the past month in a broader sell-off of AI stocks ([CBS News via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-stock-slumps-amid-board-141523990.html)).
- **A new report.** The New York Times reported that a Commerce Department investigation into Megaspeed, a Singapore company suspected of smuggling Nvidia AI chips into China and led by the executive U.S. officials believe is a Chinese spy, was transferred to the Justice Department late last year. That happened after Nvidia's general counsel emailed investigators saying a top Commerce official had given Nvidia permission to ask about the case. The Justice Department is now running a criminal inquiry into Megaspeed. Nvidia says it consults with officials to answer questions and share information, and Commerce says it handled the case "strictly by the book" ([NYT via The Daily Gazette](https://www.dailygazette.com/nyt-feed/commerce-dept-chip-inquiry-was-halted-after-nvidia-intervention/article_689206b1-57d7-5d0a-bb41-e4b1f4f5cb92.html)).
- **China's response.** China's foreign ministry called the spy story "entirely baseless" and a "smear" ([CBS News via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-stock-slumps-amid-board-141523990.html)).
- **Huang stays.** Nvidia's board says it has confidence in him, and most analysts don't expect him to step down, though they acknowledge uncertainty around succession, Reuters reports.

None of the espionage claims have been proven in public. For the background, see yesterday's post on [the board's investigation](/blog/nvidia-board-investigates-jensen-huang/) and Saturday's on [the spy-agency reports](/blog/nvidia-jensen-huang-china-spy-congress/).

## My take

This is the free market doing its job. Nobody in Washington had to pass a law for Nvidia to pay a price. Investors did it in a single morning. That's healthy. What I want next is an investigation that's actually independent, at the board and at the Justice Department, because "we looked into ourselves and we're fine" won't cut it with shareholders or with anyone who cares about keeping top AI chips away from Beijing. And to be clear, this isn't investment advice. I'm not telling anybody to buy or sell anything.
