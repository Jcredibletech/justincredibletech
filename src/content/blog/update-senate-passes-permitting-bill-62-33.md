*This is an update to our November 17 story, [Senate Votes 70-27 to Advance a Bipartisan Permitting Overhaul](/blog/senate-advances-permitting-reform-bill-70-27/).*

The big bipartisan bill to speed up approvals for power lines and energy projects has cleared the Senate. Now it's the House's turn.

## What happened

On **Thursday night, November 19, 2026**, the Senate voted **62-33** to pass the **Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act**, [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/senate-passes-sweeping-bill-simplifying-permitting-rules-build-america-rcna610196) and [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/11/19/sweeping-permitting-overhaul-passes-senate-on-to-house) reported. **31 Democrats** and **two Republicans**, Sens. **Josh Hawley** (R-Mo.) and **Mike Rounds** (R-S.D.), voted no, according to Roll Call.

The bill was written by Sens. **Shelley Moore Capito** (R-W.Va.), **Sheldon Whitehouse** (D-R.I.), **Mike Lee** (R-Utah) and **Martin Heinrich** (D-N.M.). No additional amendments got votes after Sens. Jeff Merkley and Ron Wyden (both D-Ore.) declined to agree to a time deal, Roll Call reported.

## What the bill would do

According to NBC News and Roll Call, the bill would:

- **Speed up environmental reviews** with tighter deadlines under laws including NEPA.
- Give the **Federal Energy Regulatory Commission** new authority over **interstate transmission lines**.
- **Limit agencies' power to revoke** existing permits, including on ideological grounds.
- Require **new data centers of 20 megawatts or more** to pay the full added cost of grid infrastructure built to serve them, per the Bipartisan Policy Center.

## What each side says

Capito: "For far too long, critical investments in our nation's infrastructure, energy production, and economy have been forced to sit on the sidelines due to endless red tape, duplicative reviews, and regulatory uncertainty."

Whitehouse: "Democrats are focused on lowering energy costs, reducing emissions, and stopping data centers' free ride."

Opponents include environmental groups such as the **Sierra Club** and **NRDC**, some Native American tribal organizations, and a coalition of 17 Democratic-led states, Roll Call reported. Merkley said he hoped "to address the many legitimate concerns that stakeholders have raised." The **White House** has said it supports the bill.

## What happens next

The bill goes to the **House** after the Thanksgiving recess. House Majority Leader **Steve Scalise** (R-La.) said, "we want to get a bill to the president's desk," while noting "the details matter." Senate Majority Leader **John Thune** (R-S.D.) said the clearest path is for the House to pass the Senate version unchanged. Some Democrats want to wait until the new Congress, when their party will hold majorities; the bill's Democratic authors argued any law needs President Trump's signature anyway.

## Why it matters to you

Electricity demand is rising, partly because of data centers. This bill could change how fast new power lines and plants get built, and who pays for grid upgrades, which can show up on your power bill.
