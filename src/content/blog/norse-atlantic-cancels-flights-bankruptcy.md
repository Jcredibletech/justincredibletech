**Norse Atlantic Airways**, the low-cost airline that flew Boeing 787s on long-haul routes from Europe, suspended all flights on **Thursday, Nov. 26**, after failing to secure short-term funding. On **Friday, Nov. 27**, the board of its Norwegian operating subsidiary, Norse Atlantic Airways AS, said it would file for **bankruptcy** with the Agder District Court in Norway. All Norse flights are cancelled and ticket sales have stopped.

## What happened

Norse warned on Wednesday, in a statement to the Oslo Stock Exchange, that its financial situation was critical, according to the [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c9kgmd0ldy0lo). After flights stopped, the company said it was talking with investors about a takeover. That didn't come together in time.

In its [Friday filing notice](https://news.cision.com/norse-atlantic-airways-as/r/norse-atlantic-asa--norwegian-operating-subsidiary-filing-for-bankruptcy,c4430282), CEO Eivind Roald said: "We are deeply sorry for the impact this has on our passengers, employees and partners." The listed parent, Norse Atlantic ASA, and its UK subsidiary are **not** part of the bankruptcy filing, and the company says it is still trying to find a way for the rest of the business to keep operating.

Key numbers:

- **12** Boeing 787 aircraft in the fleet, per the BBC.
- Routes included London Gatwick to **Orlando**, Cape Town, Bangkok and Phuket, plus flights from Rome and Oslo.
- **$94.6 million** loss in the first half of 2026, according to the BBC.
- Pakistan International Airlines, which leased planes and crews from Norse, had to cancel some UK flights and switch to its own aircraft.

## Why it matters

Norse launched in 2021 to fill the gap left when Norwegian pulled out of long-haul flying. The BBC reports it never hedged fuel, so it was fully exposed when jet fuel prices spiked this year during the war with Iran, at one point roughly doubling. That's a reminder of how thin margins are for budget long-haul carriers.

For travelers, it matters right now. Orlando was one of Norse's U.S. destinations, and anyone holding a ticket needs a new way home or a new trip. The [UK Civil Aviation Authority](https://www.caa.co.uk/newsroom/news/uk-civil-aviation-authority-statement-norse-atlantic-uk-limited-norse/) urged passengers **not to go to the airport**. It says people who booked directly by credit card may be covered under UK card protections, debit and charge card users should ask their card issuer about chargebacks, and people who booked through a travel agent should contact that agent first. Norway's consumer council says refunds in a bankruptcy are harder to get, and passengers stand far back in line for the estate's assets.

## What's next

A court-appointed trustee will manage the Norwegian unit's bankruptcy. The parent company says it's still working on a solution for its UK operations, but for now every Norse flight is cancelled. If a buyer steps in, routes could come back under new ownership.
