**OpenAI** fired back at **Apple** on **Friday, Nov. 27**, filing counterclaims in federal court in San Jose, California, that accuse the iPhone maker of taking OpenAI's hardware trade secrets and pressuring manufacturers not to work on OpenAI devices. Apple denies the claims and says they are "a transparent attempt to distract" from the lawsuit it filed against OpenAI in July.

## What happened

The fight started in **July**, when Apple sued OpenAI and two former Apple engineers, alleging a "pattern of theft" of confidential information about unreleased Apple products. OpenAI has denied wrongdoing. In **October**, according to [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/openai-accuses-apple-of-trade-secret-theft-unfair-hiring-restrictions-126112800093_1.html), OpenAI voluntarily agreed not to use the information Apple says was taken after the judge signaled he was likely to order it.

Now OpenAI is suing back. Its filing, an 81-page answer with **12 counterclaims** in *Apple Inc. v. Liu* before U.S. District Judge Edward Davila, makes three main allegations:

- **Trade secrets:** OpenAI says an engineer who worked there for about six months, then went back to Apple, sent OpenAI files about at least one unreleased device to personal accounts and phone numbers, and later briefed senior Apple staff on OpenAI's hardware plans "at Apple's request," as the [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cm15ekqvlezeo) summarized it. The employee isn't named.
- **Supplier pressure:** OpenAI claims Apple asked shared manufacturers to identify their OpenAI projects and workers. In one example, a finishing supplier allegedly moved OpenAI work to a night shift to keep it separate from Apple's.
- **Employee agreements:** OpenAI argues Apple's employment contracts define confidential information so broadly that they unfairly keep engineers from leaving.

## What Apple says

An Apple spokesperson told the BBC: "This case is about OpenAI employees deliberately stealing, accessing, and using Apple's secret and confidential information regarding our unreleased technologies, processes, and products - not about inadvertent retention of Apple information." Apple also said it has policies to stop employees from using a former employer's confidential information and found no evidence to back OpenAI's claims, according to Business Standard.

None of either side's allegations has been tested in court yet.

## Why it matters

This is really a fight over the next big gadget. OpenAI is building its own AI devices and, in the filing quoted by [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/5f374b32b07b/technology/openai-countersues-apple-stealing-trade-secrets-ft-reports-2026-11-27/), says they could "outcompete Apple on what Apple views as its own turf." Both companies rely on the same small group of contract manufacturers in Asia, so any claim that Apple used its buying power to squeeze a rival could draw attention beyond this one case. Apple, for its part, is trying to protect its product secrets, something it has guarded fiercely for decades. We've covered Apple's own hardware roadmap, including the [foldable iPhone Duo](/blog/apple-iphone-duo-foldable/), and OpenAI's latest models like [GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol](/blog/openai-releases-gpt-6-2-astra-sol/).

## What's next

OpenAI has asked for a jury trial on its counterclaims and has separately asked the judge to throw out Apple's case. Apple will get to respond to the new claims in court. No trial date has been reported.
