Google is back in the smart glasses business. On Thursday, November 12, 2026, it opened **preorders for Intelligent Eyewear**, glasses with Gemini built in, made with **Samsung** (which engineered the hardware) and eyewear brands **Warby Parker** and **Gentle Monster**. They go on sale in stores and online on **December 10**.

## What you get

According to [Google's announcement](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/xr-ar/google-intelligent-eyewear-collections-pre-order/) and [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/12/google-intelligent-eyewear-glasses/):

- **No display.** These are camera-and-audio glasses. You talk to Gemini and hear answers through open-ear speakers.
- **Price.** Warby Parker frames (Alden, Reid and Wren) start at **$395**. Gentle Monster's six bolder designs start at **$449**. Prescription lenses are available for most frames.
- **Camera.** A 12-megapixel ultra-wide camera shoots photos and video (up to 3K at 30 fps), with stabilization and HDR.
- **Battery.** Up to nine hours per charge, and the case holds about seven more charges. The frames weigh around 40 to 44 grams.
- **Phones.** They work with Pixel 7 and newer, recent Galaxy phones on One UI 9.0 or higher, and iPhone 13 Pro and newer.
- **Availability.** U.S. and South Korea only for now, and you need an 18+ Google account.

## What they do

Say "Hey Gemini" or tap the frame to ask about what you're looking at, add items to a grocery list, check your calendar, get turn-by-turn Google Maps directions, or hear live speech translation in more than 70 languages. A feature called **Guided Vision** describes surroundings for people who are blind or have low vision; at launch it requires an Android phone. You can also take calls and play music.

## The privacy pitch

Camera glasses make bystanders nervous, and Google is leaning hard into that concern. It says:

- A **white light** shows when you're saving a photo or video, and recording **stops if the light is covered** or disabled.
- Images Gemini looks at aren't saved to your Gemini History or used to train AI models or target ads, and bystanders' **faces are automatically blurred** before Gemini processes them.
- Every pair has a **physical off switch**, so they can work as regular glasses.

Those are Google's descriptions of how the product is designed; independent reviewers will be able to test them once the glasses ship.

## Why it matters

Meta has had the smart glasses market largely to itself. A Google and Samsung product sold through a mainstream eyewear chain with 360+ stores gives shoppers a real alternative, and it's the first big test of Gemini as an always-with-you assistant outside the phone.
