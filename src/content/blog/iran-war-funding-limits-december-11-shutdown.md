Congress is on its Thanksgiving break, but the clock is running. Current government funding expires **December 11**, and the biggest sticking point is the war with Iran.

## What happened

- **Democrats' proposal:** Sen. Tim Kaine (D-Va.) told [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/democrats-float-blocking-iran-war-funding-for-more-defense-money) on November 20 that a group of Senate Democrats has mostly finalized language that would bar funding for the U.S.-Israel war with Iran, with some exceptions. It could ride on a short-term bill to keep the government open, in exchange for Democratic support for defense spending Republicans want. The full Democratic caucus still has to see it.
- **A possible trade:** Speaking at the Halifax International Security Forum on November 21, Kaine said Democrats could agree to part of the administration's request for more munitions money in exchange for limits on war spending, [Inside Defense](https://insidedefense.com/daily-news/kaine-floats-dem-budget-leverage-options-munitions-deals-white-house-funding-cuts) reported.
- **Republican response:** On Monday, [The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/11/23/senate-gop-leaders-signal-openness-cutting-off-iran-war-funding/) reported that key Senate Republicans say they are willing to consider the Democratic demand to fund the Pentagon while cutting off money for the war. No deal has been announced.

## Where each side stands

According to [Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/congress-struggles-to-avert-us-shutdown-as-iran-war-divides-lawmakers-5041259):

- **President Trump** has asked for **$350 billion** in new Pentagon spending. Senate Republicans have not agreed to pass it on a party-line vote through budget reconciliation and instead are pursuing bipartisan talks on a $1.1 trillion defense bill that includes $69 billion of that request. Republicans say reconciliation is still on the table.
- **Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-S.D.)** said another shutdown "would be a colossal mistake and a real disservice to the people of this country." Republicans argue Democrats have stalled talks ahead of their own December 1 leadership vote. Reuters notes Democrats have forced three shutdowns since January 2025.
- **Democrats are split.** "We don't want to fund the war, we don't want to shut down, how do you solve that," said Sen. Peter Welch (D-Vt.). Sen. John Hickenlooper (D-Colo.) said high energy prices from the war make the situation "pretty dire." Rep. Rosa DeLauro (D-Conn.), the top Democrat on House Appropriations, said, "Shutting the government down doesn't stop the war."
- **Sen. Mike Rounds (R-S.D.)** warned, "We're going to run out of time" without faster talks.

A bipartisan funding package needs 60 votes in the Senate. Democrats take control of both the House and Senate in January after winning the November midterms.

## Why it matters to you

If Congress misses December 11, agencies that don't already have full-year funding would shut down just before Christmas, which can delay federal paychecks and some government services. Separately, a war-funding limit would be one of Congress's most direct moves yet to constrain military action against Iran. Lawmakers return the week after Thanksgiving with about two weeks to strike a deal.
