America's largest measles outbreak in decades has claimed another life, days before millions of families travel for Thanksgiving.

## What happened

The Pennsylvania Department of Health [confirmed Monday](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/health/newsroom/pennsylvania-department-of-health-confirms-ninth-measles-associa) that an **unvaccinated resident of Centre County** who tested positive for measles has died. It's the state's **ninth measles-associated death in 2026**. The department did not release the person's age or other details, citing privacy.

Where Pennsylvania's outbreak stands, according to the department:

- **1,638 confirmed cases** across **46 counties** since the outbreak began in late April
- **49 new cases** since November 18
- **330 hospitalizations**
- **Nine deaths**, in Centre, Clinton, Jefferson (2), Lancaster (3), Mifflin and Montour counties
- **5 cases** in vaccinated people, less than 1% of the total

[CIDRAP](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/measles/virginia-pennsylvania-report-new-measles-deaths-bringing-us-total-13) at the University of Minnesota calls Pennsylvania the nation's measles hot spot and its outbreak the largest of any state since the early 1990s. With Virginia's [first measles death since 1978](/blog/virginia-child-measles-death-2026/), reported last week, states have now reported **13 measles-related deaths** this year. The CDC has confirmed six of them so far, CIDRAP notes.

## Why it matters

Measles is highly contagious. It spreads through the air when an infected person breathes, coughs or sneezes, and it can linger for up to two hours after they leave a room, according to the Pennsylvania health department. Complications can include pneumonia and brain swelling, and the department says nearly 20% of Pennsylvania cases have been hospitalized.

The deaths come a week after the U.S. officially [lost its measles elimination status](/blog/update-paho-confirms-us-loses-measles-elimination-status/) on November 17. CIDRAP reports the 426 new cases the CDC confirmed last week were the largest one-week jump in decades.

Pennsylvania officials say demand for the MMR vaccine has surged: providers gave nearly 62,000 doses in October, compared with about 25,000 in a typical month. The department says two doses are about 97% effective at preventing measles, and it recommends an extra early dose for infants 6 to 11 months old in counties with sustained spread.

This is news, not medical advice. Anyone unsure of their vaccination status, or worried about an exposure, should talk with a doctor or pharmacist. Pennsylvania's health hotline is 877-PA-HEALTH.
