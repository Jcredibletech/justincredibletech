Tesla's supervised self-driving software just got a green light in one of Europe's biggest car markets, before the European Union as a whole has decided.

## What happened

On **Friday, November 20, 2026**, Italy's Deputy Prime Minister and Transport Minister **Matteo Salvini** said on X that the transport ministry had given **provisional approval** to **Tesla Assisted Driving (TAD)**, the name Tesla now uses in Europe for what it calls **Full Self-Driving (Supervised)** in the U.S., [Reuters reported](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/italy-gives-green-light-tesla-self-driving-system-ahead-eu-approval). Salvini said the decision followed "a thorough technical feasibility assessment" and called it "another step forward toward innovation, technology and the mobility of the future."

Tesla Europe posted that the system is "now approved in Italy, rollout begins soon," along with a video of a car driving itself through Rome, according to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/20/tesla-fsd-italy-approval-assisted-driving/). Tesla hasn't given a firm rollout date.

## What it is, and isn't

TAD is a **Level 2 driver-assistance** system. Reuters describes it as "hands-off, eyes-on": it can steer, brake, accelerate, and handle intersections and lane changes, but the driver must **supervise at all times** and remains fully responsible for the car. It is **not** an unsupervised robotaxi system.

## Where Europe stands

- The **Netherlands** gave the first European approval in April, and countries including **Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark and Belgium** followed, per Reuters.
- Electrek counts Italy as the **12th** European country to allow the system and the first of the EU's large markets.
- A bloc-wide decision rests with the EU's **Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles**, which Electrek says isn't expected to vote until **December at the earliest**. EU-wide approval needs a qualified majority of member states.

## The other side of the world

Not every regulator is moving Tesla's way. On the same day, South Korea's transport ministry said it **could not review** Tesla's request for an FSD exemption, citing insufficient documentation, [Yonhap reported](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20261120007100320).

## Why it matters

Europe has been one of the hardest places for Tesla to sell its driver-assistance software. Italy moving ahead of Brussels adds pressure on the EU vote and gives Tesla a much larger market to sell FSD-style subscriptions in. For drivers there, the key point is that the car still needs you watching the road.
