Japan's car companies compete hard on showroom floors. Behind the dashboard, they now plan to start building with the same rulebook.

## What happened

At a Tokyo news conference on **November 19, 2026**, the **Japan Automobile Manufacturers Association (JAMA)** laid out a plan for its members, including **Toyota, Nissan and Honda**, to standardize parts and materials that customers rarely see. The association is chaired by **Koji Sato**, Toyota's vice chairman, [Automotive News](https://www.autonews.com/toyota/an-japan-industrywide-auto-parts-standardization-1119/) and [Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a74490092/toyota-nissan-honda-standardize-parts-lower-costs/) reported.

The first three targets:

- **Steel:** consolidating low-volume grades, unifying the extra requirements each automaker adds on top of Japan Iron and Steel Federation standards, and using one set of quality tests.
- **Plastic resin:** starting with recycled resin, with shared quality standards and work with the chemical industry on low-carbon materials.
- **Wiring harnesses:** JAMA members use about **70,000 different kinds** worldwide, largely built by hand, according to [Toyota Report](https://toyotareport.com/2026/11/toyota-leads-a-push-to-end-japans-70000-kinds-of-wiring-harness/). The plan includes more aluminum wire in place of copper, common rules such as one shade of orange for high-voltage cables and the force needed to seat a connector, plus more automation.

JAMA's slides describe a short-term phase of about three years for vehicles already in production and a five-to-10-year phase for new models, with more components added over time. Talks start with Japanese suppliers, and international suppliers will be brought in later, Car and Driver reported.

## Why they're doing it

"We all share this strong sense of crisis, creating a momentum for change," Sato said, as quoted by Car and Driver. Japan's seven automakers face growing pressure from Chinese brands, and the companies hope to put the savings into new technology. The steel, resin and harness areas were picked because they are big cost items and don't affect what makes each brand distinctive.

Honda president Toshihiro Mibe said customers don't see things like harness routing, so that area can become common, Toyota Report noted. The task force also said it would proceed with close attention to antitrust law.

## What it means for buyers

Don't expect a Civic and a Camry to share a dashboard. The savings, if they come, would show up indirectly in prices or features. JAMA hasn't put a dollar figure on them, and it hasn't said whether the standards will apply at its members' North American plants.
