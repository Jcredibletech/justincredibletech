Germany's car factories, long the gold standard for well-paid manufacturing jobs, are heading into what one consultant calls a "difficult winter." The first sign showed up this weekend: production lines at Mercedes-Benz went quiet.

## What happened

IG Metall, Germany's largest union, called on **more than 23,000 workers at 19 sites in northern Germany** to join a 24-hour "warning strike," [Euronews reports](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/23/mercedes-bmw-hit-by-24-hour-strikes-as-german-pay-dispute-widens). The walkouts began Sunday night as night shifts started, including at Mercedes-Benz plants in **Bremen and Hamburg**. German broadcaster ARD reported the Hamburg lines had been idle since 10 PM Sunday. Wind-turbine maker Siemens Gamesa and the Meyer Werft shipyard were also affected.

This follows nationwide walkouts on Friday that IG Metall says drew 151,000 workers, including at Audi's main plant in Ingolstadt and three BMW plants in Bavaria.

## The two sides

- **The union** wants a 5% raise over one year for 3.7 million metal and electrical workers, including auto workers. It says the employers' offer would leave workers worse off after inflation.
- **Employers** have offered a one-time payment in July 2027 worth 1% of annual pay, then a 1.3% raise from July 2028. The employers' group Südwestmetall called the 24-hour strikes an "unnecessary escalation" and urged a return to talks, a statement Mercedes-Benz said it fully supports.

Mercedes confirmed its plants were affected and said it was adjusting operations, without giving production numbers.

## The bigger picture

[Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/german-automakers-face-winter-of-discontent-as-union-battles-loom-5044405) lays out why both sides are dug in:

- Mercedes-Benz and BMW's nine-month auto profit margins fell to **1.9% and 3.5%**, down from 14.9% and 8.7% four years ago. Volkswagen last month posted its biggest operating loss in more than a decade.
- German auto workers earn about **€65 ($71) an hour**, more than one and a half times what U.S. workers make, according to the Kiel Institute.
- Volkswagen starts separate talks with IG Metall on Tuesday. VW is seeking a two-year pay freeze, and the union has threatened strikes from January 1 if there's no deal. VW says four German sites have no planned successor models.
- Chinese competition, U.S. tariffs and weaker demand are squeezing profits that once paid for high wages.

## Why it matters to you

German brands build many of the cars sold in America, and Mercedes and BMW also run big U.S. plants. A long labor fight could mean tighter supply of some imported models, while the cost-cutting behind it shows how hard Chinese competition is hitting legacy automakers worldwide.
