Your next phone bill might have a rocket company's logo on it.

According to a [Washington Post report](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/11/02/inside-elon-musks-plan-upend-wireless-phone-industry/) published Monday morning, SpaceX is in talks to buy **Boost Mobile**, or at least get access to its network. "Boost Mobile" is trending on X this morning, and [Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/spacex-in-talks-to-buy-boost-mobile-or-use-its-network-washington-post-reports-4990666) and [MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/in-the-hunt-for-1-trillion-of-revenue-spacex-reportedly-targets-boost-mobile-for-a-deal-7eb4ea49) have both picked it up. Here's what's reported, and what isn't settled.

## What happened

The Post, citing two people familiar with the negotiations, says SpaceX has been talking with **EchoStar**, Boost's parent company, in recent months about launching its own wireless service through a deal. Elon Musk reportedly handled some of it personally and met EchoStar chairman **Charlie Ergen** last month.

The deal could take two shapes, per the report:

- SpaceX **buys Boost Mobile** outright, or
- SpaceX **gets access to Boost's network**, which Boost leases from AT&T and T-Mobile.

Nothing has been signed or announced. SpaceX and Musk didn't respond to the Post, and EchoStar declined to comment.

## Why Boost?

Because the big three said no. The Post reports Musk asked Verizon, AT&T and T-Mobile in recent months to give SpaceX access to their networks, and all three turned him down.

Boost is a different kind of door. EchoStar had about **7.38 million wireless subscribers** at the end of the second quarter, mostly on Boost and Gen Mobile, according to Reuters. That's a ready-made customer base.

## The catch

Boost doesn't operate its own cell towers, as The Next Web notes. It leases capacity from AT&T and T-Mobile, and people familiar with the talks told the Post those lease deals usually can't move to a new owner without the carrier's permission. So the very companies that told Musk no could still get a say.

## Why SpaceX wants this

SpaceX has been building toward **Starlink Mobile**, a service that mixes satellites with regular ground-based cell equipment. Satellite signals alone have a hard time indoors and in crowded cities, so a ground network fills the gap. In October, SpaceX agreed to buy about **$8 billion** in spectrum licenses aimed at cell service where satellites can't reach, and its CFO said on the latest earnings call that SpaceX wants to "become a major mobile carrier" in the US.

The prize is big: research firm MoffettNathanson puts the US wireless market at about **$235 billion**, per the Post. Investors noticed, too. Reuters reported EchoStar shares rose about 3.4% in premarket trading Monday while T-Mobile slipped about 2%.

## My take

Musk built a Supercharger network nobody thought he could pull off, so I wouldn't laugh this off. But wireless is a brutal business: towers, customer service, phone subsidies, all of it. Boost would give SpaceX customers on day one, but the real test is whether AT&T and T-Mobile play along.
