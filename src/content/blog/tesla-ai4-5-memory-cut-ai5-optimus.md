While Tesla owners spent the weekend posting videos of the new FSD v15 "Reality View," Elon Musk was on X explaining what's inside the computers that run it. Two details stood out: the next car computer is getting less memory than planned, and the next big Tesla chip isn't going into cars at all.

## What Musk said

**1. AI4.5 gets 24GB, not 32GB.** Replying to a post about the upgraded board on Sunday, November 22, Musk [wrote](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2124631858340049214): "RAM is super expensive, so we reduced AI4.5 from 32GB to 24GB. That will be fine. It's hard to use 32GB anyway, given memory bandwidth constraints." Tesla had earlier planned to double memory from 16GB to 32GB, [Drive Tesla reports](https://driveteslacanada.ca/news/tesla-ai4-5-ram-reduced-24gb-lpddr5x/).

**2. It's a modest upgrade.** Musk said AI4.5 is "a relatively small performance improvement that is mostly due to needing to upgrade RAM to LPDDR5X," a faster type of memory. Tesla previously targeted around mid-2027 for AI4.5 production, depending on Samsung.

**3. AI5 goes to Optimus.** Musk said Tesla's next-generation AI5 chip will be reserved for its Optimus humanoid robot, while vehicles stay on AI4, which he says has enough computing power for "superhuman" driving, according to [TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/11/23/news-tesla-cuts-ai4-5-memory-from-32gb-to-24gb-amid-soaring-costs-ai5-reserved-for-optimus/) and Drive Tesla.

**4. Costs are up.** Musk also said the current AI4 computer now costs about twice as much to make because of rising memory and storage prices, per Drive Tesla and TrendForce.

## The skeptics' view

[Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/23/tesla-fsd-v15-ai4-last-shot-compute-limit/) argues Musk's posts show how little headroom AI4 has left. Musk described Reality View as roughly a 1-billion-parameter model running on "half of one AI4 TRIP accelerator" and admitted its latency is "a bit high due to compute constraints." Electrek also questions how much backup computing an unsupervised car would keep if one chip failed, something Tesla hasn't explained.

Tesla hasn't said which vehicles will get AI4.5 or announced an official launch date.

## Why it matters to you

If you own or are shopping for a Tesla with AI4 (often called HW4), this is good and bad news. The good: Musk is promising your hardware is the long-term platform for FSD, not something AI5 will make obsolete. The open question: whether AI4 can actually deliver unsupervised driving, which Tesla has not yet shown. The memory squeeze also shows how soaring RAM prices are pressuring hardware makers, carmakers included.
