Turns out a lot of people did their Black Friday shopping before the pie came out.

U.S. shoppers spent a record **$7.1 billion** online on Thanksgiving Day, Thursday, November 26, 2026, according to Adobe Analytics data reported Friday morning by [Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/americas/us-online-spending-hits-record-71b-on-thanksgiving/4140816), [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4677049-black-friday-came-early-for-retailers-as-thanksgiving-delivered-record-71b-in-sales) and [TheFly](https://www.tipranks.com/news/the-fly/adobe-data-shows-ai-traffic-soaring-as-thanksgiving-online-sales-reach-7-1b-thefly-news).

## The numbers

- **$7.1 billion** spent online on Thanksgiving, up **10.4%** from a year earlier.
- That beat Adobe's own forecast of **$6.9 billion** (up 8.5%), which it published in [September](https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/09/adobe-us-holiday-shopping-season-to-hit-record).
- Last year's Thanksgiving came in at $6.4 billion, so the growth rate roughly doubled.
- Gaming and electronics led purchases, Adobe said, according to Anadolu.

Spread over 24 hours, $7.1 billion works out to roughly $4.9 million every minute. (That's our math, not Adobe's.)

## The tech angle: AI shopping and phones

The most interesting number is about how people shopped. Traffic to retail sites from AI-powered sources, like chatbots and AI browsers, rose **162%** from last year and converted 74% better than other traffic, according to a LinkedIn post by Adobe's global retail and consumer lead, as reported by TheFly. The same report said phones accounted for a record **63.2%** of online spending.

Those AI figures come from a single Adobe post, not a full press release, and other outlets have quoted different AI measurements, so treat them as early. They line up with Adobe's September forecast, which expected AI traffic to retail sites to jump 159% on Thanksgiving.

## What's next

These are forecasts, not results yet. Adobe expects:

| Day | Adobe forecast (U.S. online) | Change vs. 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Black Friday (Nov. 27) | $12.9 billion | +9.2% |
| Cyber Monday (Nov. 30) | $15.1 billion | +6.2% |
| Cyber Week (Thanksgiving to Cyber Monday) | $47.5 billion | +7.4% |
| Full season (Nov. 1 to Dec. 31) | $275.1 billion | +6.7% |

Cyber Monday would be the first single day of U.S. online spending above $15 billion. The National Retail Federation, which counts stores and online together, expects 182.7 million people to shop between Thanksgiving and Cyber Monday, down from 186.9 million last year, with Black Friday the busiest day at 126 million shoppers.

Adobe's numbers cover online sales at U.S. retail sites only, not in-store purchases. Adobe typically reports actual Black Friday results the following day, so we'll know Saturday whether the $12.9 billion forecast held up.

If you're shopping today: check prices against earlier in the month before you buy, and watch your card statements for anything you don't recognize.
