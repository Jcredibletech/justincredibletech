**This is an update** to our October preview, [SpaceX Will Try to Catch the WHOLE Starship on Flight 15](https://justincredibletech.com/blog/starship-flight-15-double-catch/). Launch day is here, and so is one last hurdle.

## The plan for today

SpaceX is targeting **Friday, November 13, 2026**, for Starship's 15th test flight from **Starbase, Texas**. The 75-minute window opens at **4:45 p.m. Central Time** (5:45 p.m. Eastern), according to [Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-launching-historic-double-catch-starship-test-flight-today-watch-it-live) and [Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/spacex-to-attempt-first-starship-spacecraft-recovery-on-land-in-15th-flight-5024836).

If it flies, here's the sequence:

1. **Booster catch, minutes after launch.** The Super Heavy booster is scheduled to return to Starbase for a tower catch about 6.5 minutes after liftoff.
2. **Starlink deployment.** The Starship upper stage heads to low Earth orbit, about 275 km up, and deploys **26 Starlink V3 satellites** over roughly 42 minutes. It's only the second batch of V3 satellites, after Flight 14 carried 26 on September 28.
3. **A day in orbit.** Ship circles Earth about 14 times.
4. **Ship catch on Saturday.** About 22.5 hours after launch, around **2:45 p.m. CT on Saturday, November 14**, Ship is scheduled to fly back to Starbase and be caught by the tower's "chopstick" arms. **That has never been done before.** SpaceX says it will divert Ship to a Pacific Ocean splashdown if anything goes wrong.

SpaceX calls a full recovery "a breakthrough moment in making Starship a reliable, rapidly reusable rocket."

## The catch before the catch: the FAA license

As of Friday morning, the **Federal Aviation Administration had not issued Flight 15's launch license**, Reuters reported. The FAA has typically issued Starship licenses about a day ahead. [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/prediction-markets/26/11/63238410/starship-flight-15) notes this flight is unusual because Ship's return path crosses land, including northern Mexico, and that the FAA's operations plan lists **Saturday, November 14** as a backup launch date. The FAA hasn't publicly said why the license is still pending, and SpaceX hadn't announced a delay as of this writing.

## Why it matters

Catching both stages and reflying them is the core of SpaceX's plan to make launches cheap and frequent. Elon Musk has said he expects weekly Starship launches by the first half of 2027. NASA also needs Starship to work: it has paid SpaceX about **$3.2 billion** to develop it as the Artemis moon lander, and SpaceX has spent at least **$15 billion** of its own money, according to Reuters. Big milestones like orbital refueling still lie ahead.
