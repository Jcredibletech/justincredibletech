If you were planning to buy the cheapest all-wheel-drive Tesla Model Y, that option just disappeared. As of Friday, November 13, 2026, Tesla has **pulled the $41,990 Model Y All-Wheel Drive** from its U.S. online configurator, according to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/13/tesla-kills-41990-model-y-awd-configurator/) and [Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/tesla-trims-model-y-offerings-all-wheel-drive-gets-axe/). Tesla has not commented publicly.

## What's left

Here's the U.S. Model Y lineup Electrek reported on Friday (prices before destination fee):

| Trim | Price | EPA range |
|---|---|---|
| Model Y Rear-Wheel Drive | $39,990 | 348 miles |
| Model Y Premium Rear-Wheel Drive | $45,990 | 355 miles |
| Model Y Premium All-Wheel Drive | $49,990 | 327 miles |
| Model Y Performance | $57,990 | — |
| Model Y L Premium (six seats) | $61,990 | — |

The base AWD trim launched in February 2026 with **294 miles of range** and a **4.6-second 0-60 mph** time. It was the stripped-down Model Y with a second motor, priced $2,000 over the base RWD.

Electrek also reports the Premium RWD and Premium AWD each cost $1,000 more than they did in February. Put together, the cheapest AWD Model Y has gone from **$41,990 to $49,990** in about nine months, an increase of roughly 19%.

## Can you still get one?

Some base AWD cars remain in Tesla's **inventory**, Teslarati reports, so buyers may still find one on a lot. The $39,990 RWD model is still available to order. Teslarati says Premium AWD deliveries are currently quoted for **January to March 2027**.

## Why Tesla might be doing this

Tesla didn't say. Electrek suggests two possibilities: Tesla may want to use limited Model Y production slots for higher-margin Premium trims, or the base AWD simply wasn't selling well. It fits a broader pattern of Tesla trimming its lineup. The company recently cut Cybertruck orders down to the Cyberbeast as it prepares to wind down Cybertruck production by late summer 2027 to make room for the Cybercab robotaxi.

## Why it matters

For buyers in snowy states, AWD isn't really optional. Electrek points out that the Chevrolet Equinox EV LT 1 AWD (from $40,295) and Ford Mustang Mach-E Select AWD (from $40,795) now undercut the cheapest AWD Model Y by more than $9,000. Electrek also notes the Model Y makes up most of Tesla's sales volume, so changes at the bottom of its lineup affect a lot of shoppers.
