The ground stop had not ended; the FAA reissued it at 4:46 p.m. Central and it remained in effect Friday night. The Federal Aviation Administration on Friday afternoon, November 13, 2026, ordered a nationwide ground stop on departing flights at U.S. airports after what Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy described as a communication issue affecting Air Route Traffic Control Centers.

The FAA's alert said the halt was "due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety," and that "all departing traffic should remain ground stopped until further notice." Inbound international flights would be managed case by case, according to [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/13/faa-nationwide-ground-stop), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/faa-orders-nationwide-ground-stop-departing-flights-reasons-safety-rcna609247), and [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/nation/2026/11/13/faa-orders-nationwide-groundstop-at-us-airports-citing-safety/92765499007/). USA Today also reported that flights already in the air were not being told to land immediately, consistent with the advisory's focus on departing traffic.

## What officials said

In a statement reported Friday afternoon by [BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/live/c6z9dwn12037t) and [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/nation/2026/11/13/faa-orders-nationwide-groundstop-at-us-airports-citing-safety/92765499007/), Duffy said: "The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop for all U.S. airports following a communication issue impacting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers. FAA technicians are investigating the issue. We will provide immediate updates as we have them."

The Associated Press also reported that the FAA grounded flights nationwide Friday afternoon. The agency has not issued a detailed public explanation of what failed, and this report does not go beyond what officials have confirmed.

[USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/nation/2026/11/13/faa-orders-nationwide-groundstop-at-us-airports-citing-safety/92765499007/), citing the advisory, said the notice listed an effective window from 3:54 p.m. Eastern time (2:54 p.m. Central) to 11:30 p.m. Eastern, while still telling departures to remain stopped until further notice. That listed end time is not a promise that flights would resume then.

## Where things stood later Friday afternoon

The notice that appeared on the FAA's website was later removed, [the BBC reported](https://www.bbc.com/news/live/c6z9dwn12037t). As of **3:32 p.m. Central time**, the FAA's live [National Airspace System status](https://nasstatus.faa.gov/) board no longer listed a nationwide ground stop. Several airports still showed departure delays, some attributed to equipment or traffic-management initiatives.

Shortly before the nationwide alert, Chicago's O'Hare and Midway airports were listed on ground-stop notices due to an "equipment outage," [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/faa-orders-nationwide-ground-stop-departing-flights-reasons-safety-rcna609247) reported, citing the NBC station in Chicago. The FAA board listed those Chicago stops as running until 4:30 p.m. Eastern (3:30 p.m. Central).

Nationwide ground stops are rare. Officials have not said how many flights were held, or when operations would return to normal. If you are traveling Friday, check your airline's app or website before you go to the airport. A ground stop holds departures; it does not automatically cancel every flight.
