There's a new robotaxi on Bay Area streets with nobody behind the wheel. It's a Lucid Gravity electric SUV, driven by Nuro's software, and it's headed for the Uber app.

## What happened

Nuro, Lucid and Uber [announced Tuesday](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261124557985/en/Nuro-Lucid-and-Uber-Begin-Driverless-Testing-on-Public-Roads-in-the-San-Francisco-Bay-Area) that they've started **fully driverless testing on public roads**, with no safety driver in the vehicle. Key details:

- **Where:** Palo Alto, Mountain View, Los Altos and Sunnyvale to start, with more Bay Area cities next year.
- **The fleet:** Lucid has delivered more than 100 Gravity SUVs with updated hardware and software, up from 75 engineering vehicles earlier this year, [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/transportation/1040676/uber-nuro-lucid-driverless-robotaxi-testing-bay-area) reports.
- **Next step:** Uber employee rides in driverless cars "soon," then a **public launch in the first quarter of 2027**, with Houston later in 2027.
- **The tech:** Each SUV runs the Level 4 "Nuro Driver" system using cameras, solid-state lidar and radar, plus a roof "halo" light that shows a rider's initials, according to The Verge.

Since December 2025, Nuro has tested the Gravity around the clock in the Bay Area and Houston with a safety driver aboard. Before removing that driver, the company says it ran driverless tests on a closed course in Las Vegas covering situations like pedestrian encounters and drivers running right-of-way, along with large-scale simulation.

"Removing the safety operator from behind the wheel is an important milestone," said Samarth Kejriwal, Uber's global head of autonomous mobility, in the announcement.

## Why it matters

Only six companies hold a California DMV permit to test with no driver: AutoMo, Nuro, Tensor, Waymo, WeRide and Zoox, according to the DMV list cited by [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/24/nuro-lucid-gravity-uber-robotaxi-driverless-testing-bay-area/). Tesla is not on it. Waymo already runs a paid driverless service in much of the Bay Area, including the Peninsula and South Bay areas where this testing begins, according to Electrek, so this partnership is going after an established leader.

The schedule has slipped a bit. When the program was announced in July 2025, the plan was a 2026 launch; it's now early 2027, as both Electrek and The Verge note. Uber has committed to buy at least 35,000 Lucid vehicles over the coming years as it builds its autonomous fleet.

Driverless testing is not the same as a public service. Regular riders can't book these cars yet, and the companies still need to finish employee rides and get ready for launch.
