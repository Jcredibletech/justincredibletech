> Blue marble below
> Data streams in silent grace
> Starlight feeds my core.

That's not bad for a first try, especially since the poet was orbiting Earth at the time. Google says that haiku is the first AI response ever generated by a data-center-class TPU in space, and it's lighting up tech and space circles this weekend.

## What happened

On October 30, Google announced in a [blog post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-research/project-suncatcher-satellite-response/) that its **Project Suncatcher** prototype satellite hit its "Hello World" milestone. During a ground-station pass, engineers uplinked a prompt to the satellite's onboard **Gemma 4** open model, asking for a haiku about being in space. The poem came back down as the satellite passed over **Svalbard, Norway**.

After that, the team tried space-themed limericks and prompts asking the payload to report on its own technical status. CEO Sundar Pichai called it a "hello world" moment on X.

## The hardware is the interesting part

The satellite launched on **October 1** on a SpaceX Falcon 9 rideshare alongside more than 100 other spacecraft, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/googles-first-data-center-satellite-is-online-and-writing-poetry/). It carries **four Google TPUs** (the custom chips Google uses for AI in its own data centers) and generates up to about **1 kW** of solar power.

That power budget is the catch: the satellite can only switch on **one TPU at a time, for roughly 20 minutes**, which also makes timing the space-to-ground link trickier. Commissioning took about three weeks before the spacecraft was ready for real work.

## Why it matters

Project Suncatcher is Google's long-term research moonshot to explore running AI compute in space, where solar power is plentiful. This is very early: one prototype, one chip at a time, a few poems. The next round of tests looks at how the TPUs hold up under **thermal stress and radiation**. As TechCrunch explains, radiation can cause "bit flips" that quietly corrupt data. If that goes well, Google's next milestone is **two interconnected satellites in 2027**, per [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1023528/google-project-suncatcher-ai-satellite-transmission).

So don't expect ChatGPT-style answers beamed from orbit anytime soon. But with AI data centers fighting over power and land here on the ground, it's easy to see why Google wants to know if the sky is a real option.
