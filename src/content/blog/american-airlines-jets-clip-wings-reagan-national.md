Two **American Airlines** jets made contact on the ground at **Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport (DCA)** outside Washington, D.C., on **Friday evening, Nov. 27**, in the middle of the busy Thanksgiving travel weekend. The wingtip of one plane struck the other while both were taxiing for departure. **No injuries** were reported, according to the FAA and the airline.

## What happened

According to the [Federal Aviation Administration, as reported by CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/american-airlines-jets-clip-wings-reagan-airport/), American Airlines **Flight 638**, headed to **Dallas**, clipped the wing of American **Flight 2152**, headed to **Chicago O'Hare**, a little after 5 p.m. Eastern (about 4:10 p.m. Central). Both were **Boeing 737-800s**, [NBC Chicago](https://www.nbcchicago.com/news/local/wing-of-american-airlines-plane-bound-for-ohare-airport-clipped-by-2nd-plane-at-reagan-national-airport/4021987/) reported.

Both planes went back to their gates and were "removed from service for inspection by our maintenance team," American said. The airline apologized and said passengers would be put on replacement planes and leave later Friday night. The Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority said its fire and rescue crews responded to both aircraft and that no other flights were disrupted. A photo shared on X showed damage to one plane's wingtip.

Key facts:

- **2** American Airlines 737-800s involved, both taxiing to depart
- **0** injuries reported
- Flights **638** (to Dallas) and **2152** (to Chicago)
- Both jets taken out of service for inspection

## Why it matters

Wingtip strikes on the ground are usually low-speed and rarely hurt anyone, but they ground planes for repairs and ripple through schedules, which is the last thing travelers want on one of the busiest weekends of the year. They're also not as rare as you might think. CBS noted that earlier this month a **Copa Airlines** jet's wingtip hit a UPS cargo plane at Miami, causing minor injuries to two passengers, and in October a **Delta** wingtip clipped an Air Canada jet's tail at LAX.

DCA gets extra scrutiny. It has three runways, including what is considered the nation's busiest single runway, and tightly restricted airspace near the White House and Capitol. In January 2025, an Army Black Hawk helicopter collided with an American Airlines regional jet landing at DCA, killing all 67 people aboard both aircraft. Friday's incident was a much smaller ground event, but any problem at DCA draws attention. We've also covered recent travel disruptions like the [FAA's nationwide ground stop](/blog/faa-ground-stop-still-in-effect-friday-night/) and [Delta's weight-and-balance outage](/blog/delta-outage-weight-balance-system-flight-delays/).

## What's next

Neither the FAA nor American has said what caused the two planes to touch or how long they will be out of service. Any review would likely look at taxi routes, spacing and instructions from ground control. We'll update if investigators release findings.
