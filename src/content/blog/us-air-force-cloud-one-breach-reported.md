Hackers have broken into **Cloud One**, the U.S. Air Force's flagship cloud computing platform, according to a **Reuters** report citing three people familiar with the matter. The intrusion was discovered within roughly the past month during work to upgrade the system. Reuters said it could not establish who the hackers are, how far the intrusion reached, or whether any data was stolen.

## What happened

Cloud One started in 2017 as a one-stop shop for Air Force IT. It combines cloud resources from **Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet (Google)** to run applications for personnel and pay, logistics and maintenance, according to [Reuters' report as published by CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/27/hackers-breach-us-air-force-flagship-cloud-one-computing-platform.html). Reuters said those include a cloud-based command-and-control system that pulls together air defense radar feeds across North America, and a system that tracks munitions at more than 200 Air Force locations.

Per the sources Reuters spoke to:

- **U.S. Cyber Command** has been called in to assess the damage.
- Moves of back-office applications onto Cloud One have been **paused**, and updates to apps already on the platform were **blocked**.
- Some users had their **administrator rights revoked**.
- One source said the intruders used a software vulnerability to reach the platform's "control plane," which the source compared to a universal remote. It's unclear whether the hackers used that access to look at anything.

The Air Force told Reuters it "does not comment on the security posture, vulnerabilities or operational status of specific networks and systems," and said it continuously takes "prudent measures" to protect its systems. [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/us-air-force-cloud-platform-reportedly-hacked-in-latest-federal-breach/) reported the same core details. Reuters also noted that Leidos took over from SAIC as Cloud One's prime contractor in 2025; Leidos referred questions to the Air Force.

## Why it matters

This is the latest in a run of reported intrusions at federal agencies in November. In the past week alone we covered [the reported hack of the FBI's NCIC crime database](/blog/fbi-ncic-crime-database-hack/) and [the breach of an unclassified CIA and ODNI system](/blog/cia-odni-unclassified-intelligence-system-hacked/). Reuters counted at least six other recently disclosed government cyber incidents. BleepingComputer says it's not known whether any of them are connected, or whether AI-driven hacking tools played a role in this one.

Blocking updates is a real cost too: Reuters notes it left Air Force staff unable to patch apps on the platform just as a fresh batch of serious industry-wide software vulnerabilities was disclosed.

## What's next

Cyber Command's assessment should show what the intruders touched and whether data left the network. Nobody has publicly claimed responsibility, and neither the Air Force nor Cyber Command has confirmed the breach on the record. We'll update if officials release findings.
