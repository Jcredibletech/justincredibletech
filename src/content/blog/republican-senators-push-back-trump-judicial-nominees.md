Republicans have about six weeks left in control of the Senate before Democrats take over in January, and confirming President Donald Trump's judicial nominees is at the top of their list. But some of the biggest speed bumps aren't coming from Democrats. They're coming from a few Republican senators.

## What's happening

Senate Majority Leader John Thune said judicial confirmations are a top priority for the lame-duck session, [Reuters reports](https://www.investing.com/news/politics-news/trumps-judge-appointment-blitz-faces-republican-resistance-5027827). The Senate has confirmed **57** of Trump's district and appeals court nominees this term, confirmed its first of the lame-duck session on Thursday, and has **23** still pending. A White House official told Reuters, "We expect all judicial nominees pending in the Senate to be confirmed this year."

The friction is over **home-state input**. By Senate custom, senators help pick judges for their states and can block trial-court nominees by withholding a "blue slip." Here are the three disputes:

- **Arkansas (8th Circuit):** The White House on Tuesday, November 10, withdrew U.S. District Judge **Lee Rudofsky** and nominated Arkansas Supreme Court Justice **J. Cody Hiland** after Arkansas Sens. **John Boozman** and **Tom Cotton** opposed Rudofsky, per Reuters and [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/11/12/politics/senate-gop-push-to-get-trump-judges-confirmed). Hiland now needs a new hearing.
- **Texas:** Sen. **John Cornyn** hasn't committed to supporting **T. Elliot Gaiser**, head of the Justice Department's Office of Legal Counsel, for a Dallas judgeship. Gaiser skipped the usual Texas screening process. Without Cornyn's blue slip, he can't get a hearing.
- **Oklahoma:** Sen. **Alan Armstrong** withdrew his support for **Zach West**, a lawyer in the state attorney general's office. Armstrong leaves the Senate November 30, and his successor, Sen.-elect **Kevin Hern**, could take a different view.

Sen. **Thom Tillis**, who is retiring and sits on the Judiciary Committee, is backing his colleagues. "When I've got two Republicans from the state telling me to move on it, I move on it," he told reporters. "If I have one or more asking me not to, then I'm not." He said the White House has sometimes told senators "This is our guy," adding, "That's not how it works here."

## Both sides of the calendar fight

Judiciary Chairman **Chuck Grassley** is moving nominees quickly. He said Democrats are "planning to stonewall a president's ability to appoint judges" next year, calling that "what is unprecedented," per CNN. Ranking Democrat **Dick Durbin** argues the pace breaks committee norms on hearing spacing and review time. Once nominees reach the floor, Democrats can force up to 30 hours of debate on each appeals court pick unless a deal is reached.

## What comes next

The Senate is set to vote Monday on **Daniel Domenico** for the 10th Circuit, and the Judiciary Committee holds a hearing on Wednesday, November 18. Whatever gets confirmed by mid-December is a lifetime appointment.
