*This is an update to our November 13 story, [Chris Van Hollen Launches a Challenge to Chuck Schumer for Senate Democratic Leader](/blog/van-hollen-challenges-schumer-senate-leader/).*

The race to lead the incoming Senate Democratic majority got a new voice on Sunday: the president's.

## What happened

On **Sunday, November 22, 2026**, President Donald Trump wrote on Truth Social: "It is going to be very hard for me to work with Chuck Schumer should he become Senate Majority Leader." He said Schumer "failed badly as 'Leader' once before" and accused him of perpetuating "the Russia, Russia, Russia Hoax." The post was reported by [The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/6235788-trump-critiques-schumer-senate/), [The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/11/22/trump-weighs-senate-democrats-leadership-fight/) and [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/11/22/trump-chuck-schumer-democrats-hakeem-jeffries/92912050007/). Trump did not endorse Schumer's challenger.

The post was a change in tone from Friday, when Trump called Schumer and House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries "smart" at a White House news conference. "If they want to get things done, I want to get things done too," he said then, according to [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/trump-targets-schumer-as-van-hollen-challenges-him-for-senate-leadership-12753718) and USA Today. He also predicted vetoes followed by possible compromises, "especially on healthcare."

## Where the race stands

- **Who:** Schumer, Senate Democratic leader since 2017, versus Maryland Sen. **Chris Van Hollen**, who launched his challenge November 12.
- **When:** A closed-door, **secret-ballot vote on December 1**. Newsweek reports 55 senators and senators-elect are eligible, so **28 votes** wins.
- **Public support:** Tallies differ slightly. The Hill's whip list shows **15 for Schumer and 5 for Van Hollen**; Newsweek counts **16 and 6**. Most senators haven't said publicly.

Schumer says he has the votes. "I'm confident I'll be the next leader," he told reporters Tuesday, per The Hill. Van Hollen calls himself the underdog but says "this is a wide-open race." His supporters include Sen. Elissa Slotkin of Michigan, Sen. Bernie Sanders and Sens.-elect Abdul El-Sayed and Peggy Flanagan. Van Hollen favors a more confrontational approach to the administration and supports ending the legislative filibuster, though he says the caucus should decide on rule changes.

## Why it matters

Democrats won control of both chambers in this month's midterms and are expected to hold **56 Senate seats** in January, Newsweek reports. The majority leader sets the floor agenda and decides which bills and nominees get votes. Trump will still need Senate votes for nominees, and Democrats will need his signature or a two-thirds override for any bill to become law, so the working relationship at the top matters for the next two years.
