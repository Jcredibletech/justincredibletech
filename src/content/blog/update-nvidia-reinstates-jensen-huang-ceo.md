*This is an update to our November 17 story, [Jensen Huang Says He'd Return as Nvidia CEO; the Board Says It Doesn't Intend to Reinstate Him](/blog/nvidia-board-rejects-huang-return-earnings/).*

Six days ago, Nvidia's board said it did not intend to bring Jensen Huang back. On Monday it did exactly that.

## What happened

In a [statement on Monday, November 23](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director), Nvidia said Huang has been reinstated as president, CEO and a member of the board, effective immediately. Key points from the company:

- The board voted **unanimously** after "a series of discussions and meetings" with Huang.
- It said it "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and, based on that input, is confident his return "poses no national security risk."
- Harvey Jones stays on as board chair. Colette Kress, who served as interim CEO, keeps her job as chief financial officer.
- The independent committee investigating Huang "will proceed to conclusion," and Huang says he will cooperate. Nvidia is also creating a new Chief Compliance Officer role.

"I am honored to return to NVIDIA," Huang said in the release. "I can't wait to get back to work."

Huang resigned earlier this month after reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman described as an alleged Chinese spy. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo.html) reports that, according to people familiar with the talks, senior officials briefed the board late last week that no national security laws were violated, which prompted directors to reach out to Huang over the weekend.

## The White House role

President Trump told CNBC he had spoken with the board "directly and indirectly" and that Huang "did nothing wrong." On Truth Social he wrote that the board made "the correct and proper decision" and that the government "does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct." [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/23/2026/jensen-huang-reinstated-as-nvidia-ceo), citing people familiar with the matter, reported that Trump dialed into the board meeting to argue for Huang's return. White House communications director Steven Cheung told Semafor, "President Trump was totally involved."

## The pushback

Not everyone agreed. Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) called the move "reckless" and said Nvidia still owes Congress answers on export controls. Sen. Andy Kim (D-N.J.) said Americans "deserve answers." Per CNBC, Huang is still scheduled for a closed-door interview with House committees on December 1, with Nvidia's general counsel set for December 2.

## Why it matters to you

Nvidia's chips power much of today's AI, and its stock is one of the biggest pieces of S&P 500 index funds. CNBC reports shares jumped about 33% Monday after falling roughly as much since the scandal broke. The leadership question is settled for now, but the internal investigation and the congressional questions are not. This is news, not investment advice.
