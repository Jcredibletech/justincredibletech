For many people with diabetes, the tubing on an insulin pump is the part they like least. It snags on doorknobs, gets tangled in clothes and makes it harder to forget you're wearing a medical device. On Thursday, November 12, 2026, **Tandem Diabetes Care** said the **FDA has cleared a tubeless cartridge and infusion set** for its small **Tandem Mobi** pump.

## What was cleared

According to [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20261113/fda-clears-tubeless-automated-insulin-delivery-system-for-people-with-diabetes) and the company's SEC filing as reported by [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/sec-filings/tandem-diabetes-care-receives-fda-clearance-for-tubeless-insulin-delivery-system-93CH-5025523):

- The clearance is an **FDA 510(k) clearance**, the pathway for devices shown to be substantially equivalent to ones already on the market.
- The new parts let the **existing Tandem Mobi pump** be worn **without tubing**, stuck directly to the body. Users can choose tubed or tubeless.
- The tubeless **infusion set** is cleared for **up to seven days of wear**, and the **cartridge** for **three days** of use.
- Tandem Mobi runs **Control-IQ+**, an automated insulin delivery system that uses continuous glucose monitor readings to predict glucose up to 30 minutes ahead and adjust insulin. It's controlled from a smartphone app.
- The tubed Mobi was first cleared in July 2023 for people age 6 and older.

Tandem says the Mobi is now the only pump cleared for both tubed and tubeless use. That's the company's claim; we haven't independently verified it.

## When you can get it

Not yet. Tandem plans a **U.S. commercial launch in the first half of 2027**, sold **through pharmacies**. Pricing and insurance coverage haven't been announced.

## Why it matters

Automated insulin delivery systems, sometimes called hybrid closed-loop systems, can take some of the constant math out of managing type 1 diabetes. More wear options may make that technology appealing to people who have avoided pumps because of the tubing. It's still a medical device decision: anyone interested should talk with their endocrinologist or diabetes care team about whether it fits their needs once it's available.

Investors noticed, too. Tandem shares rose about 11% after the announcement, according to Investing.com.
