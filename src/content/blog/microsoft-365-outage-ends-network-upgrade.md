**This is an update** to our earlier coverage, [Microsoft 365 Outage Enters a Second Day as Recovery Begins](https://justincredibletech.com/blog/microsoft-365-outage-day-two-recovery/). The outage is over, and Microsoft has given a first explanation.

## What happened

Microsoft said early on **Saturday, November 14**, that work to fix the underlying problem was complete and that people using Outlook, Teams and Copilot should no longer be affected, [the BBC reports](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6rrg51rkz09o). The [AP](https://apnews.com/article/microsoft-outage-outlook-email-365-recover-ca395c0f1e51af784b6d70afb6554f6a) also reported that Microsoft says services are fully restored.

Problems started around midday Eastern on **Thursday, November 12**, when users began reporting Outlook failures. The outage spread to more than 70 countries, according to the BBC and [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/microsoft-365-crawls-back-to-life-after-a-24-hour-outage/), and services came back gradually through Friday.

## What Microsoft says caused it

In a statement to WIRED, a Microsoft spokesperson said: "This was a technical issue not related to cyber or AI/agents. The incident was introduced by an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced following a recent network change."

Microsoft also apologized. "We are deeply sorry for the disruption this incident has caused our customers and partners," a spokesperson said, per the BBC. The company says it will publish a **full post-incident report** after its review, so the deeper technical details aren't public yet.

## Who it hit

The BBC lists a wide range: the Japanese government, the Philippines Supreme Court, Australia's spy agency, parts of the UK's NHS, hospitals in Canada, and U.S. House staff who told Politico they lost a day of legislative work. Microsoft 365 has more than 430 million paid commercial users worldwide.

## What you might notice now

- **A flood of old mail.** WIRED reports Microsoft extended its email queue time to 48 hours so messages sent during the outage still get delivered. Expect late arrivals and a burst of notifications.
- **Not related to the FAA.** Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said Friday's nationwide flight ground stop had no connection to the Microsoft outage, according to CBS News.

## Why it matters

This was one of the longest outages of Microsoft's core productivity tools, and it landed on a workday for much of the world. Experts quoted by the BBC called it a reminder of how much businesses and public services rely on a handful of cloud providers, and said organizations should have a plan for working without these tools when they fail. The post-incident report should show whether the fix is permanent and what Microsoft changes about how it rolls out network upgrades.
