U.S. shoppers spent a record **$13.1 billion online on Black Friday**, up **11.1%** from last year, according to final numbers from Adobe Analytics reported Saturday by [Reuters](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Black+Friday+online+shopping+rises+to+%2413.1+billion+in+the+US%2C+Adobe+Analytics+data+shows/27409568.html). That beat Adobe's own forecast of about $12.9 billion. It follows a record [$7.1 billion on Thanksgiving Day](/blog/thanksgiving-online-sales-record-7-1-billion-black-friday/).

## What people bought

Reuters says electronics and gaming were among the strongest categories, and shoppers also used discounts to stock up on everyday essentials like cleaning supplies, pet care and personal hygiene products. Meanwhile, store foot traffic was **down 2.2%** from a year earlier, at nearly 930 million visits, according to geospatial analytics firm PassBy.

## The AI angle

Adobe's numbers, detailed by [CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/digital-experience/who-won-black-friday-conversions-ai-in-a-landslide/), show:

- Shoppers who arrived at retail sites by clicking a link from an AI chat service or AI browser **converted 68% better** than non-AI visitors, up from 38% on Black Friday 2025.
- AI traffic to U.S. retail sites **rose 100%** year over year.
- AI-referred visits brought in 126% more revenue per visit.

Caveats: Adobe doesn't say what share of all visits came from AI tools, it only counts clicks from AI services to retail sites, and Adobe sells the analytics software that measures it.

## What's next

Adobe expects a record **$15.1 billion** in online spending on **Cyber Monday**, up 6.2%, and about **$47.5 billion** across the five days from Thanksgiving through Cyber Monday. Buy now, pay later is expected to account for about **$1.09 billion** of Cyber Monday spending, per Adobe.

These figures cover online sales at U.S. retail sites only, not in-store purchases, and they're dollar totals, not the number of items sold.
