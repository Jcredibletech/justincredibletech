Remember Scion? The boxy, quirky Toyota youth brand that disappeared after 2016 is back, and its first new vehicle is about the last thing anybody expected: a factory kei truck, built in America, that tops out at 25 miles an hour. "Scion" and "kei truck" were all over car X and the Hacker News front page today.

## What happened

- **Scion is back as "Scion by Toyota."** Toyota Motor North America introduced the brand at simultaneous events in Ypsilanti Township, Michigan, and Frisco, Texas, on November 9, then laid out the details in a November 10 release. Toyota calls it a "freedom lab" for new kinds of mobility ([Toyota Newsroom](https://pressroom.toyota.com/toyota-says-okei-to-america-introducing-the-scion-by-toyota-brand-and-kei-01/)).
- **The first vehicle is the KEI-01.** It's an ultra-compact utility truck with a 660cc three-cylinder engine, a CVT and standard four-wheel drive. Toyota Report puts it at just over 11 feet long with up to 600 pounds of payload, and a bed that folds down on three sides ([Toyota Report](https://toyotareport.com/2026/11/scion-is-back-and-its-first-truck-tops-out-at-25-mph/)). Car and Driver describes it as essentially a rebadged Daihatsu Hijet Jumbo ([Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a74380423/toyota-scion-kei-01-truck-revealed/)).
- **Why only 25 mph.** Toyota designed the KEI-01 to meet FMVSS No. 500, the federal safety standard for low-speed vehicles, and says its maximum speed will be 25 mph. Car and Driver notes that this is how it avoids the crash-test standards that regular cars and pickups must meet, and keeps its right-hand-drive layout. Engineers told the magazine the limit is baked into the engine computer.
- **Built in Indiana, fleets first.** Toyota Indiana in Princeton will assemble it starting in the second half of 2027, backed by a $34 million investment and about 50 new jobs ([14 News](https://www.14news.com/2026/11/10/new-vehicle-be-assembled-princeton-toyota-plant/)). Sales start with a fleet-focused pilot through a small group of Scion by Toyota dealers. Toyota says it "may consider" limited retail sales later, and no price has been announced ([The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/toyota-revives-scion-brand-with-us-legal-kei-truck-and-theres-more-where-that-came-from)).

## Why it matters to you

Kei trucks have a cult following on farms, ranches and job sites, but in the U.S. most of them are 25-year-old imports. A new one with a Toyota dealer network behind it is a big deal for farmers, campuses, cities and small businesses. For everyday drivers, though, 25 mph means side streets and private property only, so this isn't a replacement for your pickup.

## My take

I love that Toyota is building this in Indiana, but the 25 mph cap tells you a lot about how car rules work. Japan has sold these little trucks on public roads for decades, and here the only quick way in is to classify one as a glorified golf cart. I'd like to see regulators give buyers a sensible middle lane for small, cheap work vehicles instead of an all-or-nothing rulebook. That's my opinion; the specs above are Toyota's and the outlets cited.
