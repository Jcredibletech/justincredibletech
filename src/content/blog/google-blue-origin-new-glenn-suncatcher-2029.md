*Update to our October 31 story on Google's Suncatcher prototype writing a haiku from orbit.*

Google's plan to put AI data centers in space now has a ride.

## What happened

On **Thursday, November 19, 2026**, Google and Blue Origin announced a launch services agreement for **Project Suncatcher**, Google's long-term research "moonshot" to run AI computing in orbit on solar power ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-research/blue-origin-project-suncatcher-partnership/), [Blue Origin](https://www.blueorigin.com/news/google-selects-new-glenn-for-project-suncatcher)).

The key details, from both companies:

- **Rocket:** Blue Origin's heavy-lift **New Glenn**.
- **Target date:** the **first quarter of 2029**.
- **Payload:** Suncatcher satellites carrying Google's AI chips, called Tensor Processing Units (TPUs). Google says each one has "roughly the capacity of a data center rack."
- **Orbit:** **sun-synchronous**, an orbit that keeps satellites in steady sunlight for their solar panels.

Google calls it its "first dedicated heavy-lift launch" for Suncatcher. [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/new-glenn-to-launch-project-suncatcher-data-center-satellites-for-google/) described the payload as prototype data center satellites. Neither company announced a price.

"Project Suncatcher represents the ambitious missions New Glenn was built to support," said Jordan Charles, Blue Origin's senior vice president for New Glenn.

## The context

Last month a Suncatcher prototype ran a Gemma AI model on a TPU in orbit, as we covered on October 31. This deal is the step toward launching satellites in larger numbers.

It also comes at a tricky moment for Blue Origin. New Glenn has been grounded since one exploded on its pad in May, and CNBC reports the company aims to fly again in January, according to [Orbital Pulse](https://orbitalpulse.io/daily/2026-11-19). The same BE-4 engines power ULA's Vulcan, which failed on Sunday; ULA's early finding blames inadequate fuel supply to the engines, not the engines themselves.

## Why it matters

AI uses enormous amounts of electricity, and data centers on the ground are running into power and community limits. Tech companies are betting that constant sunlight in orbit could help. It's still an experiment: 2029 is more than two years away, and the economics of computing in space are unproven.
