*This is an update to our November 17 story, [ULA Says Vulcan's Booster Underperformed Before Sunday's Failed Launch](/blog/ula-vulcan-failure-amazon-leo-booster-underperformed/).*

ULA now has a better idea of what went wrong when its Vulcan rocket failed to reach orbit last Sunday with 40 Amazon Leo satellites, and the early answer points away from Blue Origin's engines.

## What's new

In an update posted at **11 a.m. EST on Wednesday, November 18, 2026**, United Launch Alliance [said](https://blog.ulalaunch.com/blog/vulcan-leo-1-updates) it had finished an initial integrated review of flight data. Its key line: "preliminary indications are that the low booster thrust was the result of inadequate fuel supply from the vehicle to the BE-4 engines, not from the performance of the engines."

[Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/11/18/vulcan-failure-investigation-focuses-on-inadequate-fuel-supply-to-booster-engines/), [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/vulcan-failure-caused-by-inadequate-fuel-supply-to-engines-ula-says/) and [Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/ula-traces-vulcan-centaur-rocket-failure-to-inadequate-fuel-supply-to-1st-stage-engines) all reported the finding. Important caveats:

- It is **not a final root cause**. ULA did not say which part of the rocket failed to deliver enough fuel, or why.
- The **Centaur upper stage** and its RL10 engines started and ran as planned but couldn't make up the lost performance.
- The six **solid rocket boosters** performed normally after nozzle fixes made for this flight.
- The **FAA** is overseeing the ULA-led investigation and must approve the final report and any fixes. There is **no return-to-flight date**.

## Why the engine detail matters

Vulcan's first stage uses two methane-fueled **BE-4** engines built by **Blue Origin**, the same engine family that powers Blue Origin's **New Glenn**. New Glenn has been grounded since one exploded on its pad during a test in May, a failure Blue Origin traced to a main oxygen valve on one engine. Spaceflight Now notes that if ULA's finding holds, it points away from the BE-4 as the culprit, which matters for New Glenn's planned return to flight, reportedly targeted for January.

It also leaves the record of Vulcan's **Centaur V** upper stage intact. NASA is weighing a stretched Centaur V as one option for its SLS Moon rocket on Artemis IV.

## Who's waiting

With both Vulcan and New Glenn grounded, the **U.S. Space Force** is waiting on national security launches, and **Amazon**, which bought 38 Vulcan launches for its Leo internet constellation, is relying on its other booked rides on Ariane 6 and Falcon 9, according to Spaceflight Now. Sunday's loss was ULA's first outright mission failure since the company was formed in 2006.
