**This is an update** to our Friday night report, [The FAA's Nationwide Ground Stop Is Still in Effect Friday Night](https://justincredibletech.com/blog/faa-ground-stop-still-in-effect-friday-night/). The ground stop is over, and officials have now given a cause.

## What happened

The FAA halted departures at U.S. airports at 2:54 p.m. Central (3:54 p.m. Eastern) on Friday, November 13. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said U.S. airspace reopened at **8:15 p.m. Eastern (7:15 p.m. Central)**, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/5f374b32b07b/world/us/faa-issues-national-ground-stop-due-extraordinary-circumstance-reasons-safety-2026-11-13/). The [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c6wy9lynq4qno) called it the longest such stop since the September 11, 2001, attacks.

The fallout was huge. FlightAware counted **9,159 cancellations and 7,190 delays** on Friday, according to [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/faa-issues-nationwide-ground-stop/). On Saturday morning FlightAware still showed more than 3,800 U.S. cancellations, led by Delta, United and American.

## What officials say caused it

Duffy said switches and routers on the FAA's older network "got overloaded with data" and started producing inconsistent information, so the system was shut down. "We have old, old switches, old routers, we use 1980s little copper lines," he said, per the [Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/politics/story/2026-11-13/what-caused-national-ground-stop-here-is-what-we-know). He described the problem as intermittent radar and voice communication, not a total blackout.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said new equipment and security patches being added to the older system appeared to "overwhelm" it, and that there is no evidence of a cyberattack or a rogue AI agent. He also said the outage was **not connected** to this week's Microsoft 365 outage.

The picture is still preliminary:

- Senior government sources told CBS News the FAA believes the issue was limited to **Cisco routers built between 2001 and 2006**, which have been identified and isolated.
- Other sources told CBS an **update to the private network** that links air traffic control facilities caused the system to reboot and crash. CBS said it was getting conflicting information.
- Bloomberg reported, citing a source, that the FBI is looking into whether a software update was involved and does not believe it was a cyberattack.

## Why it matters

The FAA's telecom backbone has been flagged before. CBS notes a 2023 FAA-commissioned review called the network obsolete. Duffy and Mullin are now asking Congress for about **$50 billion** in emergency funding for a full air traffic control replacement, above the $30 billion-plus the administration requested earlier. "You will have more of these," Duffy said, "unless the American people and the Congress tell us to build."

For travelers, Duffy said Saturday that operations are "slowly returning to normal" and it is safe to fly, but expect some lingering delays as airlines reposition crews, just ahead of Thanksgiving travel.
