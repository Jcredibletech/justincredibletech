The **World Health Organization** has issued updated recommendations for fighting the **Ebola** epidemic in the **Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC)**, saying it remains a **Public Health Emergency of International Concern**, the WHO's highest alert. More than **12,000 cases** and **5,900 deaths** had been reported as of **Nov. 24**, making it the deadliest Ebola outbreak in the country's history.

## What happened

WHO Director-General **Dr. Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus** issued the new recommendations on **Nov. 25**, after an expert Emergency Committee met on Nov. 17, and [the WHO announced them on Nov. 27](https://www.who.int/news/item/27-11-2026-who-issues-updated-ebola-response-recommendations--calling-for-improved-security-and-accelerated-research). The committee called for:

- **Faster clinical trials** of candidate vaccines and treatments, while keeping scientific, ethical and safety standards high
- Stopping spread **inside health facilities**, including infections tied to unsafe injection practices
- Getting sick people **diagnosed and into supportive care sooner**
- Better **security** so health workers can safely reach patients

For other countries, WHO recommends **targeted screening** of travelers who may have been exposed, but says measures shouldn't unnecessarily interfere with flights and international travel.

"Six months into this emergency, the Committee remains deeply concerned by continued transmission and the high number of Ebola-related deaths," said committee chair **Prof. Lucille Blumberg**.

## Why this outbreak is different

This epidemic is caused by the **Bundibugyo** species of Ebola, which is much rarer than the Zaire strain behind the big 2014-2016 West Africa epidemic. That matters because the Ebola vaccine and antibody treatments people may have heard of were developed for Zaire. According to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/26/dr-congo-ebola-cases-surpass-12000-as-fighting-disrupts-response-in-east), which cited AP and Reuters, there are **no approved treatments or vaccines for Bundibugyo**. Any candidate vaccines are still at the clinical-trial stage, not proven or approved.

Violence is making things worse. The outbreak was declared in mid-May in **Ituri province**, and cases have now been reported in **eight of the DRC's 26 provinces**. WHO has verified at least **94 attacks on health care** in the DRC since the emergency declaration. This week, WHO said about a dozen people, including two WHO staff, were evacuated after being trapped for three days in a hospital caught in crossfire, Al Jazeera reported.

## Why it matters

It has already crossed borders. The [European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/public-health-events/2026-eip-00026/2026-11-26) counts **20 cases in Uganda** and **9 in Kenya**, with some local spread in both, plus one case in Nigeria that was medically evacuated to Switzerland. ECDC rates the chance of infection for people living in Europe as **very low**, and WHO isn't recommending travel bans. Still, an outbreak this size, in a conflict zone, with no approved vaccine, is the kind of situation health officials watch closely. We've covered other outbreaks this year, including [measles in the U.S.](/blog/pennsylvania-ninth-measles-death-us-total-13/), which recently led to the [loss of U.S. measles elimination status](/blog/update-paho-confirms-us-loses-measles-elimination-status/).

## What's next

Watch for vaccine and treatment trial results, and whether security in eastern DRC improves enough for responders to work. This article is general news, not medical advice. Anyone who has traveled to an affected area and feels sick should contact a health care provider.
