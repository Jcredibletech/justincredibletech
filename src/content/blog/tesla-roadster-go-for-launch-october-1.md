Three words. One date. Nearly nine years of waiting.

On Saturday, Tesla posted **“Go for launch”** with a graphic stamped **10.01** — and the Roadster page now has a countdown aimed at the same day. Outlets from TechCrunch to Electrek to Not a Tesla App read it the same way: **Thursday, October 1, 2026** is the unveil for the long-delayed **second-generation Roadster**.

If you’ve been following this saga since the car popped out of a Semi in November 2017… yeah. Bring snacks. And maybe a calendar eraser.

## What’s actually new today

- Tesla’s X post: **“Go for launch”** + **10.01** artwork (launch-pad lighting vibes, thruster energy in the teaser).
- A **countdown** on Tesla’s official Roadster order page pointing at that date.
- Reporting that reservation holders are getting **invites** for an event in **Waco, Texas** (about 90 minutes from Austin / near SpaceX McGregor lore), with RSVP deadlines mid-September and a 21+ guest list — details via ticket screenshots circulating on X.

Tesla hasn’t, in the same breath, locked a public livestream or a “buy it next Tuesday” production date. Musk has previously said that even after a big reveal, production can still be **12–18 months** out. So October 1 is the *show*. Delivery is still a sequel.

## The thruster elephant in the garage

Part of why this car became internet folklore is the optional **SpaceX cold-gas thruster** package — the “it can fly / hover / pull sci-fi Gs” pitch that’s followed the Roadster since the late 2010s. Today’s teaser art leans into that rocket aesthetic without needing a press release paragraph.

Fun consumer framing (not Wall Street framing): this isn’t just “another sports EV.” It’s Tesla reminding everyone that the halo car and the rocket company share a meme universe. Whether October 1 shows a hover, a hop, or just a very loud sports car under dramatic lights… we’ll find out with popcorn.

Also worth remembering: reporting says the **2017 design was abandoned** for a yet-to-be-public redesign. So even hardcore fans may be seeing a *new* shape, not a dusted-off prototype.

## Why the wait became the story

Sports cars slip. Halo cars slip harder. The Roadster slipped into a cultural punchline — $50k reservations, Founders Series lore, endless “almost” dates through 2026. That history is why today’s three-word post lands so loud: for once, there’s a stamped date on official channels instead of a shrug.

Keep expectations split in two:

1. **Reveal night** — design, numbers Tesla wants to brag about, maybe a thruster demo.  
2. **Ownership night** — years later, when a normal buyer can configure one without a time machine.

Both can be exciting. Confusing them is how hype hangovers happen.

## Evening poll (be honest)

**October 1 is circled. What’s your move?**

- **A) Reserve / stay reserved** — I want in, thrusters or not  
- **B) Wait for the thruster demo** — if it doesn’t “fly,” I’m out  
- **C) Just give me a normal sports EV** — skip the rocket cosplay, ship a great car  
- **D) Spectator mode** — I’ll watch the stream, keep my Model Y / Duo money  

There’s no wrong answer — only different definitions of “worth the wait.”

## The lighthearted takeaway

Tesla spent Saturday telling the internet the Roadster finally has a launch window. Not a production line. Not a delivery ETA. A **reveal**. After nearly a decade of teases, that’s still a win for countdown culture.

Set a reminder for **October 1**. Decide whether you care about cold-gas drama or just want a stupid-fast convertible. And if you’ve held a reservation since the Obama-adjacent smartphone era… hydrate. The finish line of the *trailer* is in sight.
