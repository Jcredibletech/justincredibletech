A business jet nose-dived into a South Florida neighborhood on Sunday afternoon, and U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy says **all nine people on board were killed**. This isn't a tech story, but it's a big one, so here's what officials have confirmed so far. This is a developing story, and details may change.

## What happened

According to the **FAA**, a **Learjet 60** crashed in a **residential area near Cresthaven, Florida**, in **Palm Beach County**, at about **12:50 p.m. local time on Sunday, November 1**. The FAA says **nine people were on board**. The jet had departed **Austin-Bergstrom International Airport** in Texas and was headed to **North Eleuthera** in the Bahamas. The FAA notes that this information is **preliminary and subject to change**.

Secretary Duffy confirmed the toll on X:

<blockquote class="twitter-tweet"><p lang="en" dir="ltr">Today's plane crash in Palm Beach County, Florida is a horrific tragedy. Here's what we know: At 12:50 PM ET, a Learjet 60 crashed in a neighborhood near Cresthaven, FL. All 9 people on board were killed. The aircraft departed from Austin, Texas, and was headed to the Bahamas</p>&mdash; Secretary Sean Duffy (@SecDuffy) <a href="https://x.com/SecDuffy/status/2116996729962721424">November 1, 2026</a></blockquote>

The **Associated Press** reports the jet nose-dived and appeared to **just miss homes** near a busy roadway. WPBF reports the plane's registration as **N722BD**, citing FAA records. The names of the people on board have **not been released**.

**Palm Beach County Fire Rescue** responded and closed Military Trail, calling it an "extended incident." The photo at the top of this post is from their public post:

Palm Beach County says **Military Trail is closed between 10th Avenue North and Purdy Lane/Alemeda Drive**. Here's the county's closure map:

According to WPTV, WPBF and CBS12, county officials say about **600 residents** of a nearby mobile home community are being **relocated** while crews work. County Commissioner Joel Flores described the area as a "crime scene," which is standard language for securing an accident site; no one has alleged a crime. Shelter is available at the **Greenacres Community Center**, and the Red Cross is on standby. WPBF reports Gov. Ron DeSantis said there was no additional information yet on injuries on the ground. WPBF describes it as the deadliest plane crash in Palm Beach County.

If you live nearby, follow Palm Beach County and PBC Fire Rescue for official updates and stay clear of the area.

## How NTSB investigations generally work

This is the general process, not anything specific to this crash yet:

- **On-scene work:** investigators document and recover the wreckage and map the debris field.
- **Recorders and data:** if an aircraft carries a cockpit voice recorder or flight data recorder, they're sent to the NTSB lab in Washington. Investigators also review radar and ADS-B tracking data.
- **The bigger picture:** air traffic control communications, weather, the aircraft's maintenance records, and the crew's training and history.
- **Timeline:** a **preliminary report** usually comes out within about **30 days**. The final report, with a **probable cause**, often takes **12 to 24 months**.

## What we don't know yet

The big one is **why it happened**. You'll see plenty of theories online, including reported radio audio and expert guesses. None of that is an official finding, and I'm not going to guess. The NTSB is expected to share more at its briefing in West Palm Beach on Monday, and I'll update this post when officials say something new.
