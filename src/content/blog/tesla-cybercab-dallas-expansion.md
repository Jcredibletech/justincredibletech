The shiny gold robotaxi with no steering wheel is about to get a passport. Well, a Texas driver's license, anyway.

On Tesla's **Q3 2026 earnings call on Wednesday, October 21**, the company said its two-seat **Cybercab** will expand beyond Austin, starting with **Dallas next month** and spreading to **other cities by the end of this year**. Cybercab service first went live in Austin on **September 3**.

CFO **Vaibhav Taneja** didn't exactly play it cool: "We're starting with getting people from one place to the other, but we want to basically own transportation of any kind on Earth." No pressure.

## The numbers that jumped out

- **125,000+ a year:** that's the installed annual Cybercab manufacturing capacity Tesla says it has at its Texas factory.
- **Fleet sales by year end:** Tesla says it'll start **selling Cybercabs to fleet operators** before 2026 is over, so it won't only be Tesla's own robotaxi network.
- **Existing robotaxi fleets:** Tesla already runs small robotaxi fleets using the **Model Y** in Dallas, Houston, Miami, Orlando and Tampa. The Cybercab is the purpose-built version.

## The catch: federal safety rules

In September, just hours after the Austin launch, the **National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA)** opened an investigation into whether the Cybercab meets current federal motor vehicle safety standards. Automakers self-certify, and Tesla says the car complies, but it's missing things the current rules expect, like a **brake pedal** and **side mirrors**.

NHTSA has proposed dropping some of those requirements, but that rule change is likely still at least a few months away. Taneja said Tesla needs "all the regulatory approvals" to deploy Cybercabs "as fast as we can produce them." So the factory might be ready before the paperwork is.

## Why it matters

The robotaxi race in the U.S. right now has a clear leader, and it isn't Tesla. **Waymo** has **more than 4,000 robotaxis** running in **more than a dozen cities**. Tesla's pitch is that the Cybercab is cheaper and easier to build, so it can undercut everyone on price. But that only matters if it can scale outside Austin, which is exactly what Dallas is supposed to prove.

If you live in Dallas, you might be hailing a car with no pedals before Thanksgiving. That's either the coolest thing ever or slightly terrifying, depending on how much you trust software in rush-hour traffic on I-35.

## Bottom line

Tesla is going from "one city" to "lots of cities" in about two months, while the safety review is still open. Bold move. Let's see how it plays out.
