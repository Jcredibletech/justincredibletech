Yesterday I wrote that Jensen Huang had [resigned as Nvidia's CEO](https://justincredibletech.com/blog/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo/), effective immediately, and that the stock had dropped as much as about 14% before trimming that loss in the afternoon. Here's what happened after that, and what to watch next.

## What's new since yesterday afternoon

- **The Thursday close is in.** Nvidia finished the regular session down **4.3% at $166.19**, after falling as much as about 14% earlier in the day. That is a little better than the roughly 5% afternoon loss I noted in yesterday's post, and it kept the company's market value just above $4 trillion into the close ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo-live-updates.html); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/nvidia-inches-higher-premarket-after-stock-slides-following-huang-resignation-5024641)).
- **Friday premarket is slightly higher.** As of about 7:15 a.m. Central Time, Nvidia was **about 1.6% to 1.7% higher** in premarket trading ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/13/nvidia-nvda-stock-jensen-huang-transition.html) reported 1.7% as of 8:13 a.m. Eastern Time; [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/nvidia-inches-higher-premarket-after-stock-slides-following-huang-resignation-5024641) reported 1.6%). Premarket moves can change quickly, and the regular session has not opened yet.
- **Earnings are still on for Tuesday.** Nvidia has stuck with its plan to report fiscal third-quarter results on **November 17**, around 1:20 p.m. Pacific Time, with a call at 2:00 p.m. Pacific ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo-live-updates.html); [Nvidia Newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition)). That will be the first major public test of the company under interim CEO Colette Kress, who also remains CFO.
- **The search for a permanent CEO is underway.** Nvidia's board said Thursday it is considering both internal and external candidates while Kress serves as interim chief ([Nvidia Newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition)). Reuters, in a Friday profile, also reported that Kress has been on calls to reassure customers and suppliers ([Reuters, via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/colette-kress-the-cfo-who-challenged-huangs-bets-is-nvidias-interim-ceo-5023936)).

Huang has agreed to appear before Congress, according to earlier reporting, but he has not testified yet. I'll cover that when it happens.

## Why it matters to you

If you own an S&P 500 index fund, Nvidia is still one of the largest weights in it, so Thursday's swing showed up in a lot of retirement accounts even if you never bought the stock directly. A modest premarket bounce does not erase the drop, and it does not tell us how Tuesday's earnings will go. What it does tell us is that the market is no longer pricing in the worst of yesterday morning's panic, at least before the opening bell.

The products and chip roadmap were set before this week. The open question is whether Nvidia can keep that plan on track, and keep customers confident, without the founder who has run the company since 1993.

## My take

The close mattered more than the open. A 14% plunge at the open is a shock. A 4.3% close, followed by a small premarket bounce, is the market catching its breath, not a verdict on Kress or the next CEO. Tuesday's earnings are the real test: guidance, customer demand, and whether the leadership change changes anything about the outlook.
