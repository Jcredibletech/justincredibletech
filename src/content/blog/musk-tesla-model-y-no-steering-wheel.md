Tesla's next driverless car may be one you already know.

## What happened

Speaking at the **New York Times DealBook Summit** on **Wednesday, November 18, 2026**, Elon Musk said Tesla plans "an autonomous version of the Model Y that won't have steering wheels or pedals," [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/11/19/musk-tesla-model-y-no-steering-wheel-7-seat/) and [EVXL](https://evxl.co/2026/11/19/tesla-model-y-no-steering-wheel-six-seven-seats-musk/) reported.

He pitched it as a fix for a gap in the lineup. Tesla's **Cybercab** seats two. "We still need autonomous vehicles if you've got 4 or 6, or a whole family," Musk said, describing the new Model Y as "good for transporting groups of 6 or 7 people."

What he didn't say: **no timeline, no price**, no factory, and no word on whether Tesla would sell it to owners or keep it in its own Robotaxi fleet.

In the same answer, Musk said Tesla probably needs to move all Model Y production to the stretched, three-row **Model Y L**, which starts at $61,990 in the U.S., EVXL reported. He did not announce a decision.

## The rules question

U.S. safety standards were written for cars with a steering wheel and brake pedal. The federal brake standard, FMVSS 135, still says service brakes are applied with a foot control, EVXL noted. NHTSA proposed rewriting it for driverless cars in June but hasn't finalized it.

NHTSA has also been auditing how Tesla self-certified the pedal-free Cybercab since September 3. The agency has not found the car out of compliance, EVXL reported. A controls-free Model Y would face the same questions.

## Do buyers want it?

Electrek pointed to Cox Automotive's 2026 Autonomous Vehicle Study: only **10%** of U.S. consumers picked fully driverless (Level 5) as their most appealing option, down from 17% in 2016. The top pick, at 28%, was Level 4: full self-driving with the option for a person to take over.

## Why it matters

If Tesla can get a familiar, high-volume car approved without controls, it could grow a robotaxi fleet much faster than with a new two-seater. But regulators and buyers still have to come along, and Musk has missed timelines on self-driving before.
