Tesla's Cybercab, the two-seat robotaxi with no steering wheel or pedals, is getting a second home. Tesla says it will spend billions to build it in Germany.

## What happened

On **Monday, November 16, 2026**, Tesla announced plans to invest **€3 billion (about $3.3 billion)** to expand its **Gigafactory Berlin-Brandenburg in Grünheide**, its only European factory, according to [DW](https://www.dw.com/en/tesla-planning-3bn-investment-in-german-factory-to-produce-cybercabs/a-79961416) and [electrive](https://www.electrive.com/2026/11/16/tesla-announces-multi-billion-euro-expansion-of-gigafactory-grunheide/). The plan includes:

- A **new production building** with capacity for **up to one million Cybercabs a year**. DW notes the timeframe for reaching that number wasn't specified.
- **Auxiliary buildings** and an **additional wastewater treatment plant** on the site.
- More **battery cell production**, which electrive reports is expected to reach full capacity by mid-2027.

## The timeline

- **By the end of 2026:** Tesla submits the building permit application.
- **First half of 2027:** construction is scheduled to begin.
- **2029:** the first Cybercabs are set to roll off the line.

"This will lay the foundation for the most technologically advanced Tesla factory in the world," said plant manager **André Thierig**, as quoted by electrive.

## Local reaction

Brandenburg's state premier, **Dietmar Woidke**, called it "a decision of enormous economic significance." Water use has long been a sore point with neighbors of the plant, and DW notes the factory has sparked tensions with residents over water access. Electrive, citing German broadcaster RBB, reports that Tesla's in-house wastewater treatment means the expansion shouldn't need more than the water volumes already agreed with the local utility.

## A factory on the rebound

The site slowed down after weak sales in 2024 and 2025. Now, electrive reports, it's building about **7,000 vehicles a week**, close to its current technical limit of 7,500, and its workforce has grown back from about 11,000 to **12,600**. Hiring for the expansion could add several hundred workers, DW reports.

The announcement came days after Elon Musk said Tesla would wind down Cybertruck production, and as Tesla opens Cybercab orders to some individual buyers in the U.S.

## Why it matters

Building a robotaxi factory in Europe is a bet that self-driving cars will be allowed, and wanted, on European roads by the end of the decade. Today only a few places allow driverless Cybercabs, including Austin, Texas, so this is a long-range wager on both the technology and the regulators. For EV fans, it also shows where Tesla's priorities are heading.
