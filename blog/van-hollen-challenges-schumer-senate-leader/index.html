Chris Van Hollen Launches a Challenge to Chuck Schumer for Senate Democratic Leader

Maryland Sen. Chris Van Hollen said on November 12, 2026, that he will run against Sen. Chuck Schumer to lead the incoming Senate Democratic majority. Schumer says he already has the votes. The closed-door caucus vote is set for December.

By Justin @JCredibleTech
November 13, 2026· 3 min read

Senate Democrats just won a majority in the midterms, and now they have a leadership fight. On Thursday night, November 12, 2026, Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland announced he will run for Senate majority leader, challenging Sen. Chuck Schumer of New York, who has led the Democratic caucus since 2017.

What happened

  • The challenge. In a statement, Van Hollen said voters “called for change” and that “we need new leadership. This is not about any one person.” (Roll Call, NBC News)
  • Schumer’s response. Schumer said on X that he welcomes the discussion, pointed to “55 seats and counting,” and said, “I have the votes to continue serving as Democratic Leader.” (NBC News)
  • The odds. Van Hollen called himself “the underdog” in an MS NOW interview and said more than 10 senators have pledged to support him. NBC News reports Schumer remains favored, though this is likely the most serious challenge he has faced for the job.
  • How it’s decided. Senate Democrats vote on their leaders for the next Congress in December, behind closed doors by secret ballot. A simple majority of the caucus wins.

Who is backing whom

For Van Hollen: Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-Vt.), who had said earlier in the week that he would not back Schumer, and Michigan Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed, who first urged Van Hollen to run back in April.

For Schumer: Sen. Angus King (I-Maine), who called Schumer “one of the principal architects” of the party’s midterm win and asked, “Why would you fire the manager when you just won the World Series?” Sen. Tammy Baldwin (D-Wis.) and Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand (D-N.Y.) have also publicly backed him.

What each side is pitching

Van Hollen’s team says he would push the new majority to use the upcoming government funding bill, needed to avoid a December shutdown, as leverage to end the Iran war. He also wants to restore Medicaid funding cut in last year’s budget reconciliation law, extend Affordable Care Act tax credits, and reverse President Trump’s “big, beautiful bill,” according to NBC News and Roll Call. He said he would not take sides in contested Democratic primaries.

Schumer’s allies argue his experience running the Senate floor is exactly what a new majority needs, and that the party should not change leaders right after its best election in years.

Why it matters

Whoever wins will set the Senate’s agenda starting in January: which bills reach the floor, how hard the chamber pushes back on the White House, and how a Democratic Senate handles a Republican president for the last two years of his term. Republicans, now heading into the minority, will be watching to see whether the caucus picks a deal-making veteran or a more combative new leader.

Van Hollen, 67, joined the Senate in 2016 after more than a decade in the House. Schumer, 75, was first elected to the House in 1980 and to the Senate in 1999.
