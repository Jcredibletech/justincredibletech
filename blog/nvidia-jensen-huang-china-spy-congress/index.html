US Intel Says China's Spy Agency Targeted Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang — House GOP Wants a Briefing

The New York Times reports US intelligence believes China's Ministry of State Security recruited a Megaspeed executive to get close to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. The FT says officials believe the relationship was romantic. Three House Republican chairs want Huang at a closed-door briefing on November 18.

By Justin @JCredibleTech
November 7, 2026· 3 min read

If you use an AI app, odds are it runs on Nvidia chips — and right now the man who runs Nvidia is at the center of a China spy story that Congress wants answers on. Mentions of “Jensen Huang” plus “spy” on X jumped from a few hundred posts Friday to thousands Saturday, and it’s the lead story on Techmeme.

What’s been reported

  • The New York Times reported that US intelligence officials told the administration that Alice Huang, an executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed International, was working for China’s Ministry of State Security (MSS), which recruited her to collect information on Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang — and possibly to nudge Nvidia toward making its most advanced chips available to China (via Bloomberg/Seattle Times). The two are not related.
  • The Financial Times then reported that a US government memo drafted earlier this year says officials believe the relationship was romantic (via Investing.com). The White House and CIA declined to comment.
  • Megaspeed is part of Nvidia’s cloud partner network: it buys Nvidia hardware and rents computing power to AI developers. US authorities have scrutinized it over whether advanced chips were diverted to China.

Nvidia’s side

Nvidia hasn’t commented on the memo or the romance claim. It points to earlier statements that Jensen Huang “talks with thousands of customer executives every year,” that his interactions with Alice Huang were “not unusual or unexpected,” and that cloud customers get “ordinary business information — not sensitive NVIDIA trade secrets — and zero information relevant to national security” (CNBC). Nvidia also says it checked Megaspeed’s GPU inventory and found no evidence chips were illegally sent to China (Ars Technica). China’s Foreign Ministry called the reports “baseless.”

Congress wants answers

Three House Republican chairs — John Moolenaar (Select Committee on the CCP), Brian Mast (Foreign Affairs) and Arkansas’s own Rick Crawford (Intelligence) — asked Huang to attend a joint, closed-door briefing on November 18, the day after Nvidia’s Q3 earnings. They want to hear about his interactions with Alice Huang and Nvidia’s security protocols against foreign adversary espionage. He has until November 11 to accept or offer another date (House China Committee).

“The issue of PRC infiltration and access to cutting-edge U.S. technology is core to the investigative work of our three committees,” the chairmen wrote.

Why it matters for tech

AI chips are the most strategic hardware on the planet right now, and US export controls exist to keep the best ones out of Beijing’s hands. If China’s intelligence service really was working an angle on the CEO of the company that makes them, that’s a supply-chain security problem, not just a gossip story. Nothing here has been proven in public yet; what’s next is whether Huang shows up on the 18th and what Nvidia says on its earnings call the day before.

Should chip CEOs face the same kind of security vetting as defense contractors? Let me know.
