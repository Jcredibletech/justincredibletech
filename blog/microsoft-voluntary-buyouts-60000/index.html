Microsoft Is Offering Voluntary Buyouts to About 60,000 Employees: What It Means

Microsoft's new Voluntary Exit Offer covers roughly 27% of its workforce. Here's who's eligible, what's different from a layoff, and why the timing matters.

  • Tech
  • Microsoft
  • AI
  • Jobs
  • News

Microsoft just did something it has never done at this scale. On Monday the company announced a Voluntary Exit Offer, a paid way out that about 60,000 employees are eligible to take. That’s roughly 27% of its 223,000 workers (as of June 30, 2026), according to the AP.

Who’s eligible

The offer covers eligible employees in the U.S., Canada, the U.K. and India, at level 70 and below, which is up to the vice president level. Not every team is treated the same:

  • Fully participating: Xbox and gaming, Windows, Copilot, HR and finance.
  • Partially participating: AI, legal and cloud.
  • Excluded: marketing, the commercial business and security.

That split tells you a lot about where Microsoft wants to keep its people, and where it’s comfortable getting smaller.

This is not a layoff

The key word is voluntary. Nobody is being forced out. Eligible employees have until early December to decide, and CNBC reports, citing a person familiar with the plan, that those who accept would leave on December 31.

Microsoft’s chief people officer, Amy Coleman, put it plainly: “The Voluntary Exit Offer is new for us. We’ll learn from it.”

It builds on a smaller Voluntary Retirement Program Microsoft ran earlier in 2026, which was offered to roughly 9,000 people, and more than 30% of them took it. NBC News has more on how the two programs compare. It also follows real layoffs: about 4,800 jobs cut in July 2026 and around 15,000 in 2025.

Why now?

Follow the money. Microsoft expects to spend roughly $175 billion on chips and data centers in 2026, as Reuters has reported, to keep up in the AI race. Shrinking headcount through buyouts is one way to keep costs in check while that spending climbs. The timing is notable too: Microsoft reports quarterly earnings on Wednesday. The story was first reported by Business Insider, and The Seattle Times has more on what it means for the Redmond area.

What this means for you

If you work in tech, this is a sign of where big companies are heading: fewer people in legacy product areas, more money poured into AI infrastructure. Voluntary programs like this often come before harder decisions, so many workers will be weighing a guaranteed package now against uncertainty later. (This is news, not financial advice.)

So here’s my question: if you were offered a buyout like this, would you take the money, or stay and ride the AI wave?
