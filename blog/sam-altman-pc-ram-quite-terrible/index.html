Sam Altman Says Scarce RAM Sitting in Home PCs Is "Quite Terrible" for AI, and PC Fans Are Pushing Back

On the Cheeky Pint podcast, OpenAI's CEO argued that the world's limited supply of memory gets more use in cloud data centers than in personal computers. Critics say he's ignoring privacy, everything else RAM does, and AI's own role in the memory crunch.

By Justin @JCredibleTech
October 30, 2026· 2 min read

That stick of RAM in your desktop? According to OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, it’s a pretty inefficient place for some of the world’s scarcest computer parts to live. His comments are trending hard on X today, and a lot of PC owners are not taking it well.

What he actually said

On the Cheeky Pint interview, the conversation turned to local inference, meaning running AI models on your own computer instead of in someone else’s data center. Altman said a lot of people assume that problem is solved, then made the case against it. About 29 minutes in, per Tom’s Hardware, he said:

“From a global allocation perspective, actually, it’s quite terrible to have everybody have a bunch of the world’s limited supply of DRAM sitting in their own computer and being used 5% of the time. You want maximum utilization in the cloud if you’re trying to deal with this very supply-constrained environment.”

To be precise about it: he wasn’t saying your PC shouldn’t have RAM at all. His point was about where scarce memory does the most work for AI while supply is tight. The “5%” figure was his own estimate, not a number he cited a study for.

Why people are annoyed

The pushback on X and in tech coverage boils down to a few arguments:

  • RAM isn’t just for AI. It runs your operating system, browser tabs, games and creative apps. As Notebookcheck points out, “idle for AI” doesn’t mean idle.
  • Local AI has real perks. Running a model at home keeps your prompts and files on your own machine and doesn’t need a subscription.
  • Convenient timing. OpenAI’s main products are closed models that run in its own data centers, so more cloud and less local AI lines up neatly with its business.
  • Who’s eating the RAM? Memory makers are already prioritizing data-center demand, and Notebookcheck notes that’s helping push up prices for PCs and other hardware.

Why it matters

This is a bigger fight than one podcast clip. As AI gets more capable, there’s a real tug-of-war over whether you’ll own your AI, running it on hardware in your house, or rent it from a handful of cloud companies. Memory is the bottleneck in that fight right now, and the people building the giant data centers clearly have an opinion on where it should go.

So which side are you on: would you rather rent your AI from the cloud, or run it on your own machine? More stories like this on justincredibletech.com.
