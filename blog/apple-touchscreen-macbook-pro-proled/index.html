Apple Unveils Its First Touchscreen Mac: What the New MacBook Pro Means for You

Apple's new MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display is the first Mac you can touch. Here's how touch works, what it costs, and who should care.

By Justin McBroom @JCredibleTech
October 28, 2026· 2 min read
𝕏

For years Apple insisted the Mac didn’t need a touchscreen. As of today, that stance is gone. Apple just announced the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display, and it’s the first Mac you can actually touch. Pre-orders opened October 28, and it arrives Friday, November 6.

If you just read yesterday’s news about the M6 MacBook Pro, this is a different machine sitting above it. Here’s what changes for you.

Touch, built into the screen

The new display is a tandem OLED panel with the touch sensor built right in. Apple rates it at up to 2,000 nits peak brightness with a 10,000,000:1 contrast ratio. It comes in 14-inch and 16-inch sizes, and both are lighter than before: the 14-inch is about 3 pounds and the 16-inch is about 4 pounds.

Touch works alongside the keyboard and trackpad, not instead of them. You keep typing and swiping the way you do now, and reach up to tap when it’s faster.

Will your apps work?

This is the big practical question, and Apple’s answer is mostly yes. According to Apple, many existing apps work with touch right away because macOS automatically translates touches into clicks. Developers can go further and build touch-specific features, but you don’t have to wait for updates to start tapping. Touch requires macOS 27.

The Dynamic Island comes to the Mac

The iPhone’s Dynamic Island makes the jump too. On the Mac it’s the new home for Spotlight, Live Activities and Siri AI, so things like timers, deliveries and searches live in one spot at the top of the screen.

What it costs

Here’s where you’ll want to think hard. Pricing starts at:

  • 14-inch with M5 Pro: $2,899
  • 16-inch with M5 Pro: $3,399
  • 14-inch with M5 Max: $4,499
  • 16-inch with M5 Max: $4,799

For comparison, the M6 14-inch MacBook Pro Apple announced yesterday starts at $1,999. That’s a $900 gap to get touch and the new OLED display, and CNBC described the new pricing as a notable increase. Apple rates battery life at up to 24 hours.

So who is this for?

If you edit photos or video, sketch, or already reach for your laptop screen out of iPhone habit, touch plus that OLED panel could genuinely change how you work. If you mostly type, browse and stream, the M6 model gives you a lot of Mac for $900 less, and nothing about your workflow requires touch today.

The smart move is probably to wait for hands-on reviews closer to November 6, and see how well everyday apps really handle taps.

Would you pay $900 more for a Mac you can touch, or is the trackpad all you need?
